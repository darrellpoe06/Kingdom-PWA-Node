**The material has two halves, and only the first is the lesson.**

**Half one — built.** The 1929 excavation of Ugarit (Ras Shamra) on the Syrian coast; roughly a thousand cuneiform tablets in an unrecognised language; the decipherment, which turned on counting the signs and finding only about thirty — therefore an alphabet, not a syllabary; and what Ugaritic gives back to the reading of biblical Hebrew, including the parallel between the Ugaritic sea-serpent lotan and the Word's leviathan.

## Decision

Build **half one** as a Word-first lesson of ten numbered movements at the sov9/sov10 standard, in all four bands, placed in Living Lessons because the course is where this house teaches the Word to a mixed household rather than to a seminar — a parent, a teenager and a grandparent each need this particular skill, and the skill is not academic.

The gate pins the spine in every place a reader actually reads:
- **19 strands quoted in every band** — Genesis 1:1, Genesis 1:21, Psalms 104:26, Job 26:13, Isaiah 27:1, Psalms 74:14, Job 41:1, Psalms 96:5, 1 Corinthians 8:4, 1 Corinthians 8:6, 1 Kings 18:21, Psalms 29:10, Nehemiah 8:8, Proverbs 15:3, 1 Samuel 16:7, Genesis 16:13, Isaiah 40:8, Deuteronomy 6:7, Proverbs 27:17;
- **61 further strands** verbatim somewhere in the lesson;
- the ten movements in order, and every band closing on Isaiah 40:8 — what was still standing when the dust settled;
- the two honest limits on the source stated in the lesson's own prose, not only in this record;
- the three limits on the parallel itself named narrowly;
- Jesus confessed as the Lamb of Yahweh and the Eternal Son of Yahweh, with John 1:29 beside it, in every band.

**The hinge, which is the whole teaching.** In the neighbours' telling the sea-monster is a rival the storm-figure must defeat in order to be somebody. In the Word it is inventory: "his hand hath formed the crooked serpent" (Job 26:13); "there is that leviathan, whom thou hast made to play therein" (Psalms 104:26); "And God created great whales" (Genesis 1:21). Same vocabulary, categorically different account of reality. The lesson refuses **both** available errors and says plainly that the second is ours: granting the inference, and denying the parallel in order to protect the Word — because a faith that requires a true thing to stay hidden collapses the instant it surfaces.

The two stories teach that hinge rather than decorate it:
- **The Thirty Marks** (light; Nehemiah 8:8): a grandmother's private shorthand cracked by a boy who counted thirty marks, found the commonest, and tested it in the two-letter words — and who, having learned to read her hand, had still not written one word of a single recipe.
- **What the Tablet Did to Howard** (solemn; 1 Thessalonians 5:21): a man who stopped teaching after a documentary showed him a real parallel, and the friend who did not argue with the tablet but AGREED with it and then opened the Book — nothing taken from him that spring except a defence he should never have been handed.

## Impact

A reader gets a two-move skill that works on every version of this challenge they will ever meet: concede out loud whatever is actually established, then open the Book and show what the Word DOES with the shared material. A child learns that Yahweh made the thing everybody else was afraid of, and that a true fact cannot hurt Him. A youth learns that a shared word is not a shared deity. A teen learns to sort a source into what is established, what is genuinely open, and what the Word corrects — and learns it on a real source rather than a tidy example. A senior gets the pastoral centre: the believer most at risk here is not the sceptic but the one who was handed a defence that was not true. Every band sends the reader to their parents, their children and a friend with the same question, which is the one a household can actually carry: could a true fact ever hurt Yahweh?

### Limits, named rather than left

1. **The source is truncated.** At about 1,773 words it is the shortest transcript in the whole intake and **it ends mid-sentence** — mid-clause, in the middle of a thought about secular ritual. Nothing was extrapolated past that edge; the lesson teaches to the end of the page and stops, and says so in its own prose. Anything the conversation went on to say is unknown to this lesson.
2. **The source capitalises the false god's name.** It writes the storm-figure with a capital, and the other members of that pantheon with it. This house writes every one of them in lower case and **the rule is senior to the source** (CLAUDE.md), so the conflict is surfaced here and corrected in the lesson rather than copied through — and the lesson says out loud, in every band, WHY, so a reader learns typography as theology rather than as style. The repository's own KJV corpus already prints `baal`, `satan` and `leviathan` in lower case, so no quotation was forced into the conflict, and none was altered.
3. **Not a quotation of any person.** The captions are a machine's hearing of a conversation, so the source is summarised and nothing is placed in anyone's mouth inside quotation marks. The captions name no speaker; no speaker is named, and no faith label, affiliation or motive is attached to anyone — the correction World Issues 21 had to take.
4. **One step of the decipherment carried as the source's telling, not as fact.** The source says the decoders fixed the commonest sign as mem and reasoned outward from two-letter words. The frequency reasoning is established — Hans Bauer had been a wartime cryptanalyst — but that exact first move could not be corroborated independently, so the lesson attributes it to the source rather than asserting it.
5. **Three limits on the parallel, in the lesson itself.** The Ugaritic line mentioning seven heads sits in a damaged part of the tablet and is reconstructed with less confidence than the surrounding text; no one can show Isaiah had a document in front of him, and the source itself concludes he almost certainly did not, which is the right register; and the Tel Hazor seal cannot be assigned Israelite rather than Phoenician workmanship, which its own publication concedes.
6. **One book named but not read.** The source cites a volume by Ara Norenzayan as its authority for the evolutionary account. The lesson names that as the source's own citation, states that this house has not read it, and imputes no motive to a living man. It is not treated as verified and it is not treated as discredited.
7. **"Hebrew did not exist" is an inference sitting on a measurement.** The measurement stands and is stated plainly: no Hebrew inscription we hold is as old as the Ugarit tablets, which fell with the Late Bronze Age collapse around 1200 BC. The inference some draw from it — that the Word therefore inherited its God from that pantheon — is a model stacked on the measurement, and the Word answers the model. An older manuscript is not an older truth.
8. **What was corrected in the source before it was taught (DR-0192).** It compresses the plow-strike and the excavation into one year; the strike was spring 1928 at Minet el-Beida and Schaeffer's dig and the first tablet were April and 14 May 1929. It leaves the decipherment anonymous; it was Hans Bauer in 1930, corrected and completed by Edouard Dhorme and Charles Virolleaud. Its seven-headed-serpent find is real and was verified — a stamp seal excavated on the Tel Hazor acropolis in 2022, published 2024, eighth century BC — but it is a seal rather than an inscription, and the lesson says so.
9. **How the external facts were actually verified, at their true grain.** Outbound web search DID work from this session, which is worth saying because a sibling building the Amarna lesson had every fetch refused by the egress proxy and had to state its facts coarsely. Four searches ran and returned real results: the 1928 plow-strike at Minet el-Beida and the 14 May 1929 first tablet; the thirty signs, Bauer's wartime cryptanalysis, the 22 April 1930 transcriptions and the Dhorme/Virolleaud corrections; the lotan/leviathan cognate with the Baal Cycle passage; and the Tel Hazor seal. **But what was read were SECONDARY summaries** — encyclopedia entries, a book's descriptive blurb, an academic society article, and press coverage of the seal's publication. **No primary source was opened:** not the Ugaritic text itself (KTU 1.5 I), not Schaeffer's excavation reports, not Uehlinger's *Near Eastern Archaeology* article on the seal. So the discovery dates, the sign count, the decipherers and the cognate are carried as **well-attested secondary scholarship**, which is the right confidence for a lesson and is not the same as collation. One divergence the search itself surfaced and the lesson therefore handles: the Ugaritic serpent epithets are rendered variously — "fleeing"/"fugitive" as commonly as "wriggling" — so the lesson says the pairs stand *remarkably close* rather than claiming them identical, and the claim that Isaiah directly quotes the Ugaritic is one scholar's position rather than consensus, which is why the lesson follows its own source in saying Isaiah almost certainly had no text in front of him.
10. **This is a lesson, not an archaeology course.** It does not survey the Ugaritic corpus, the Baal Cycle, or the Late Bronze Age collapse beyond what the teaching needs. `re-review: 2026-11-24` — read the lesson in each band on the live app and record whether a reader can perform the two moves on a source they met themselves, which is the only thing this lesson is for.
