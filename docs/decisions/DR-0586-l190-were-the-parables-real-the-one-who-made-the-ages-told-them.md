## What Darrell sent

> *"Lesson. How do we know of the stories or parables Jesus told were real or not? They could be from other timelines in other ages..."*

A question spoken into the app, which under the standing rule is build input. It is answered from the Word only, and the lesson says out loud where the Word is silent.

## The answer, and where each part comes from

1. **The form was chosen on purpose and prophesied.** "without a parable spake he not unto them" (Matthew 13:34) fulfils "I will open my mouth in a parable: I will utter dark sayings of old" (Psalm 78:2). Mark 4:33-34 shows the calibration to the crowd and the private exposition; Matthew 13:10-13 gives the purpose in Jesus' own words.
2. **The Word labels its own genres.** A likeness opens as one: "The kingdom of heaven is likened unto a man which sowed good seed in his field" (Matthew 13:24); "A certain man had two sons" (Luke 15:11). A record opens by its sources and purpose: Luke 1:1-4, John 21:24, and Peter's "we have not followed cunningly devised fables" (2 Peter 1:16). The reader is not left to sort them by instinct.
3. **A story can be invented and its verdict true.** Nathan's ewe lamb (2 Samuel 12:1-7): no rich man, no lamb, and "Thou art the man" (2 Samuel 12:7). This is the anatomy of a parable, and it reframes the question: real or not concerns the characters, who were never the target.
4. **The Teller made the ages.** "by whom also he made the worlds" (Hebrews 1:2); Hebrews 11:3; John 1:3; Colossians 1:17; "Before Abraham was, I am" (John 8:58); Hebrews 13:8; Isaiah 46:10; Ecclesiastes 1:9; Matthew 13:35 and Revelation 13:8 on "from the foundation of the world". So the parables are true in every age because their Author stands over every age.
5. **Other timelines are the Word's silence, and the lesson does not fill it.** Deuteronomy 29:29; 1 Timothy 1:4; Colossians 2:8; 2 Timothy 4:4. The one named parable (Luke 16:19-20, Lazarus) is left exactly where the Word leaves it.
6. **How the words are known to be His.** John 14:26; 2 Timothy 3:16; the fruit test of Matthew 7:16-20; John 6:63.

**One word-study claim, flagged (DR-0076 §8).** The lesson says the Greek word rendered *worlds* in Hebrews 1:2 is the word for *the ages* (aiōn, Strong's G165). The repo holds no Strong's corpus, so this is not gate-verified; it is standard, uncontested lexical fact and is stated as such, in prose, never inside a quotation.

## Not claimed

Nothing here decides whether any parable's characters lived. The lesson teaches that the Word marks its genres, that a parable's truth arrives at the hearer regardless, and that the Author of the ages told them. It asserts no timeline the Word does not describe.
