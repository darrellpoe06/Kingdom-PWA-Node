## Why this course

Nine courses covered the ground, the stewardship, the transaction, the tenant, the building, the partner, the lender, the record and the loss. Every one of them depends on an act none of them teaches: **looking at a thing properly before you are bound to it.**

## The find this course is built on

**Leviticus 14:33-45 is a complete, staged building inspection in the law of Yahweh,** and it carries an element almost no modern protocol has:

1. **The owner reports a suspicion, not a diagnosis** — *"It seemeth to me there is as it were a plague in the house"* (14:35).
2. **The house is emptied before the inspector enters,** and the reason is written into the verse: *"that all that is in the house be not made unclean"* (14:36). The inspection is ordered so it cannot condemn the contents merely by taking place. The principle generalises: **an inspection must not do harm merely by happening.**
3. **Stated visible criteria** — hollow strakes, greenish or reddish, lower than the wall (14:37). Observations a man can be held to, not impressions.
4. **A waiting period and a second look** — shut it up seven days, and *"the priest shall come again the seventh day, and shall look"* (14:38-39). A spreading defect and a static one are not the same defect and one look cannot tell them apart. The law does not require the inspector to be clever enough to tell at a glance; it gives him **time and a second measurement**.
5. **Targeted remediation with disposal away from the site** (14:40-42) — the affected stones, not the whole house.
6. **A re-inspection of the repair** (14:44) — the stage at which most real processes quietly stop.
7. **A stopping rule, named in advance** (14:45).

A household that ran only those seven steps on a building would be ahead of most buyers alive.

## The arc, one argument in eight moves

1. **The plague in the walls** — Leviticus 14:33-45
2. **I viewed the walls** — Nehemiah 2:11-18 (the night survey taken before a word was said; the rubble beat his transport and he kept going on foot)
3. **See the land, what it is** — Numbers 13:17-20 (six answerable pairs, and *bring of the fruit*)
4. **Two reports, one land** — Numbers 13:25-33; 14:6-9, 24
5. **They came to search it out** — Joshua 2:1-6, 23-24 (the second survey, scoped smaller and reported shorter)
6. **Man looketh on the outward appearance** — 1 Samuel 16:6-7; John 7:24; Proverbs 14:15
7. **He digged deep** — Luke 6:46-49; Isaiah 28:16-17
8. **Prove all things** — 1 Thessalonians 5:21; Acts 17:11; 1 John 4:1; 2 Kings 22:7

## The four limits this course holds, each pinned as a machine check

1. **Leviticus 14 is NOT a building code.** The text states plainly that Yahweh put the condition there (14:34). This is His law concerning ritual uncleanness, and it **must never be used to tell a family that damp walls or a failing roof are a judgment on them** — that is a cruelty the text does not license. Why one house had it is His business and the passage does not hand it to us. The **procedure** carries, completely.
2. **The ten were not faulted for reporting the facts.** Strong people, walled cities, the children of Anak — every clause of Numbers 13:28 is true and is exactly what the brief in 13:18 asked them to establish, and Caleb contradicted none of it. Teaching the chapter as though honest measurement was the sin inverts the brief. What Scripture itself calls an evil report is the addition in **13:32** — *a land that eateth up the inhabitants*, which contradicts the milk and honey they were holding — with its tell in **13:33**, where the first half is honest self-report (*in our own sight*) and the second hands in a feeling as a fact about the enemy's perception (*and so we were in their sight*). Nobody interviewed a giant.
3. **1 Samuel 16:7 is about Yahweh choosing a king.** It is no reason to skip a foundation, a document, a survey or a reference, and it is deployed constantly to shut down scrutiny — usually by whoever would prefer not to be examined. It names what the eye cannot **reach**; it nowhere says close the eye. The working instruction is John 7:24, where **JUDGE appears in both halves**: judge not by appearance, judge *righteously* — more evidence, not less.
4. **Stay where Scripture stays, in three places.** On Joshua 2 the Word records Rahab's words to the king's men and commends her faith elsewhere without evaluating them, so this course builds no doctrine on that silence **in either direction** — no defence and no rebuke. On Luke 6, verses 46-47 make hearing-and-doing the topic and the house the illustration; a teacher who reduces it to construction advice has taken the smaller half. And on 2 Kings 22:7, *no reckoning made* is a **description** of those men, never a licence to dismantle a control — if somebody quotes it that way, ask who benefits.

## Where the course is going, which is not suspicion

Lesson eight closes on Josiah's repair fund: *"Howbeit there was no reckoning made with them of the money that was delivered into their hand, because they dealt faithfully."* (2 Kings 22:7) **The trust came after the record and did not replace it.** That is the destination of eight sessions of careful looking — not permanent suspicion, which is exhausting to carry and dishonouring to good people, but a class of people and places you no longer have to check because a record exists that earned it. And the final instrument is to name the person whose record retired the reckoning, and tell them why.
