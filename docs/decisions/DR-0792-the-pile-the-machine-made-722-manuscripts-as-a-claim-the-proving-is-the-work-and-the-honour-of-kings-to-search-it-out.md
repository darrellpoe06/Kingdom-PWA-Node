## Context

The issue's lead story reports that OpenAI released a body of mathematical research produced by a model it has not shipped: *"722 manuscripts across 372 families of related results after being given roughly 4,000 open research problems."* with about three hours of paid thinking compute per result, many proofs carrying Lean formalizations so a computer can mechanically check each step — and then, to the newsletter's credit, it takes its own number down in the same story: *"But this is not 722 gold-star-certified discoveries."* and *"OpenAI says the collection contains results at different stages of verification, not every manuscript has a Lean proof yet, and some unformalized results could still contain errors."* Its own reading of what changed is the hinge this lesson turns on: *"The research bottleneck may be moving."* and *"Not how many manuscripts the model can generate, but how many survive serious independent scrutiny."*

A claim that one model handled seven hundred and twenty-two mathematical manuscripts is exactly the kind of number that gets repeated as fact and then becomes common knowledge with no source behind it. So the lesson carries it as a claim, attributed, on a named day, quoted word for word — and the Word governs the question underneath it: measure and number are part of a frame that was written first (Hebrews 11:3), He conceals on purpose so that searching it out is kingly honour (Proverbs 25:2), and the ratio the industry just discovered is one the Lamb named looking at a crowd (Matthew 9:37).

## Decision

- The headline figure is **carried as a claim and never restated as established fact**, in our own voice as well as in quotation: the lesson says in so many words that it is *The Neuron's report of what OpenAI says*, repeats the publisher's own caveat as loudly as the figure, and a dedicated check refuses prose of the form "the model proved/discovered 722" or "722 confirmed discoveries" (proven-to-catch).
- **Three piles, kept apart, in the lesson itself** (what the source said / what this house has built, with its limit / what we did not check and so do not teach as fact), and **DR-0100's three tiers** named in the text: machine-checked proof is established and stated plainly; whether the collection is good mathematics is open and flagged **narrowly**; over-reach is corrected in **both** directions — "the machine discovers truth now" by Proverbs 18:17, and "none of it is real, it is all marketing" by Daniel 12:4.
- **All four bands at full coverage** (P60), each a full reading that names its lesson in its opening, differentiated rather than resized, ascending in length and in reading grade, and each ending with its own three talk-together directions.
- `sovereign-ai-sov34-verses.test.js` had an order check of the form "sov34 is LAST", with a proven-to-catch asserting that appending a `sov35-` week reports *not last*. It was true the day week 34 landed and false the moment any later week was written — week 36 made that file red without week 34 changing by one letter. It is **re-anchored to what week 34 actually promises** (it comes directly after week 33, and no higher week is filed before it), which holds for every later week and still fails on a week filed out of place. Week 36's own order check is written in that durable form from birth. **A gate that fails on correct new work is a tax, not a brake.**
- **Week 35 is recorded as held in flight** in `sovereign-ai-class.test.js` (`HELD_IN_FLIGHT = [35]`), dated, following the convention `living-lessons-id-collision.test.js` documents. That entry is deleted in the merge that brings week 35 in — which the same assertion forces, because it fails the moment 35 is present.
- Counts stay **derived** (DR-0677): `weeks` is a getter over the series, and the course-band baseline was regenerated from a fresh scan by `course-band-baseline-write.mjs` rather than hand-edited.
