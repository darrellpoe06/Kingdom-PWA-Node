## Impact

A teaching Darrell sent on 2026-10-13 sat for **23 days** and is now **ll274** in **Living Lessons**. That placement is by subject and was measured rather than assumed: the material is neither technology (Sovereign A.I.), nor health (Healthy Living), nor project organization (Project Management). It is a question about stewardship, public trust and the standard of judgment — devotional and ethical ground — and Living Lessons already holds the neighbouring disciplines, which is precisely how the collisions above were found. The number is contiguous: the course runs to ll273 and this is ll274.

**The teaching is that a trust is spent at a cost to the one who spends it, or it has not been honestly spent.** The Word's own cases run both directions. Portions collected for workers that never arrived, with the rulers contended with rather than the workers or the givers — *"Then contended I with the rulers"* (Nehemiah 13:11) — and a repair that is an office rather than an appeal: four men named in the record, *"for they were counted faithful, and their office was to distribute unto their brethren"* (Nehemiah 13:13). Against it, seventy pieces of silver *"out of the house of baalberith"* (Judges 9:4) that bought one man a following, and Jotham's parable, where the olive declines because *"Should I leave my fatness, wherewith by me they honour God and man"* (Judges 9:9) while the bramble — which casts no shade — says *"come and put your trust in my shadow: and if not, let fire come out of the bramble"* (Judges 9:15), and the fire runs both ways in the end (Judges 9:20). Then the name: a prince who *"reared up for himself a pillar"* on public ground and *"called the pillar after his own name"* (2 Samuel 18:18), **for a reason the lesson refuses to read smugly** — he was afraid of being forgotten, which is ordinary, and is why the Word wrote it down. And the inverse, from a king handed a lawful free altar, free credit and a whole nation's relief with the city watching: *"neither will I offer burnt offerings unto the LORD my God of that which doth cost me nothing"* (2 Samuel 24:24). The cost is what makes it his.

**And the lesson's second half is the part that makes it teachable about the news without taking a side.** Judgment is a public trust on exactly the same terms, so it answers the same two questions. An accusation that **cost its speaker nothing** — reporting another man's inner motive, from the only person who gains if it is believed, about a man who is absent and never asked — moved an entire estate in one verse: *"Behold, thine are all that pertained unto Mephibosheth"* (2 Samuel 16:4). The accused had spent every day of the absence in mourning (2 Samuel 19:24), his account was *"my servant deceived me"* (2 Samuel 19:26), and the ruling was never reversed, only split: *"Thou and Ziba divide the land"* (2 Samuel 19:29). **The teaching there is not mainly about the servant who lied. It is that a verdict issued at no cost becomes extremely expensive to reverse — one sentence made it, and undoing it defeated a king.** Against that, a hearing done right in far worse conditions: a crowd seizing a man with the verdict already settled (Jeremiah 26:8), the charge stated formally, and then **the one step the earlier passage skipped** — the accused permitted to speak in his own words in front of everybody (Jeremiah 26:12), ending in *"This man is not worthy to die"* (Jeremiah 26:16) from the officials **and the same crowd**, with no new facts at all, at real cost to their standing; and then the elders paying a second price by searching the record and reading out a precedent (Jeremiah 26:19). The standard the lesson leaves the reader with is Job's: *"I was a father to the poor: and the cause which I knew not I searched out"* (Job 29:16) — the unfamiliar cause, the one with no advocate, the one that obliges you to go and find things out, pursued at your own cost; and it had teeth, *"And I brake the jaws of the wicked"* (Job 29:17). **Searching out a cause is not softness. It is the only way to break a jaw and be sure it was the right one.**

**Four things are worth carrying forward.**

First, **a charged source is teachable without a side, and the absence of a side is a machine check rather than a promise.** The forbidden-names sweep runs over every string of the module, is proven to catch a planted politician's name and a planted publisher credit, and is **scoped rather than hollowed**: it excludes the verbatim fetch error DR-0108 requires, and a separate check asserts that every occurrence of the publisher's host is inside that error.
