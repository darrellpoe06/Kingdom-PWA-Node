## What was measured

**The research road returned a service notice, and that is the lesson's honest centre.** The reading step never completed — the item's own words: *"not made yet (the tower had not answered); the source below is the whole of the research."* What came back was the project's page carrying its maintainers' notice that the website and other online resources had been taken offline **as a precaution following news of a critical vulnerability affecting PHP**, with thanks to members who helped assess it — plus a one-paragraph product description.

**The accident that became the lesson.** We went to read about a validator and found its website dark. The validator does not depend on PHP; what went offline was the shopfront. The tool's own work was untouched, **because it runs on the user's machine and not on the maintainers'.** That is this course's sovereignty argument arriving as evidence instead of assertion, and it is why the week exists in this form. The maintainers' conduct is taught as the model — they acted before being forced and said why in public (Proverbs 22:3) — with one correction held beside it: open source means the logic is **readable**, not that every machine near it is safe.
