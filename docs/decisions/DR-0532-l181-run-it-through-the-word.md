# DR-0532 — L181 Run It Through the Word, and the verse that had never been taught

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-19
- **Type:** content
- **Relates to:** DR-0076 (measure, do not claim), DR-0098 (teach the Word, do not platform man's disagreement), DR-0459 (no ellipsis inside a quotation), DR-0509 (the lesson contract), DR-0530 (L180), DR-0531 (typographic theology surface)

## What Darrell spoke

A teaching, prefixed "Lesson.", told as it happened. His wife was at choir rehearsal singing a song whose lyric said she would never let the Holy Spirit down, and she stopped — not from unbelief but from **reverence**. She did not want to declare an absolute to Yahweh and then, unintentionally and in a situation she could not foresee, do exactly that.

The choir pushed back with their own lenses. They stayed in it until the Word settled it. Then everybody sang, gladly.

His thesis, in his words:

> *"Until you look at the two different minds the word talks about, the carnal mind and the spiritual mind, and you process the carnal mind's thinking through the spiritual lens to ensure that what it is that the word is saying you're okay with, and you can see it in the word and it is within the realms of what the word says is okay. That is how you reprogram and renew your mind."*

## What was measured before a word was written

Against the 179 existing lessons:

| Verse | Lessons already carrying it |
|---|---|
| Romans 8:6 — carnally vs spiritually minded | **11** |
| Ecclesiastes 5:5 — better not to vow | 2, never applied to a lyric |
| **Jude 1:24 — able to keep you from falling** | **0** |

So the two-minds **doctrine is not new here** — L177 is literally "Two Minds, and the One You Feed" — and the lesson says so plainly instead of claiming novelty. What is new is the **resolution**, and the verse that resolves it had never appeared in 180 lessons.

## The settlement, which is the lesson

Her caution is backed by the text: *"Better is it that thou shouldest not vow, than that thou shouldest vow and not pay."* (Ecclesiastes 5:5) And Peter is the case study, with the detail that matters — **all the disciples said it too** (Matthew 26:35). A room agreeing with you is not evidence you can do the thing.

The choir were holding real Scripture as well: the confession of Romans 4:17, mustard-seed faith, and the Daniel 1:8 posture of a heart purposed in advance.

Then the Word answers their instinct **more precisely than they said it**. They reached for *even against my own will, I will do what He says*; Philippians 2:13 says He works *"both to will and to do of his good pleasure."* He works the willing itself.

And Jude 1:24 ends it: *"Now unto him that is able to keep you from falling."* The subject is **Him**. The lyric was never a warranty on her steadiness; it is a confession of His keeping, and in that reading it is truer than the anxious version rather than weaker.

Both propositions stand together: she cannot guarantee she will not fall, **and** He is able to keep her from falling.

## The distinction that must not be flattened

Her thought — *I might fail, so I cannot say it* — **was not false.** She might. A carnal-mind thought is frequently accurate about the flesh and simply cannot see past it, which is why the remedy is neither denial nor self-reproach. Every band carries this, and the test pins it: a lesson that missed it would teach readers to argue with something partly true.

## DR-0098 applied where it was hardest

The customary ending is two sincere readings set side by side, relabelled "perspective", with nobody changed. The lesson **names that move and refuses it** in the reader's hearing, because the rehearsal itself refused it — they stayed in the text until the text decided, and the singing followed the settlement rather than substituting for it.

## The proof outside the room

The next morning his wife did not feel like walking, and recognised *"that her feelings had nothing to do with her capability."* They walked a mile. She then ran the same process on her notes and finished those too. **What changed was not her mood — it was her actions**, brought, in his words, in subjection underneath the will of Yahweh.
