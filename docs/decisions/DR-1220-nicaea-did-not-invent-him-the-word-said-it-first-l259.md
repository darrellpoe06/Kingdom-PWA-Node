### The external claims, verified independently (DR-0192, DR-0076 §7)

The source is one video's claims. Each checkable external claim was verified against sources outside the video before it was taught, and tiered per DR-0100.

**Tier 1 — established, stated plainly:**
- Nicaea met in 325, convened by Constantine, to settle the dispute raised by **Arius** — that the Son was a created being, that there was a time when the Son was not. Both parties argued from Scripture, which is only possible if Scripture was already the shared authority.
- **What survives from the council is a creed, twenty disciplinary canons and a synodal decree. There is no list of books among them.** Verified against reference accounts of the council's surviving documents; it is a statement about the surviving record, which is exactly what can be checked.
- The creed confesses the Son **begotten, not made, of one substance with the Father** — clarifying language aimed at one denial.
- The earliest surviving list of the twenty-seven New Covenant books is in **Athanasius's festal letter of 367**, forty-two years *after* Nicaea, and reads as a record of what congregations already used. This repository already holds that fact independently: `app/src/lib/yahweh-by-century.js`, the `ad-4th` entry, which states "The canon was not decided at Nicaea" and cites Athanasius, Festal Letter 39.
- The **Megiddo** prayer hall at Kefar 'Othnay (Legio) was excavated by the Israel Antiquities Authority **between 2003 and 2005**; the excavators' preliminary publication is titled for a Christian prayer hall of the **third century**, and coins found with the mosaic are third-century. The inscription is published as the god-loving Akeptous having offered the table to God Jesus Christ as a memorial.
- The **Alexamenos graffito** was uncovered on the Palatine Hill in the 1850s, in the Paedagogium where household slaves were trained; it shows a figure with a donkey's head on a cross and reads that Alexamenos worships his god; it is in the Palatine Museum.

**Tier 2 — genuinely unsettled, flagged narrowly and taught as an estimate, never as a date:**
- The **about-230 date** for the Megiddo mosaic is a published *estimate* from coins and construction, not a dated document. The lesson says so in every band that carries it, and the gate requires the word "estimate" and what the estimate rests on.
- The graffito's **date is a range**: published estimates run from about AD 200 into the Severan period (late 2nd to early 3rd century). The video's own reasoning places it earlier — anywhere from the end of the 1st century to the mid-2nd. **This house does not adopt the earlier end**, says so, and the gate pins that refusal.
- The **"earliest proclamation of Jesus as God"** framing around the Megiddo find comes largely from exhibition press coverage. The lesson does not use it; it claims only what the find supports — a congregation calling Jesus God in a floor roughly a century before the council.

**Tier 3 — a claim the record corrects, while the true data under it stands:**
- The video reports, from the hagiography of Nicholas of Myra, that a bishop struck Arius across the face at the council. The account is from a **much later life of a saint**, not from any record of the proceedings. **The lesson refuses to teach it as history**, says why, and the gate requires that refusal in four places. Nothing else in the video's account of the Arian controversy depends on it.

**What the graffito does and does not prove, said plainly rather than oversold.** It is a **mockery inscription**. It settles no doctrine. What it proves is that the belief existed and was common enough in Rome to be worth insulting — somebody knew a young man named Alexamenos worshipped a crucified man as a god, and sneered at him for it. Saying that plainly is stronger than inflating it, and the lesson says the sneer is the evidence.

**Not verified, and named as such.** The identification of the lake-bed ruins at Iznik as the actual building where the council sat is carried in the lesson exactly as the video carries it — *may well have sat* — and nothing is built on it. The attendance figures the video gives ("3 to 400 bishops", "the largest assembly of believers in the world at that point") are **not taught**: the early counts genuinely differ (more than 250 in Eusebius, about 270 in Eustathius, 318 in Athanasius, with 318 read by some as symbolic), so no number is asserted. The Ignatius of Antioch quotations the video reads are real and early, but they reach this house only through the captions and an unnamed English translation, so they are **not quoted in the lesson** — the lesson stands on the KJV corpus on disk, which it can verify.

## The decision

**The lesson teaches the Word's own pre-Nicene witness, and the history is the ninth movement, not the spine.** Eleven numbered movements in the adult teaching and in each band: the claim named once; John 1 ("In the beginning was the Word"); Thomas's confession and the silence that follows it; the Son as Maker and sustainer; the Father addressing the Son as God (Psalms 45:6 assigned to the Son in Hebrews 1:8); His own testimony and the charge at His trial; the Lamb of Yahweh worshipped in heaven; the writings already binding and already called Scripture by each other; the history with its estimates marked; and how to stand when the claim comes.

**Placement: Living Lessons** (`app/src/lib/living-lessons-class.js`), appended after ll258, and the reason is the shelf. The anchor opens on John 1:1, so the course's own section helper shelves it with the Gospels, next to ll39 — the hidden-Jerusalem lesson, which mentions Nicaea five times while teaching something else entirely (discernment in three tiers about a viral geography claim). ll39 holds one sentence of this subject, *"Nicaea did not invent the deity of Christ; it confirmed, against a denier, what the Word had already said"*; this lesson is the direct treatment that sentence points at, and it is cross-referenced rather than duplicated: ll39's three-tier discernment frame is not re-taught, and this lesson does not re-litigate the geography claim. The `ad-4th` entry in `app/src/lib/yahweh-by-century.js` carries the same facts as a *century record* (firsts, provision, what would have happened without Him); a timeline entry is not a lesson and has no bands, no stories and no quiz, so nothing there is duplicated either — the lesson is where a reader is taught, the century record is where a reader looks it up.

**The gate pins what a reader actually meets:**
- **27 strands quoted in the adult lesson AND in all four bands**, each fragment *measured* present in all five places rather than assumed — among them John 1:1, John 1:3, John 1:14, John 20:28, John 20:29, Matthew 16:16, Colossians 1:17, Colossians 2:9, Hebrews 1:6, Hebrews 1:8, John 5:23, John 8:58, John 10:30, John 10:33, John 1:29, Revelation 5:12, 2 Timothy 3:16, 2 Peter 1:21, 2 Peter 3:16, Colossians 4:16, 1 Thessalonians 5:27, 1 Peter 3:15, Isaiah 40:8, Deuteronomy 6:7 and Proverbs 27:17;

The two stories teach the lesson rather than decorate it:
- **The Deed and the Meeting** (light; John 1:1): a man with a folder argues that a political county hearing in 1962 decided a farm's boundary, and a grandmother answers with a deed from 1889. A meeting cannot grant or remove what a deed already says.
- **The Boy Who Was Teased at the Gate** (solemn; John 1:29): the graffito told as what it is — a cruelty that preserved the faith it meant to shame. The one who sneered is nameless; the one sneered at is named on the wall.

## Impact

A reader who meets this claim — in a video, from a grandchild, on television — has an answer that fits on a card and does not require them to win an argument about Roman politics. A child learns that you cannot vote sugar into a cake that is already baked. A youth learns to check the dates before the argument starts. A teen learns the shape of the trick, which is that the claim never asks you to open the first-century documents. A senior, who is the one in the family most likely to be asked, gets the order of events and the four verses, and the dignity of knowing the honest limits of the evidence they are carrying. Every band is sent to their parents, their children and a friend with the same question: what have you been told about where the Bible came from?

It also sets a pattern for the next apologetics lesson in this series: the popular false claim is **named once and answered by the Word**, the real history is stated plainly where it is established, and the two archaeological witnesses are carried at the weight the published record gives them — which means the lesson is *more* persuasive, not less, because nothing in it has to be walked back.

### Limits, named rather than left

- **No minute marks, by the route's design.** The transcript route joins caption segments and drops timestamps. Nothing in this lesson is cited to a minute of the video, and nothing should be added later claiming one from this record.
- **The captions are a machine's hearing.** They mishear the host's name as "Weston" repeatedly. The lesson therefore names the hosts only as the *source summary* names them — Wesley Huff and Andy Steiger — and puts no sentence in a living person's mouth beyond what the source itself states.
- **Two dates are estimates and stay estimates.** About 230 for the Megiddo mosaic, and a range from about 200 into the Severan period for the graffito. If a future edit upgrades either into a date, the gate's `estimate` cases go red; that is deliberate.
- **The video's graffito dating is not adopted.** Its reasoning (end of the 1st century to mid-2nd, from the building's construction and conversion) is earlier than the published range usually runs. The disagreement is stated in the lesson rather than resolved; this house does not have the excavation record in hand to settle it. `re-review: 2027-01-27` — if a primary publication of the Paedagogium graffiti is obtained, revisit the range and say so.
- **The Ignatius quotations are left out** rather than relayed, because they arrive only through captions and an unnamed English translation. That is a real gap in the lesson's early-witness section: the second-century written witness to the Son's Deity is taught from Scripture alone here. `re-review: 2027-01-27` — if a citable edition of Ignatius's letters is added to the repository's reference holdings, a short early-witness movement can be added with the same verbatim discipline the KJV corpus gets.
- **No attendance number is taught**, because the early counts differ. If a reader asks "how many bishops?", the lesson deliberately does not answer.
- **"God" stands in our own voice in a narrow, named set of places** — where we report what the quoted text itself does ("the Father addresses the Son as God"), where we render the Megiddo inscription's own words ("God Jesus Christ"), and where we speak of invented religions in general ("no invented religion makes its own God vulnerable"). Everywhere this house names the Father or the one true God in its own voice, it says **Yahweh** (DR-0210). The judgment is recorded here rather than left for a reader to wonder about: substituting "Yahweh" into a report of the KJV's own wording would misstate what the text says, which is the error the bright line exists to prevent.
- **The Word is senior to the archaeology, and the lesson says so.** If both finds were withdrawn tomorrow, nothing in the teaching would move: the argument rests on first-century text, and the finds are corroboration.

## Proven to catch

`living-lessons-ll259-nicaea-did-not-invent-him.test.js` — **84 cases, all passing.** It was proven to catch before it was trusted (DR-0076 §3): with `is for ever and ever` altered to `is forever and ever` throughout the ll259 module in the catalog, **five cases went red** — the "every pinned verse is quoted somewhere" check, the per-band spine check, the wide every-referenced-span check (which names the field, including `bigIdea`), and both of the gate's own mutation cases — and all 84 passed again the moment the file was restored. A first attempt at this proof altered only the first occurrence after the module id and the suite stayed green; that is why the wide `scanQuotedVerses` case over `quotedTexts` was added, so an altered verse in a benefit, a quiz explanation or a talking point is caught by *this* gate and not only by the catalog-wide one.

The gate's four built-in mutation cases:
- an altered Hebrews 1:8 is named wherever it sits — a band, the big idea, a benefit or a talking point;
- "Yahweh" substituted into a quotation of the Word is detected inside the quotation marks;
- a band stripped of only its friend-to-friend sentences is named by `placesMissingDirections` while `hasAllThree` still reads green, which is the hazard DR-0795 exists for;
- the word "estimate" removed from the senior band's history turns the restraint case red.

Catalog-wide suites run with it: living-lessons-full-levels, order, numbers-are-unique, id-collision, stories, age-appropriateness, research-integrity, sections, reading-level-gate, band-differentiation-gate, quotation-integrity-gate, quoted-verse-is-the-verse, the-voice-is-heard-as-written and lesson-completeness — 184 cases.
