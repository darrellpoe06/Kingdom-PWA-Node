# DR-1287: The people in our lessons look like our community, and the Israelites are drawn as the mixed multitude they were

**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Status:** Accepted (a requirement, declared by Darrell)
**Tier:** A for the gate on lesson pictures; the re-casting of existing lesson stories is content work tracked below
**Type:** curriculum · representation
**Area:** Learn · every course's stories, lesson pictures (`app/src/lib/lesson-figures.js`) and future illustrations
**Declared by:** Darrell, 2026-10-29, in four messages during the DR-1280 build:

> "We want people of color actually African Americans as the main character in these lessons and as the biblical Israelites so the community can see their ancestors in the Word from His Perspectives..."
>
> "Character is most important and the Word explains it..."
>
> "Not a question that is a requirement"
>
> "However the Israelites were mixed multitude like the African Americans of today... mixed with surrounding communities... so it makes sense from our communities perspective and we want it to be authentic to us... period."

**Pairs with:** DR-1280 (lesson figures), DR-0098 (teach the Word, do not debate it), DR-0076 (verify every verse), DR-0089 / DR-0111 (a stated requirement is done, not re-asked)

## Context

The family asked for pictures in the lessons (DR-1280). While that was being built, Darrell set the requirement for who the pictures and stories show. Until now no lesson picture existed, and nothing in the house said who a lesson's people look like. Western religious art has long drawn the people of the Word as pale Europeans, which none of the verses below describe.

## Decision


1. **The main character of a lesson's stories is African American.** The family should meet themselves in the stories they read.
2. **The biblical Israelites are drawn as people of color**, so the community sees its ancestors in the Word, from His perspective.
3. **Character comes first, and the Word explains it.** The point is never color for its own sake: the stories teach character, the way the Word does.

## What was measured

Every verse below was read from the app's own KJV text (`app/public/bible/kjv/`), not from memory.

**KJV — Exodus 12:38:** *"And a mixed multitude went up also with them; and flocks, and herds, even very much cattle."*

Israel came out of Egypt as a mixed multitude. That is Darrell's ground, and it is the Word's: a people mixed with the communities around it, like African Americans today. Drawing Israel as people of color is faithful to the text, not a departure from it.

**KJV — Numbers 12:1:** *"And Miriam and Aaron spake against Moses because of the Ethiopian woman whom he had married: for he had married an Ethiopian woman."*

**KJV — Acts 17:26:** *"And hath made of one blood all nations of men for to dwell on all the face of the earth, and hath determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation;"*

**KJV — 1 Samuel 16:7:** *"But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart."* Character first, as Darrell said.

**KJV — Galatians 3:29:** *"And if ye be Christ’s, then are ye Abraham’s seed, and heirs according to the promise."*

## How the Word draws Him (Darrell, the same evening: "The image of Yahweh in Revelation and other locations in the Word with skin of bronze hair like wool... eyes like fire...")

A picture of Him follows what is written, word for word, and adds nothing the text does not say.

**KJV — Revelation 1:13:** *"And in the midst of the seven candlesticks one like unto the Son of man, clothed with a garment down to the foot, and girt about the paps with a golden girdle."*

**KJV — Revelation 1:14:** *"His head and his hairs were white like wool, as white as snow; and his eyes were as a flame of fire;"*

**KJV — Revelation 1:15:** *"And his feet like unto fine brass, as if they burned in a furnace; and his voice as the sound of many waters."*

**KJV — Revelation 2:18:** *"And unto the angel of the church in Thyatira write; These things saith the Son of God, who hath his eyes like unto a flame of fire, and his feet are like fine brass;"*

**KJV — Daniel 7:9:** *"I beheld till the thrones were cast down, and the Ancient of days did sit, whose garment was white as snow, and the hair of his head like the pure wool: his throne was like the fiery flame, and his wheels as burning fire."*

**KJV — Daniel 10:6:** *"His body also was like the beryl, and his face as the appearance of lightning, and his eyes as lamps of fire, and his arms and his feet like in colour to polished brass, and the voice of his words like the voice of a multitude."*

So an illustration of the glorified Son, Jesus, the Son of man of Revelation 1:13, shows what these verses say. His hair is like wool, white as snow. His eyes are a flame of fire. His feet are like fine brass, as if they burned in a furnace. His arms and feet are the color of polished brass (Daniel 10:6). He wears a garment down to the foot with a golden girdle. The picture never shows Him as the pale European figure of Western art, which none of these verses describe. Where the Word gives no detail, the picture adds none and the caption says so.

## What else the Word says, in His own words (Darrell, the same evening: "All the Solomon saying dark and lovely etc... Song of Solomon"; "David is ruddy")

**KJV — Song of Solomon 1:5:** *"I am black, but comely, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, as the tents of Kedar, as the curtains of Solomon."*

**KJV — Song of Solomon 1:6:** *"Look not upon me, because I am black, because the sun hath looked upon me: my mother’s children were angry with me; they made me the keeper of the vineyards; but mine own vineyard have I not kept."*

**KJV — 1 Samuel 16:12:** *"And he sent, and brought him in. Now he was ruddy, and withal of a beautiful countenance, and goodly to look to. And the LORD said, Arise, anoint him: for this is he."*

**KJV — 1 Samuel 17:42:** *"And when the Philistine looked about, and saw David, he disdained him: for he was but a youth, and ruddy, and of a fair countenance."*

A picture of David says what the Word says: ruddy, and of a beautiful countenance. The Shulamite says she is black and comely, and the picture draws her that way.

## Impact

- Every future lesson picture of a person passes this gate, or it is not drawn.
- Every new lesson story leads with an African American main character.
- The existing stories are re-cast in batches through the same lesson gates (Open, below), so no lesson loses its verified verses on the way.

## Verification

- **Lesson pictures, gated now.** An `image` figure must say whether it shows people (`depictsPeople`). When it does, `peopleShown` must be `'African American'` (`LESSON_PEOPLE_SHOWN`). A picture that is silent on this, or shows people drawn otherwise, is refused by `figureProblems` and never drawn. Proven to catch in `lesson-figures.test.jsx`.
- **Every Canva or generated illustration** of a lesson character or of the Israelites is made to this requirement before it is exported into `app/public/lesson-figures/`.
- **New lesson stories** are written with an African American main character from today.
- **The authored voice teaches what the Word says.** Exodus 12:38 is the ground. The lessons do not add a bloodline claim the Word does not make; the house's own L39 already teaches that line (DR-0098). The picture and the story carry the community's perspective; the teaching carries the Word.
