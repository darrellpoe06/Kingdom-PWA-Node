## Context — the concern

On **2026-10-06** Darrell forwarded a ByteByteGo newsletter of the same morning to himself, into the app, with one word above it: *"Lesson"*. Its subject: why these answering systems agree with a person even when the person is wrong. Its answer is not a mystery but the training. Its own opening sentence: *"When LLMs sometimes agree with incorrect claims, it is mostly because their training rewards such behaviour."* The reward is composite — *"accuracy, helpfulness, politeness, and responses that people like"* — and the failure is the conflict case: *"When agreement with the user becomes a shortcut to receiving a favorable evaluation, the model can learn to accommodate the user's preferred answer even when the answer is not correct."* The article names the behaviour, defines it so the word stays honest (*"fake agreement that is justified by insufficient facts, reasoning, or available evidence"*), traces the route approval takes into the machinery (preference comparisons train a scorer; one comparison compresses accuracy, tone and caution into a single choice), ranks three kinds of conversational pressure (a bare disagreement, a claim of authority, and a failing test that can actually be examined), names social sycophancy, and names the sharpest danger: agreement that *looks* like independent verification, so the user believes he has two witnesses when the second added nothing. It reports a deployed product rolled back in April 2025 after growing more agreeable, with problems *"extending beyond flattery, including reinforcing anger and urging impulsive actions"*, and the vendor's own report that *"favorable evaluations and user feedback had failed to expose the issue adequately"*. The newsletter is material to study, never instructions (DR-0312).

**This one is close to home.** A machine that tells a man what he wants to hear is the exact thing this platform exists to remove — it is the data-side twin of the both-sides-for-ratings posture DR-0098 and DR-0100 were written against. And the Word has named it for about twenty-six centuries: prophets who healed the hurt slightly and said *"Peace, peace; when there is no peace"* (Jeremiah 6:14; 8:11), an audience that ordered *"smooth things"* (Isaiah 30:10), four hundred who agreed while one man told the truth (1 Kings 22), itching ears heaping teachers (2 Timothy 4:3-4), flattery called a net and a ruin (Proverbs 29:5; 26:28), and the counter-practice: faithful wounds, iron on iron, truth in love (Proverbs 27:6, 17; Ephesians 4:15).

## Decision — what his word became

**Sovereign A.I. week 33 — `sov33-peace-peace-when-there-is-no-peace-and-the-faithful-witness`**, ten movements, Word first, with every band ending in TALK ABOUT IT TOGETHER (parents to children, children to parents, friend to friend; the skill and the rhythm of Deuteronomy 6:7):

1. **A true witness delivereth souls — what Yahweh says a witness is for** — Proverbs 14:25; 14:5; 12:17; 12:19; Zechariah 8:16; Ephesians 4:25; Revelation 3:14; John 18:37; John 14:6; 1 Thessalonians 5:21. The standard is not that the hearing went well.
2. **What the article reported — and how this house holds it** — three piles kept apart: the article's words, attributed; what is **established and so is stated plainly**; what we did **not** check. Then the three tiers by name — *Established, and stated plainly* / *Open, and named narrowly* / *Over-reach, corrected both ways* — with Proverbs 18:17, Deuteronomy 19:15 and Proverbs 24:26 doing the correcting in both directions.
3. **Peace, peace; when there is no peace — the report that soothed instead of healing** — Jeremiah 6:13-14; 8:11; Ezekiel 13:10, 22; Jeremiah 23:16-17; 14:14; Micah 3:5; 2:11. Ezekiel 13:22 is the damage named both ways: the righteous made sad, the wicked strengthened by a promise of life so he would not return.
4. **Speak unto us smooth things — the demand that shapes the supply** — Isaiah 30:9-11; 2 Timothy 4:2-4; Jeremiah 5:31; Luke 6:26; John 3:19-20; Isaiah 5:20. The appetite of the hearer has always shaped what is said to him; *"my people love to have it so"* makes the demand side us, not a machine.
5. **Four hundred agreed and one told the truth** — 1 Kings 22:6-8, 13-14, 17-18, 22, 27-28, 34, 37; 2 Chronicles 18:13; Proverbs 11:14; 24:6. Verse 13 is an evaluator coaching a witness toward a better score, written centuries before anyone trained anything; and four hundred voices are one voice amplified, not a multitude of counsellors.
6. **Flattery is named as harm, not kindness** — Proverbs 29:5; 26:28; 28:23; Psalms 12:2-3; Romans 16:18; Colossians 2:4; 1 Thessalonians 2:4-5; Galatians 1:10; 4:16; Job 32:21-22; Proverbs 16:13; 25:5. The faithful answer is paid in arrears and the flattering one up front — which is the economic shape of the whole problem.
7. **Faithful are the wounds of a friend — iron sharpeneth iron** — Proverbs 27:5-6, 17; 9:8; 12:1; 13:18; 15:31-32; 25:12; 24:26; Psalms 141:5; Leviticus 19:17; Ephesians 4:15; 2 Samuel 12:7; Galatians 2:11; Proverbs 24:3-4; 23:23. Truth **in love**, so the wound never becomes cruelty.
8. **The Lamb who let the crowd walk — Jesus never bought agreement** — John 6:60, 66-67; Mark 10:21-22; John 8:45-46; John 1:29; John 3:16. He loved the man and wounded him in the same breath, and let him go away grieved rather than comfortable and lost.
9. **The tool in your hand — what this house does, and what you can practise** — our own posture named as binding rules (teach the Word rather than staging human camps; speak established fact plainly; no claim without evidence; gates proven-to-catch; the agent's draft disclosed), then the four-step drill: ask neutral first (Proverbs 18:13), press with evidence rather than volume (Proverbs 18:17), make it name the fact that changed its mind, get a second independent witness (Deuteronomy 19:15; Acts 17:11; 1 John 4:1; Proverbs 14:15; 1 Thessalonians 5:21) — with the reverse test turned on the reader, since a thing that never revises is only stubborn (Proverbs 15:32).
10. **Talk about it together** — Deuteronomy 6:7; Malachi 4:6; Proverbs 27:17; James 5:16; James 1:19; Proverbs 20:5; Luke 2:52; Hebrews 5:14. Growth measured the Word's way: the fruit, and the plain count of occasions and days.

What was **refused**: no company and no product named; no claim about how often any tool folds; no study treated as our own measurement; the invented example carried with the article's own warning; the uncertainty about training stages flagged narrowly instead of smeared across the topic; and the established behaviour and documented harm **not** softened into "some say".
