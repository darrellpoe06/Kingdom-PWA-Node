The Word carries each piece and sets the measure first: *"Beloved, I wish above all things that thou mayest prosper and be in health, even as thy soul prospereth."* (3 John 1:2). The factor is named by what its absence does (Hosea 4:6), priced above gold (Proverbs 3:13-15; 8:10-11; 16:16; Psalms 119:72), shown building the house (Proverbs 24:3-4) with the mechanism of a prosperous way stated (Joshua 1:8; Psalms 1:2-3). A lean season is a season, not a verdict (Ecclesiastes 3:1; Deuteronomy 8:3; Philippians 4:11-13; 1 Timothy 6:6-8; Proverbs 15:16-17). Joseph is called a prosperous man while a slave and a prisoner (Genesis 39:2, 39:23) before his season turns (Genesis 41:41); the widow's barrel and Job's latter end carry the long lean stretch. Money comes in a season with Him and He says where from (Deuteronomy 8:18; Proverbs 10:22; Matthew 6:33). The ladder of 2 Peter 1:5-8 is sustainable growth. Submission is the highest level (James 4:7; Proverbs 3:5-6; Isaiah 55:9; Deuteronomy 8:2, 8:5; Matthew 11:29), and its measure is the three in the fire: *"But if not"* (Daniel 3:17-18), with the fourth in the fire (Daniel 3:25) and the Lord who walked it first (Luke 22:42; Philippians 2:8).

**The honest line, gated.** Darrell marked the Philippians-and-the-tenth thought himself as *not studied yet*. The Word says of the Philippians *giving and receiving* (Philippians 4:15) and never names the tenth there; the tenth is written of Abraham to Melchizedek (Genesis 14:20; Hebrews 7:2, 7:4) and of the storehouse (Malachi 3:10). So every grown band says exactly that: a study to run, not a claim to make. And the Word promises supply of every need and fruit in season (Philippians 4:19; Psalms 1:3); it does not promise that every season is a rich one, and the lesson adds nothing to the text in either direction.
