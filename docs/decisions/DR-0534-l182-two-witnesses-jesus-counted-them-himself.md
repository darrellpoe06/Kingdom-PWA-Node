## What Darrell sent, and the correction that shaped it

He sent a debate between a GodLogic representative and a student of Gino Jennings on the Father and the Son — John 8:16-18 and the law of witnesses, and Hebrews 1:2 on whether the Son existed before creation or was "premeditated" in the mind of God. He asked for it Word-first.

The first draft drifted toward careful symmetry: two sincere readings, both quoting Scripture, reader left to weigh them. He stopped that:

> *"This is different because someone is using the Word to explain the Word... one is not... stay focused on the Word and those who agree are with Him...."*

He was right, and the drift would have been the violation rather than the safeguard. **DR-0098's own standard is that the Word explains the Word.** One reading lets John 8:17-18 mean what it says by the evidence rule Jesus Himself cited; the other brings a prior system and makes the text yield to it. Staging those as equivalent is precisely the ratings-style both-sides move the rule forbids. So the lesson **lands the answer** (DR-0100 — established truth stated, never hedged into "some say") while naming no teacher and no camp. The measure is agreement with the WORD, never with a man.

## The argument, and why it starts earlier than the debate did

Almost nobody brings **John 5:31** — *"If I bear witness of myself, my witness is not true."* With it, the case stops being an inference and becomes His own mouth:

1. He rules His own solo testimony insufficient (John 5:31) — submission to a standard, not doubt.
2. He cites the statute: *"the testimony of two men is true"* (John 8:17), quoting Deuteronomy 19:15, which **expressly refuses one witness**.
3. He counts: *"I am one that bear witness of myself, and the Father that sent me beareth witness of me."* (John 8:18)

Were these one Person in two modes, what He tendered is one witness testifying about Himself — the exact thing He had disallowed — and He would fail His own cited standard **in the act of citing it**. Scripture does not so contradict itself.

Pre-existence is settled the same way, from the text rather than from theory: the worlds were made **by** Him (Hebrews 1:2), He is **before all things** (Colossians 1:17), and the tense in His prayer decides it — *"the glory which I had with thee before the world was"* (John 17:5), followed by *"thou lovedst me before the foundation of the world"* (John 17:24). A purpose possesses no glory and is not loved.

## Three guards the lesson holds

1. **The opposite ditch, in every band.** Distinguishing Father and Son yields no second or third god. The test requires `The LORD our God is one LORD` (Deuteronomy 6:4) to appear in **all four bands**, so no reader can trade one error for a worse one. Distinction is never separation (John 14:9-10).
2. **No philosophy gets the referee's whistle.** Defining personhood as a rational agent with self-awareness, mind and will, then testing the Godhead against the definition, is the identical error the lesson corrects — entering from the other side. A definition men built is a tool, never the authority. The Word's own categories are relational: sent, beareth witness, loved, came forth from, go to.
3. **The charge of incoherence answered honestly.** Two things are braided in it: our categories really are too small (true), and our categories get a vote (false). Isaiah 55:8-9 is His own reply, and the objection assumes a premise the text never asserted — that distinct entails separate.
