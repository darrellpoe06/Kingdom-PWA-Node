## Why this course

Eleven courses covered the ground, the stewardship, the transaction, the tenant, the building, the partner, the lender, the record, the loss, the looking and the hardest hour. Every one of them runs on a **number**, and not one of them examines it: **what a thing is actually worth, and who says so.**

## The find, and it is the whole course

**Leviticus 27 is a complete valuation statute that almost nobody teaches**, and it carries every element a modern appraisal practice has plus two most of them lack:

- a **published schedule**, knowable in advance by everyone it touches (27:3-7)
- an **ability-to-pay override** that sets the schedule aside entirely — *"according to his ability that vowed shall the priest value him"* (27:8)
- a **disinterested valuer whose single figure BINDS** — *"as the priest shall estimate it, so shall it stand"* (27:14). No shopping for a second number.
- valuation by **productive capacity** rather than area: a field is priced by the seed it takes (27:16)
- a **pro-ration by remaining term** — *"according to the years that remain, even unto the year of the jubile, and it shall be abated"* (27:18). The value of land is the harvests you will actually get before it reverts.
- a **fixed fifth-part addition** on redemption, uniform across a house, a field and a tithe (27:15, 19, 31)
- an **expiry with no reversion** (27:20, 21) — and the destination named: it goes to the sanctuary, not back to the seller
- and the **unit defined in the same chapter** — *"twenty gerahs shall be the shekel"* (27:25)

## The arc, one argument in eight moves

1. **Thy estimation** — Leviticus 27:2-8
2. **Whether it be good or bad** — Leviticus 27:14, 32, 33
3. **According to the years that remain** — Leviticus 27:16-18, 23, 24
4. **He shall add the fifth part** — Leviticus 27:15, 19, 20, 21, 31
5. **Twenty gerahs shall be the shekel** — Leviticus 27:25; Ezekiel 45:9-12
6. **The balances of deceit** — Hosea 12:7
7. **The worth of it in money** — 1 Kings 21:1, 2, 15, 16
8. **What profit shall this birthright do to me** — Genesis 25:29-34; Jeremiah 32:25; Isaiah 55:1-2

Lessons one to five are five independent structural guards. Lesson six names in nine words what all five are guarding against, and it is not a mistake — **it is an appetite**. Lesson seven adds the question that comes before valuation entirely. Lesson eight turns the whole method onto the man performing it.

## The two sharpest things in it

**Ezekiel re-fixes the same unit centuries later, inside an indictment of princes.** Leviticus 27:25 says *twenty gerahs shall be the shekel*; Ezekiel 45:12 says *the shekel shall be twenty gerahs* — the same definition from the opposite end of the sentence. And the re-fixing arrives beside *"remove violence and spoil… take away your exactions from my people"* (45:9). That placement is the teaching: **a man need not steal visibly if the measure quietly shrinks.** Every printed price holds steady, every party can point at an unchanged document, everybody is robbed, and there is nothing to point at.

**Hosea 12:7 locates the deceit in the instrument rather than the mouth.** *"the balances of deceit are in his hand"* — he need utter no false sentence in a working day. That is why this course is built on structures rather than on character: a published schedule and a fixed unit catch what a good impression never will. And the last clause refuses every excuse a man offers — *he loveth to oppress* is an appetite, not a pressure, and the practical difference is that pressure can be relieved while an appetite must be repented of.

## The four limits, each pinned as a machine check

1. **Leviticus 27:2-7 is the money equivalent of a VOW, never what a person is worth.** Read verse 2 first or the schedule looks like a price list for human beings. **Verse 8 is the proof**: poverty sets the schedule aside entirely, and a measure of intrinsic worth could not fall because a pocket is empty. The figure was tracking the obligation. Nothing there is a statement about men, women, the young or the old.
2. **Leviticus 27:14 against 27:33.** The same chapter says *whether it be good or bad* twice with opposite force — the priest DOES estimate a house that way, and the tithe of the herd may NOT be searched that way. The difference is whose the thing already is: **what you TRANSACT you appraise carefully; what you OWE you do not shop.** Flattening them inverts one either way — apply 27:33 to a transaction and you refuse to examine what you buy; apply 27:14 to your giving and you sort your herd.
3. **1 Kings 21 — Ahab's offer was genuinely fair.** A better vineyard, or the money, with the choice given to Naboth, from a man who could have taken it. Say so before anything else, because **nothing here teaches that fair offers are wrong.** The failure is that *is it for sale* precedes *what is it worth* — and that a man who believes everything has a price concludes a failed sale is about the **seller**, who stops being a party and becomes an obstacle. Jezebel's own framing makes it explicit: *"which he refused to give thee for money"* (21:15).
4. **Genesis 25 — the text renders its own verdict** (*thus Esau despised his birthright*, 25:34), so the lesson invents none; and the passage is not a comment on Jacob's conduct here, so neither is the lesson. Esau's reasoning is taken **seriously** rather than mocked, because mocking it is how everybody escapes the lesson: his arithmetic was correct and his **input** was false. A valuation made in an extreme state prices the state, not the asset.

Additionally pinned: **the fifth part is taught by its EFFECT and no motive is put in Yahweh's mouth.** The arithmetic shows it removes the profit from leaning on a valuer; the text gives no reason, and we do not supply one.

## `premium` is a two-sense word now — and the cues were NOT widened

The plain-meaning gate fired eight times on `premium`, which entered the hard-terms list with the Insurance and Risk course carrying **insurance** cues (a regular payment made so a loss is shared). In this course it would mean a **markup over an appraised value** — a second sense, exactly the shape of the `liability` finding in DR-0526.

**The cues were not widened, and the reasoning matters more than the outcome.** Adding markup cues would give the term a *second way to pass*, which **weakens** the check rather than strengthening it — and weakening cues so a lesson passes is precisely what `plain-before-the-term.js` exists to prevent. The course was reworded instead: *charge*, *addition*, *the fifth part*. The word now appears **nowhere in the course at all** — not in a title, not in a benefit, not in the header comment — because `termFaults` only scans `lesson`, `bigIdea`, `inApp` and the bands, and keeping the word in the unscanned fields while avoiding it in the scanned ones would be **gaming the gate rather than obeying it**. The course test pins that over every field, and it is proven to catch: putting `premium` back into a single benefit fails exactly one test. The finding is recorded in the registry's own comment block so a future author reaches for the reword rather than the cue list.

## A textual claim pinned against the KJV

Lesson five claims Leviticus 27:25 and Ezekiel 45:12 set the same definition from opposite ends of the sentence. That is checkable, so **the test checks it against the repository's own KJV in all four directions** — each verse contains its own form and does *not* contain the other's — rather than asserting it in prose. This is the discipline DR-0527 adopted after its first draft overstated a repetition in Matthew 18, and it is now the standing pattern: **when a course makes a checkable claim about the text, the test checks it.** Proven to catch: rewriting the claim as *word for word the same* fails exactly one test.

Every obvious appraisal anchor was already mounted **in this same department**: Genesis 23 and Abraham weighing the silver (buying-terms); Jeremiah 32:6-15 and the sealed deed (property-principle); Proverbs 11:1 and Deuteronomy 25:13-16, the just weight (buying-terms); Ruth 4 (property-principle); Luke 14:28 and the count to finish (property-principle); 2 Samuel 24:24 and the refusal to offer what cost nothing (insurance-risk); Micah 6:11 (buying-terms); Haggai 1:6, the bag with holes (maintenance-trades).

**Eight natural lessons died there, and the course is better for it**, because the loss is what drove it into Leviticus 27 — which nobody teaches and which turned out to be the whole statute.

Measured against the mounted file, and pinned:

- **34 verses cited, ZERO shared with any other course in the Real Estate department.**
- **ZERO shared with the rest of the catalog either** — a stronger result than course eleven managed, stated because it was measured rather than aimed at.
- **TWO shared chapters, both property-principle, both at other verses:** 1 Kings 21 (here :1, :2, :15, :16 — the offer and what followed the refusal; theirs at :3, :4, :19) and Jeremiah 32 (here :25 alone; theirs at :10, :14, :15, :44).

**The Naboth narrative is shared on purpose and named rather than glossed over.** property-principle teaches the refusal and Ahab's sulk; lesson seven takes the offer and the aftermath. Same account, different verses, a different question — that course asks what an inheritance is, this one asks why a fair price did not settle it. Lesson seven deliberately **summarises** Naboth's answer rather than quoting it, **says out loud that it is summarising**, and sends the reader to the verse. That keeps the verse-level count at zero without hiding anything from the reader, and the test pins the flag as well as the count.

## The care note

This course's note refuses the one thing a reader will most want from it: **nothing here produces a number for your house.** What it gives is the SHAPE of an honest valuation — who makes it, what it is measured against, what the unit is, what a fair addition looks like — so a reader can read a real appraisal and tell whether it was done straight. A reader who believes something of theirs was valued wrongly starts at **lesson two and lesson five**, not lesson one, because those two are about who gets to make the estimate and what it is measured against.
