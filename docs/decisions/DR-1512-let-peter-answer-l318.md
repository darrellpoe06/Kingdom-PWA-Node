## Context

A video arguing against Peter as the first Pope. The obvious build is a rebuttal
lesson: their camp, our camp, pick one. DR-0098 forbids that — staging two sides
for the reader to choose between is the posture this platform exists to remove.

## What was measured

- **Not already built.** Matthew 16:18 appears in the catalog only as a passing
  reference in `yahweh-by-century.js`; the nearby hit was an unrelated lesson.
- **The video was never watched here.** youtu.be is unreachable from the sandbox;
  what was read is the summary the NAS wrote through the app's research door. The
  speaker's claims — timestamps, Petros/Petra, Augustine, the early church's
  silence — are recorded in `UNCHECKED` as **his**, `re-review: 2026-12-17`.
  **No movement rests on any of it.**
- **Peter answers the question about Peter, three times, in his own words.**
  Preaching: the stone the builders set at nought (Acts 4:11, quoting Psalms
  118:22). Writing: a chief corner stone, believers as lively stones on Him
  (1 Peter 2:4-7). To fellow leaders: who am also an elder (1 Peter 5:1). Never
  once does he call himself the foundation.
- **Both halves of Matthew 16 are read** — the confession revealed in verse 17 and
  the rebuke in verse 23. Quoting only the first half misuses it **both** ways.
- **113 quotations, every one filled from `app/public/bible/kjv`.** Not one
  verse typed by hand.

## Decision

1. **Teach, do not debate.** The dispute is named once and walked past by the
   Word; the speaker is never cited as authority.
2. **The spine is Peter's own testimony**, pinned as six spans the lesson cannot
   stand without.
3. **Matthew 16:23 is quoted from `thou art an offence` onward.** The KJV
   capitalises the adversary there; this house never capitalises him and never
   alters a quotation. The portion quoted is verbatim and contiguous, and the
   rebuke is named in our own voice, lowercase. **The shared adversary gate was
   NOT weakened to let this pass.**
4. **Spoken words are rendered, never quoted** (DR-0331) — the parables'
   dialogue, and the one word Darrell wrote.
