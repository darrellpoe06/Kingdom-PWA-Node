**Date:** 2026-10-20
**Status:** accepted
**Tier:** A (a record and a rubric; the first measurement rides the research road when the LEFT tower is up)
**Type:** decision

**Area:**

**Grounds:** Darrell, 2026-10-20, in the same hour Week 43 was being built: *"What is the difference between a research one institution and another one?"* and then *"We would like to investigate a research tiers based on rigorous processes... not based on the money but the quality... can or do we do this type of comparison?"* The answer given in the thread: the Carnegie line (R1 = doctoral, very high research activity) is drawn on research spending and doctorates awarded; nothing in the house compares institutions by the rigor of their process; we can.
**Principles:** DR-0076 (measure, don't claim; provenance on every number); DR-0100 (the three tiers: established fact stated plainly, the open named narrowly, over-reach corrected); DR-1101 and DR-1127 (the research road answers a build's question); DR-0075 (a why and a re-review date, never a silent stall).

## Context

A weight is judged by whether it is just: *"A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight."* (Proverbs 11:1, KJV). The common tier for universities weighs money and doctorate counts. Those are real numbers, honestly measured, and they measure capacity, not truthfulness. A student choosing where to be formed, and a family paying for it, want the second: does this place find out what is true and say so, and correct itself when it was wrong. That is a process, and processes leave records that can be counted.

## What was measured

Nothing yet, and this record says so (DR-0076 §8). The thresholds for the money tier quoted in the thread (fifty million dollars of research spending and seventy research doctorates a year for R1 under the 2025 revision; five million and twenty for R2) are from the agent's training, stated as established-but-unverified-here; the first act of the measurement is to fetch the current Carnegie methodology page and pin those figures or correct them.

What CAN be measured, per institution, from public records, each with its source named:

| weight | what is counted | where it is read from (to be verified on the first run) |
|---|---|---|
| pre-registration | share of the institution's recent empirical studies registered before data collection | registries such as the Open Science Framework and ClinicalTrials.gov, joined to author affiliations |
| open data and code | share of recent papers with data and code deposited and resolvable | repository records (Zenodo, OSF, Dryad, institutional repositories) and the papers' own data-availability statements |
| replication | share of the institution's findings that have been replicated, and the share of its own output that is replication work | replication registries and the literature itself, through the research road's search |
| retractions and corrections | retractions per thousand papers, and the time from concern raised to correction published | the Retraction Watch database and publisher correction notices |
| conflicts disclosed | share of papers with funding and conflict statements present and specific | the papers' own statements, machine-read |
| review in the open | share of output with open peer review or posted preprints with public review | publisher and preprint-server records |

Each weight is a share, so a small institution and a large one stand on the same scale; money appears nowhere in it. The composite is the plain average of the six, published WITH the six, never alone, so a reader can see which weight moved it.

## Impact

Unresolved, the house keeps answering "which institution is better" with somebody else's money-weighted number, which is the both-sides-by-default this platform exists to remove. Resolved, a family can read a rigor tier beside the money tier and see that they measure different things, and a student can ask an admissions office the six questions by name. The call obligates the research road to run the first measurement when the LEFT tower answers, and obligates this house to publish no composite without its six parts and their sources.

## Decision

1. The house builds a rigor-weighted research tier on the six weights above, as shares, averaged plainly, published only with its parts and sources.
2. Money and doctorate counts are not weights in it; they may be shown beside it, labeled as the capacity tier they are.
3. The first measurement is a build's question put to the research road (DR-1127) once the LEFT tower is up, on a small named set first (Illinois, where Darrell's university inbox lives, and a teaching-centered college for contrast), with every number carrying its source URL and fetch date; it ships as a session-note table and a gate that fails any row without a source.
4. ESTABLISHED: the Carnegie tier is money-weighted; the six process records exist and are public. OPEN, narrowly: the exact Carnegie thresholds until fetched; whether every registry exposes affiliation cleanly enough to compute shares without hand work. OVER-REACH, refused: a rigor score is not a verdict on any person at an institution, and a low share on one weight is a datum, not an accusation.

## Verification

- The first measured table exists in a session note with six columns, each number carrying a source and a fetch date, and the gate that fails a sourceless row is proven to catch on a row with its source removed.
- The thresholds quoted in the thread are confirmed or corrected from the fetched methodology page, and this record is amended by a new record if they were wrong (DR-0011: never a rewrite).
- `re-review: 2026-11-20` — if the tower is still dark by then, the measurement runs from the cloud sandbox's own fetch where the network allows, and this record says which rows came from where.
