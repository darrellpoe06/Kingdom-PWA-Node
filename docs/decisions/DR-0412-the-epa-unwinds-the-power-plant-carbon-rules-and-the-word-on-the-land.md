## The report

Darrell forwarded two items on 2026-09-15, each with the single word *"Lesson."*: the Morning Brew lead ("Smokin'") and the NPR Up First bullet on the same story. The newsletter claimed: the EPA, with the G20 energy summit in Houston as backdrop, is unwinding Obama- and Biden-era carbon standards for fossil-fuel power plants (coal plants to capture most emissions by 2039; gas plants to "burn cleaner"), effective shortly after publication; the EPA says the change saves the industry more than $300 billion and will "unleash" American energy; a separate proposed rule will argue greenhouse gases do not endanger health, which could block future administrations; an NYU analysis of 2022 data ranks the U.S. power sector as the world's sixth-largest emitter if it were a country; AP research says the regulations being wiped away could prevent ~30,000 deaths; the Sierra Club counts 330 coal plants retired since 2010 and 60 more announced by 2031; cheaper fossil fuels "coincide" with the AI data-center push straining grids and raising bills; legal challenges are expected.

The creator is a daily business newsletter compressing an AP story, an EPA release, an NYU brief, and a Sierra Club tally into a few sentences. The lesson treats it as ONE summary, sourced and labeled, and checks it line by line.

## DR-0100 tiers applied (verified by live web search 2026-09-15)

**Tier 1 — documented, stated plainly (with source and as-of):**
- On 2026-09-14 Administrator Zeldin signed the final *Partial Repeal of the Carbon Pollution Standards for Fossil Fuel-Fired EGUs*, announced at the G20 energy summit in Houston. It strikes the 2024 rule's guidelines for existing coal plants (90% capture by 2032 for plants running past 2039, or retire), the CCS standard for coal plants undertaking a large modification, and the Phase 2 CCS standard for new baseload gas turbines. The EPA projects **$310 billion** in avoided compliance costs — the agency's own one-pan figure, labeled as such. A supplemental proposal issued the same day would rescind every remaining power-plant GHG standard on a "do not contribute significantly" theory (final expected 2027). (EPA news release + program page; AP via MPR; Bloomberg via Insurance Journal.)
- The endangerment track: the 2009 finding was rescinded for motor vehicles on 2026-02-18 (effective 2026-04-20) and is before the D.C. Circuit on petitions from health groups and from two dozen states. (Federal Register; Utility Dive; Harvard EELP tracker.)
- **Established damage:** Henneman et al., *Science* (2023) — ~460,000 U.S. deaths 1999-2020 attributable to coal PM2.5, >43,000/yr in 1999-2007, falling as plants closed or scrubbed; coal PM2.5 carries ~2x the mortality risk of ordinary PM2.5. The 2024 MATS amendments were repealed 2026-02-20; the 2012 standards remain. (Science; NIH; EPA; CATF.)
- Sector scale: >1.5 billion tons CO2/yr; NYU Institute for Policy Integrity, 2022 data: sixth-largest emitter if a country, ahead of Canada, Japan, Brazil, Mexico. (Policy Integrity brief; NRDC.)
- Coal fleet: Sierra Club's 330 retired / 60 announced; DOE 202(c) orders in MI, CO, IN, WA; D.C. Circuit vacated the J.H. Campbell order 2026-09-11; AP: coal demand up ~10% last year. (AP; Earthjustice; Reason/Volokh.)
- Demand and prices: EIA record power use 2025-26; data centers 4.4% of U.S. electricity (2023) → 6.7-12% by 2028; residential prices +6.9% in 2025; Goldman +6% across 2026-27. (CNBC; Consumer Reports.)

**Tier 2 — genuinely open, flagged narrowly (never dismissed, never "no one knows"):**
- The AP **~30,000 deaths / ~$275 billion per year** is a reviewed, modeled projection across the WHOLE slate of ~30 rollbacks, not this rule alone: ~10,000 U.S. deaths/yr from soot, mercury, and lead rules (EPA's own RIAs) + ~25,000 heat deaths/yr worldwide from added carbon (Bressler formula, 1 death per 10,217 tons; Rhodium scenario). The lesson names the model and its inputs, keeps the U.S. share on firmer ground than the global share, and carries EDF's (>80,000 deaths, >$1T through 2047) and NYU's (~5,300 U.S. deaths from 2022 emissions) figures as their authors' projections.
- How much of the price rise is data centers versus fuel, grid, and weather is genuinely debated (Fortune). The demand and the price rise are Tier 1; the attribution is Tier 2.
- The newsletter's "coincide with the data-center push" is labeled **opinion** (framing of motive/timing); the administration says the demand is why it acted.
