## Where it sits

Six courses covered the ground, the stewardship, the transaction, the tenant, the building and the partner. This one covers the money that made every one of them possible, and the party standing on the other end of it.

## The arc, one argument in eight moves

1. **The head and the tail.** Moses sets two futures before one nation and the lending relationship appears in **both** — as blessing, *"thou shalt lend unto many nations, and thou shalt not borrow"* (28:12), and as loss, *"He shall lend to thee, and thou shalt not lend to him: he shall be the head, and thou shalt be the tail"* (28:44). The lesson's first move is to name what is **missing**: the word sin. What the text gives is a POSITION, and a position can be measured where a sin can only be hidden.
2. **When the money failed.** The fullest transfer-of-ownership account in Scripture, walked in its stages: *"when money failed in the land of Egypt"* (47:15), then the herds, then the people's own proposal — *"buy us and our land for bread, and we and our land will be servants unto Pharaoh"* (47:19). No fraud anywhere in the chapter, and a whole nation without its land or its labour.
3. **Suddenly.** *"Shall they not rise up suddenly that shall bite thee, and awake that shall vex thee"* (Habakkuk 2:7). The verbs, not the threat: both presuppose a party present the whole time and simply not moving. Made operational as the five dates that change a loan's state, against the one date most borrowers can name.
4. **Payeth not again.** The verse everyone reaches for, read correctly: the condemning predicate is not *borroweth*. And the righteous man opposite is not debt-free — *"he is ever merciful, and lendeth"* (37:26).
5. **What hast thou in the house.** A creditor at the door answered with an inventory question rather than a collection, a rebuke or a renegotiation. Her preparation set the ceiling — *"borrow not a few"* (4:3) — and the settlement has two halves: *"pay thy debt, and live thou and thy children of the rest"* (4:7).
6. **Usury, and who your brother is.** Both halves quoted together, because either alone is a false teaching. Comprehensive as to KIND, specific as to PERSON — and the damage happens at the kitchen table, not in international finance.
7. **Alas, for it was borrowed.** Four words that locate a man's conscience. A financed asset is partly another party's by construction, so the conscience is tested in deferred service and lapsed coverage, never where anyone is watching. And the harder half: he reported it at once and showed the place.
8. **He frankly forgave them both.** Jesus ends the subject of debt where no schedule can follow. Simon answers correctly and does not notice he has judged himself.

## The two rails this course had to hold at once

**It must not invent a prohibition.** Scripture nowhere forbids borrowing, and lesson 1 says so in the text. A household taught that debt is sin HIDES its position, and a hidden position cannot be worked — the pastoral cost is real and is named.

**And it must not flatter debt.** Habakkuk's *suddenly*, the staged surrender, and the four powers of the head are all in the same course. Both rails are pinned in the tutor posture, because a tutor that drops either one produces a different course.

**DR-0100, where it would have been easy to darken a text.** Genesis 47 ends with *"that Pharaoh should have the fifth part"* (47:26) — a fifth to the sovereign, four fifths retained — and with seed rather than another ration. That is a better ratio than a great many modern arrangements, and the lesson says so plainly rather than softening it to make the warning land harder.
