## Context

The door asks "Who are you?" and one of the six answers is "I own properties — Run your doors here, or bring your own portfolio." For the family that answer was always true. For anyone else it was a dead end: the card led to a sign-in, the sign-in reloaded into the app, and the app — reading no tenancy, no grant, no household and no door — showed the prospective-renter card ("sign in and your place will be here") to a person who had just said they own property. Nothing anywhere minted a landlord space for a stranger; DR-0365 made exactly one, for the family, by migration. The card's words promised a road that did not exist.

## What was measured

- `model.js` `WHO_OPTIONS`: `landlord` goes to `sign-in`, and `PropertiesDoor.jsx` rendered every account-holding portal with the same note ("tied to the email your landlord used to invite you").
- `PropertiesApp.jsx`: the role memo returned `owner` only when a rentals row could be read; the front-door gate (`!doors.length && !rentals.length && !grants.length`) sent everyone else to `PlacesToLive`. A landlord with a space and no door had no face and no board.
- `cloud.js` had no way to create a rentals row at all; every door in the system was made from the PoeTech Real Estate side or by migration. "Poe Properties by itself" would have handed a new landlord an empty board with nothing to do.
- `join_default_instance()` (0119) returns ANY existing non-church membership before minting a home, family-type first: a landlord-only person who opened PoeTech would have been handed their landlord space as "home". The full-app path therefore mints the family home itself, in 0119's `u-<uid>` shape, so the shell's ordering lands on the home.
- `instances.instance_type` allows `landlord` (schema-v2.1 §4.0.5); schema-v1's pre-rename twin `tenants_tenant_type_check` (six types) refused it until 0131 dropped it on the live database (DR-0273). The journeys' own database never replayed 0131 and refused the first local claim; 0287 drops the twin by name, idempotently, so any database that replays it can hold what it makes.
- The journeys' database cannot replay 0207 (its rentals carry no `entity_slug`), so the resolver the door reads did not exist there; 0287 declares `my_properties_instance_role()` again with the identical body and grants.
- 0056's `instances` policy fix was also missing from the journeys' and CI's door databases (the smoke's cross-tenant read hit "infinite recursion detected in policy"); both now apply 0056 first.
- The tier: a landlord-type instance with no `instance_subscriptions` row has `instance_active_tier()` NULL, and `rentals_tier_enforce` returns early — the same state DR-0365 chose for Poe Properties.

## Impact

- Unresolved: a prospective landlord cannot use the product. The card invites them, the sign-in works, and the app tells them to wait for an invitation from a landlord.
- The call obligates: a space the person makes for themselves must be theirs alone (slug derived from their id; owner membership; audited), must never be a second one for a person already seated, must never be minted on a seat read that merely failed, and must leave the family's instance, the tenant road and every delegate road untouched.
- Pricing is not decided here (see Decision 6); nothing in the app takes a card or moves money (DR-0094).

## Decision

1. **One RPC, by the person's own hand.** `claim_landlord_space(display_name_in, path_in)`: signed in; `path_in` in (`properties`, `poetech`); a landlord seat of any role already held comes back as `already` with that seat and makes nothing; otherwise a landlord-type instance `ll-<uid>` owned by the caller, audited (`create` / `instance`, the path in `to_value`). `poetech` also mints the family home `u-<uid>` (0119's shape) when the person has no family-type membership — on a first claim or a later one, so a properties-only landlord upgrades with one more tap. No subscription row.
2. **The intent rides the address.** The landlord card says what the sign-in leads to; tapping Sign in writes `?who=landlord` into the page's address before the form opens, and the reload after the sign-in reads it back. No storage: a re-opened link says the same thing on every device.
3. **The seat is read first; a failed read is said, never acted on.** `OwnSpace` reads `my_properties_instance_role()`: a seat of any role → the app, and the intent comes out of the address; no seat → the two paths; a read that failed → "Could not check your account (reason). Nothing was made." with Try again, and the chooser does not render (DR-0946).
4. **The owner's face is earned by the seat.** `PropertiesApp` reads the seat beside the four spine reads (not as one of them: no seat is the ordinary family case). An owner or admin seat with no grants and no household is the owner's face, and the front-door gate lets that person through to the board instead of the places-to-live card.
5. **The first door is made where the board is empty.** `AddDoor` (street, unit, what it is, city, state, monthly rent) is open on the empty board and behind Add a door on a full one; `addRental` files the row on the seat's space with the signed-in person as `created_by`, status `vacant`, the slug the address decides (`r-<address+unit>`, numbered when taken). The rest is edited in Edit, as every door is.
6. **Not in this decision.** A subscription/tier for a self-made landlord space — real money, a bright line (DR-0111 §1): `re-review: 2026-10-27`, with the measurement being the `audit_log` rows `note = 'claim_landlord_space'` (how many claimed, which path). The "bring your own portfolio" import the card's blurb names: `re-review: 2026-10-27`. The PoeTech shell's own "Who are you?" (a landlord arriving at poetech.us first) — the same RPC serves it; the surface is `re-review: 2026-10-20`.
