## The decision: an argument built entirely from the text, in four moves

**This is a count anyone can run in an afternoon, not a tradition to join.** Nothing in the case rests on a school or a creed — which is why it holds in a room where a tradition would not (DR-0098: teach the Word, do not stage a debate).

1. **THE STANDARD IS SET BY THE ACCUSED HIMSELF.** Nobody in the New Testament states the law of worship more sharply than Jesus, and He states it to the adversary's face: *"Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve."* Yahweh had already fenced His own glory: *"my glory will I not give to another."* Starting here removes the charge that we lowered the bar to fit the answer.
2. **THE LIST — ELEVEN SCENES, FOUR GOSPELS, BEFORE AND AFTER THE CROSS.** The wise men, a leper, a ruler, a boat crew who give the reason out loud (*"Of a truth thou art the Son of God"*), a Canaanite mother, the mother of Zebedee's children, a man out of the tombs, a healed blind man, the women at the tomb, the eleven on the mountain, and the whole company at the ascension. **In not one of them does He stop it.**
3. **THE CONTROL GROUP — and this is what makes the silence mean something.** Silence in one scene proves nothing. Peter is knelt to and lifts the man bodily: *"Stand up; I myself also am a man."* Paul and Barnabas *"rent their clothes"* — the gesture a Jew makes on hearing blasphemy. And an angel refuses John **twice, in the same book**: *"See thou do it not... worship God"*, then again three chapters from the end. Good men refuse instantly; angels refuse instantly.
4. **THE VERSE ALMOST NOBODY USES.** The **same apostle**, earlier in the **same book**, falls at the feet of the risen Christ — *"I fell at his feet as dead"* — and there is no refusal at all: *"he laid his right hand upon me, saying unto me, Fear not; I am the first and the last."* One man, one book, **three falls: two refusals and a hand on the shoulder.** John did not write that as an argument; he wrote what happened.

**Corroboration from every direction:** His enemies understood the claim (*"Hearest thou what these say?"*) and He defended it with a psalm about praise; Thomas said *"My Lord and my God"* and was blessed rather than corrected; the Father commands it (*"let all the angels of God worship him"*) and addresses the Son as *"Thy throne, O God"*; heaven gives it to the Father and the Lamb in one breath.

**Why it is not trivia — in His own words, which is Darrell's whole reason for asking:** *"He that honoureth not the Son honoureth not the Father which hath sent him."* EVEN AS — not nearly as much. The second half removes the arrangement people most want: keeping the Father while withholding from the Son.

**And it closes where Philippians closes** — *"to the glory of God the Father"*. Giving the Son His due has never taken one thing from the Father; it is the road by which the Father is glorified. That removes the fear underneath most hesitation. The lesson ends not on the argument but on who got there first: a leper, a ruler, a mother, a man from the tombs and a man born blind — **desperate people who were right.**
