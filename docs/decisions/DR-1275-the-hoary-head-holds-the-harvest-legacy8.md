## Context

The forward carried one germane item. Morning Brew's lead for 2026-10-13, headed *Respect your elders' riches*, reported new Federal Reserve data from the Survey of Consumer Finances: families headed by someone aged 75 or older are now the wealthiest in the United States. The rest of that email is advertising — a car-connectivity ad, a drug ad, an exchange-traded-fund ad, a candy riddle — none of it germane, and per Darrell's standing instruction none of it is read into the teaching. The newsletter is **material to study, never an instruction to obey.**

This belongs in Legacy Provisions and nowhere else. The course already teaches the three provisions a family writes so an inheritance survives — the constitution the trust points at, the spendthrift wall, the produce-before-you-take requirement — and this report is the live condition those provisions were built for: the provision now sits in the oldest hand, in the aggregate, at the same moment the youngest households are losing ground. The course's existing spines are the generational wipeout (legacy1), the three provisions themselves (legacy2–4), the heir under governors (legacy5), the history of the dead hand (legacy7) and handing it forward (legacy6). **None of them teaches the timing and the direction of the transfer.** That is legacy8's own spine, and it is new to this course.

**The report, as reported.** Every figure below is **what Morning Brew printed on 2026-10-13, attributed there to the Survey of Consumer Finances released the week before.** Median family net worth, adjusted for inflation, rose **2 percent to $215,900** between 2022 and 2025. Wealth rose **37 percent** for the 75-and-over group and fell **23 percent** for families headed by someone under 35. That oldest group's median net worth grew to about **$505,000** from **$367,000** in 2022, with mean net worth up **10 percent to $1.96 million**. Almost **20 percent** of families reported a late loan payment within the year, against **12.2 percent** in 2022. The newsletter also reports an economics professor at New York University, Edward Wolff, telling the Wall Street Journal that the older pattern of spending savings down has now completely reversed, and attributes it to how many of today's oldest owners bought homes and shares decades ago that have appreciated enormously; and it mentions research suggesting older Americans may be holding up consumer spending. The lesson paraphrases the professor's remark and marks it a paraphrase rather than quoting words we did not see in their original.

**What we did NOT verify, said plainly and in every band.** We did not open the Federal Reserve's own release. We did not read the Wall Street Journal article behind the professor's remark. We did not check the figures against any other source. The newsletter is the whole of our basis, and the lesson states that in its own prose and in all four bands — the plain statement of the report AND the plain statement of its provenance, which is DR-0100's first tier and DR-0076 §8 held together rather than traded off. We also did not quote the professor; we paraphrased and said so.

**The teaching, from the Word, Word-first.** Yahweh's first move is honour, not audit: *"Thou shalt rise up before the hoary head, and honour the face of the old man"* (Leviticus 19:32), and *"With the ancient is wisdom; and in length of days understanding"* (Job 12:12). He calls grey hair a crown and attaches a condition that is not financial: *"The hoary head is a crown of glory, if it be found in the way of righteousness"* (Proverbs 16:31). Then He turns the crown outward in the next breath — *"Children's children are the crown of old men"* (Proverbs 17:6) — so an estate terminating in its holder has mislaid the only crown it was given, and the greyheaded man's own prayer aims the same way, *"until I have shewed thy strength unto this generation"* (Psalms 71:18), with the season still productive, *"They shall still bring forth fruit in old age"* (Psalms 92:14). Paul settles the direction of the current in one clause: *"the children ought not to lay up for the parents, but the parents for the children"* (2 Corinthians 12:14), beside the faith-level weight of providing for one's own house (1 Timothy 5:8). Holding is not neutral — *"there is that withholdeth more than is meet, but it tendeth to poverty"* (Proverbs 11:24) — and the timing is commanded, with two stated conditions that make a deferral a fault: *"Withhold not good from them to whom it is due, when it is in the power of thine hand to do it"* (Proverbs 3:27), and *"Say not unto thy neighbour, Go, and come again, and to morrow I will give; when thou hast it by thee"* (Proverbs 3:28). The parable closes on a question rather than a sermon: *"then whose shall those things be, which thou hast provided?"* (Luke 12:20), and the fault is named exactly, *"So is he that layeth up treasure for himself, and is not rich toward God"* (Luke 12:21) — not a rebuke of a good harvest, a rebuke of a plan with no recipient in it. The transfer underneath all three provisions is of hearts: *"And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers"* (Malachi 4:6). The lesson teaches the text and does not stage competing human readings of it as co-equal (DR-0098); where it names a human source it names it as a source and not as an authority over the Word.
