## Context

The descent — what Jesus did between the cross and the resurrection — is one of the
few doctrinal questions where **both** available errors are common, and where the
platform's own standing rules point in opposite directions unless the position is
written down once.

DR-0098 says teach the Word rather than staging man's disagreement for the reader to
referee. DR-0100 says state established truth plainly and never hedge it into "no one
knows." DR-0076 says never fabricate and mark honest uncertainty. Applied naively to
this subject those three collide: one popular teaching supplies a detailed three-day
battle Scripture never narrates (fabrication), and the reflex correction is to retreat
into "we can't really know" (false skepticism about something the Word states
plainly). Neither is acceptable, and a lesson written without deciding this in advance
will drift into one of them.

L129 had already met the adjacent case and handled it correctly — it is commonly
preached that Jesus took the keys *back* from the adversary, and the Word says only
that He **has** them (Revelation 1:18) and that He destroyed him that had the power of
death (Hebrews 2:14); **it narrates no handover**, and L129's gate pins that. This DR
generalises that instinct into a position, and L130 is its worked example.

## Decision

**The descent is taught with both fences up, and neither may be removed.**

1. **The disarming is REAL and it is DATED AT THE CROSS.** Stated plainly, not hedged
   (DR-0100). *"It is finished"* (John 19:30) was said before He died; Colossians
   2:14-15 places the public triumph "in it" — the cross of the preceding verse;
   Hebrews 2:14 names the weapon as the dying itself, **through death**. The three days
   are possession being taken, not a contest being decided. He was never at risk
   (John 10:18; Acts 2:24).

2. **The adversary was NOT annihilated, and the proof is inside the Word.** Every
   letter cited was written by men who knew the tomb was empty and still describe him
   as active (1 Peter 5:8; Ephesians 6:11-12; Revelation 12:12). Hebrews 2:14's
   *destroy* is `katargeō` (G2673) — render inoperative — and 1 John 3:8 names the
   object as the **works**. His execution is future and fixed (Revelation 20:10).

3. **The three dates are kept apart** — verdict (John 12:31; John 16:11), binding
   (Revelation 20:2-3), fire (Revelation 20:10). Collapsing them is what makes the
   subject look self-contradictory; separating them dissolves the contradiction
   without anyone having to win an argument.

4. **The `hadēs` / `geenna` distinction is TAUGHT, and taught from the Word rather
   than asserted.** The KJV renders both with the one English word *hell*, and
   Revelation 20:14 — *"And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire"* — is
   Scripture proving the two are distinct. Most of the difficulty in this subject is a
   translation matter, and naming it is teaching, not taking a side.

5. **The hours Scripture does not narrate stay unwritten, and the silence is said out
   loud.** No dialogue, no struggle, no ransom paid to the adversary, no timeline.
   Deuteronomy 29:29 is the fence and it is quoted in the lesson body. A vivid
   retelling is an **addition**, and every addition of that kind moves the adversary
   toward the center of a book about the Son.

6. **Proportion is a doctrinal requirement here, not a stylistic one.** Four Gospels
   on the Lamb, one verse on the adversary's end. Any teaching that spends more time
   on a fight in the dark than on the blood has inverted the book it claims to open.
   This is the teaching-side sibling of the Color Theology reservation (DR-0099): the
   Blood keeps the center.

**On naming the popular teaching (DR-0098 boundary).** The lesson may NAME the
three-day-fight teaching in order to educate past it **by the Word** — pointing at
`"It is finished"`, at Colossians 2:15's date, and at the four verbs Scripture
actually gives. It may not stage it as a co-equal view for the reader to choose
between, and it does not name or weigh any teacher. That is the DR-0098 line, applied.

## Consequences

- **`ll130-…`** ships as the worked example, with `LIVING_LESSONS_META.weeks` 128 → 129.
- **`living-lessons-l130-verses.test.js`** enforces the position rather than trusting
  the prose: assertions exist for the cross-dating, for the not-annihilated half, for
  `katargeō` precision, for the `hadēs`/`geenna` teaching, for the three dates, for the
  silence fence (including a negative pattern that fails on any invented fight
  narrative creeping in later), and for the DR-0100 both-directions commitment.
- **Verbatim discipline held at 173 quoted spans**, with an intentionally EMPTY
  `NOT_SCRIPTURE` allowlist — every double-quoted span in the lesson is KJV.
- **Two real defects caught before shipping**, and both are now proven-to-catch:
  a quotation **welded Colossians 2:14 and 2:15 across the verse boundary** (both
  halves genuine, the concatenation not), and the **child level under-quoted
  Revelation 1:18** by dropping *"of hell and"* — under-claiming inside a paraphrase,
  which is the DR-0100 failure at the youngest age band.

## Honest limits

- The position is bounded to what the cited texts state. Questions the Word does not
  settle — the composition of the "spirits in prison" of 1 Peter 3:19, the mechanics
  of Matthew 27:52-53 — are left where Scripture leaves them, and the lesson says so.
- **Not claimed:** the lesson has not yet been read on the live build by a steward
  (DR-0104). That pass remains the outstanding proof.
