## The report

Darrell, 2026-09-15, forwarded the Morning Brew newsletter ("Smokin’") with the single word **"Lesson."** — build input. Its bullet "Not all job markets are equal" summarizes a Burning Glass Institute analysis reported by the Wall Street Journal: workers 22–34 **without** college degrees are in one of the best job markets in nearly two decades, with one of the lowest unemployment rates since 2003, driven by a drop in immigration and retiring blue-collar workers; unemployment is **rising** for bachelor's-degree holders amid AI replacing entry-level white-collar roles; this is unusual (the two groups tracked each other in the pandemic and after 2008); and, big picture, degree holders 25–54 still averaged 2.7% unemployment for the year ending July versus 4.7% for high-school-only, with the more hands-on and in-person jobs showing the lowest unemployment relative to their own history. A second bullet in the same edition — the 10-year Treasury yield briefly topping 5% on 2026-09-14 — is carried only as context on the cost of borrowing.

## The Word settles what the debate cannot — work itself

**Word first:** work preceded the fall — "to dress it and to keep it" (Genesis 2:15); the sweat came after (Genesis 3:19). The Word honors the craftsman by name (Exodus 31:2-5) and binds the gift to teaching (Exodus 35:34-35). The Lord Himself worked with His hands (Mark 6:3; Matthew 13:55); the apostle who studied under Gamaliel made tents and said so (Acts 22:3; 18:3; 20:34). The believer is commanded "to work with your own hands" as a witness (1 Thessalonians 4:11-12); "if any would not work, neither should he eat" (2 Thessalonians 3:10-12); "In all labour there is profit" (Proverbs 14:23); and the diligent hand "shall stand before kings" (Proverbs 22:29). The laborer is paid, on time — "worthy of his hire" (Luke 10:7; 1 Timothy 5:18), before sundown (Leviticus 19:13; Deuteronomy 24:15) — and withheld wages cry to the Lord of sabaoth (James 5:4); the employer in Stage 3 is bound by that side as tightly as the worker by the other. The cost is counted before the loan (Luke 14:28; Proverbs 24:27 — the field before the house; Romans 13:8; Proverbs 22:7). The fruit is judged by the Word's own measures of work — paid on time? cost counted? craft honored and taught? — with the ant and the sluggard (Proverbs 6:6-11), and every work brought into judgment (Ecclesiastes 12:14). Anchor: Genesis 2:15; Proverbs 22:29 — "the market will move again; the Word on work will not."

**Stewardship:** a church that apprentices its own — COLG has tradesmen in its pews and PoeTech's own founder is a building-controls technician who learned a craft with his hands — pairing every capable teenager with a journeyman, walking each household through a real union wage sheet and a real college cost sheet side by side (ties to Issue 11), and holding both to the Word's test. Grace note condemns no soul: not the struggling graduate, the parent who steered toward a loan in good faith, the young man who chose a trade, nor the employer short of hands.
