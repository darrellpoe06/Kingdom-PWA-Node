## Context

Darrell photographed two pages of a chart and asked for timelines of the Bible as lessons, Word first. The chart is a writing-order list: Job first, marked Unknown and Anonymous; Nehemiah the last Old Testament book at about 424 to 400 BC; James the first New Testament book at AD 44 to 49; Revelation last at AD 94 to 96. It marks its own uncertainty with Unknown, Anonymous, Multiple Authors, Solomon primarily, and a question mark after Samuel (Ruth) and Ezra (Chronicles).

**The honest frame, set before any date.** The chart is one published chart's estimate. It was **not** compared against any other published chart, and the lesson says so in its own text and in `inApp`. What the lesson claims as fact is what the Word itself says about its writers and its dates, and the lesson's job is to show which of the chart's lines the Word underwrites.

**This lesson's danger is a number remembered wrong.** A lesson can quote every verse verbatim and still misquote its own source by saying James was written AD 45 to 50 from memory. So the chart is kept as **data** (`app/src/lib/progress-of-revelation.js`, transcribed from the photographs line for line, the chart's own blanks kept exactly) and the gate pins every date the lesson speaks to that data, derived and never typed twice.

## What was measured

**The phrase is the Word's own.** The chart's heading is the opening of Hebrews: *"God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son"* (Hebrews 1:1-2). Sundry times is the progress; divers manners the forms; one speaker holds fifteen centuries of writing together as one Book (2 Peter 1:21; 2 Timothy 3:16).

**The writing was commanded, not collected**, from the first order to the last: Exodus 17:14, Exodus 24:4, Numbers 33:2, Deuteronomy 31:9 and 31:26, Isaiah 30:8, Jeremiah 36:2, Habakkuk 2:2, Revelation 1:11 and 1:19.

**The Word dates itself, often to the day** (Haggai 1:1; Zechariah 1:1; Ezekiel 1:2-3; Jeremiah 1:2-3; Isaiah 1:1; Hosea 1:1; Amos 1:1; Luke 3:1). So the chart's *approximate* belongs to converting the second year of Darius into our calendar with records from outside the text, never to the Word being vague. The order is Scripture's own; the year numbers are a translation of Scripture's dates into ours.

**Two rules, worked from the text, settle most of the chart.** *A book is no older than its last event*: Kings ends with Jehoiachin's release in the thirty-seventh year (2 Kings 25:27) and the chart says 561 to 538 BC; Chronicles ends with Cyrus (2 Chronicles 36:22) and Ezra opens with the same sentence (Ezra 1:1), which is the chart's Ezra with a question mark; Ruth ends with David (Ruth 4:22); Proverbs dates one of its own sections under Hezekiah (Proverbs 25:1), the one verse behind the chart's 971 to 686 BC and *Solomon primarily*; the Psalms run from Moses to the rivers of Babylon (Psalms 137:1; 72:20). *A book is no younger than its first reader*: Daniel reads Jeremiah by books (Daniel 9:2; Jeremiah 25:11; 29:10); Jesus names the book of Psalms (Luke 20:42), the threefold Old Testament (Luke 24:44) and its first and last martyrs in the Hebrew order (Luke 11:51; 2 Chronicles 24:21); Peter shelves Paul's letters with *"the other scriptures"* (2 Peter 3:16) while Paul lived.

**The chart's blanks are the Word's silences, and the lesson keeps every one** (Deuteronomy 29:29): Job and Hebrews Unknown; Samuel, Kings, Esther Anonymous; Ruth and Chronicles queried. The uncertainty is narrow, a name and never a book, and the gate refuses any sentence that fills the Hebrews blank with Paul.

**The twenty years without a Gospel** are taught from Hebrews 2:3-4, Acts 17:11 and the Spirit's promise in John 14:26 and 16:13, as the sundry times inside one generation rather than a weakness to explain away. The close is what did not move in fifteen centuries: Psalms 119:89, 119:160, Isaiah 40:8, Psalms 12:6-7, 1 Peter 1:25, Matthew 24:35, Hebrews 11:3, Colossians 1:17, Hebrews 4:12, Isaiah 34:16.
