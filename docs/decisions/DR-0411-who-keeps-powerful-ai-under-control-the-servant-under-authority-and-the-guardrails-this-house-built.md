## The report

Darrell forwarded **The Neuron** of 2026-09-15 (theneurondaily.com), *"Microsoft: Maybe we still put humans first??"*, with the single word **"Lesson."** The newsletter's frame: the whole industry keeps asking *who should keep powerful A.I. under control?*, and on Monday 2026-09-14 Microsoft and the President landed on opposite answers. It reports that Microsoft AI published a draft Code of Conduct for its future MAI models (stop when a human pauses/redirects/cancels/shuts down; stay inside authorized tools, data, permissions, and task scope; reject legal personhood; never claim feelings, consciousness, or motivations; give up autonomy or capability if meaningful human control requires; sub-agents inherit the rules; when the whole thing is told to stop, all of it stops) — a roadmap, it notes, not a description of today's models. It reports (citing AP) that the President said a strong, smart, high-IQ president is the only guardrail A.I. needs, opposed enhanced oversight, emphasized staying ahead of China, and phoned NVIDIA's chief executive live at the All-In podcast taping. It separates "guardrails" into two layers — inside the model (access/do/hide/refuse) and outside it (what governments or companies put on the builders) — carries a former OpenAI research VP (alignment is an unsolved algorithm problem) against an NVIDIA researcher (methods exist; generalization to unseen situations is the question), steelmans the former A.I. czar's position (a lab may slow itself; liability law applies; transparency and audits are sound; a government-wide slowdown while China races is the objection) and an investor's definition of "pacing" (keep improving models, shift compute toward testing, monitoring, alignment), and closes with four questions no internal rulebook can answer for itself (prove the rules hold in unfamiliar situations; keep testing as capabilities change; know who is responsible when they fail; decide what accountability exists outside the company) and the line: *"A guardrail that only works when everyone is behaving carefully isn't much of a guardrail."*

## The Word settles what the debate cannot

The anchor is the centurion's servant — "I say to this man, Go, and he goeth; and to another, Come, and he cometh; and to my servant, Do this, and he doeth it." (Matthew 8:9) — and the servant's posture, "We are unprofitable servants: we have done that which was our duty to do." (Luke 17:10). Microsoft's rulebook rediscovers that servant line by line (scope = the garden; minimum permissions = the talents; no personhood = the idol that cannot answer; stop = the prophet's spirit subject to the prophet). The newsletter's real question — whether the controls survive contact with capability, convenience, competition, and speed — is the Word's two layers: self-rule inside the servant and a watchman outside it who must give account. Neither a strong man nor a sincere lab is the wall; counsel, a spoken boundary, and an account rendered are. The fruit: a learner leaves able to judge any tool by four verbs (goes, comes, does, stops), to give every automation a budget, a lock, a stop, and a watchman, and to open the OpsBoard and see the house's own brakes — the servant under authority, built into a machine, under a Master who is not one.
