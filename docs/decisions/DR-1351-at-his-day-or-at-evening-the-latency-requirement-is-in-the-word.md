## The teaching

**Yahweh wrote a real-time requirement into His own law, and He set the clock by the worth of the result to the person waiting** — *"At his day thou shalt give him his hire, neither shall the sun go down upon it; for he is poor, and setteth his heart upon it"* (Deuteronomy 24:15), and again with the hour named (Leviticus 19:13). **And the same Word runs a batch window with no apology** — the vineyard settled at evening in one pass (Matthew 20:8), lawful because **the window closes inside the deadline**. So the standard is never the mechanism; the standard is when the result must be useful to the one waiting. Choosing between them is an engineering question. Being late is a sin question.

Then the manna as a system with no stored state and one announced window (Exodus 16:19–24); Joseph's accumulation against a boundary where waiting is free (Genesis 41:35, 41:49); the watchman, where Yahweh prices lateness at **blood** (Ezekiel 33:6) and then gives the mechanics without sentiment — a fast first arrival carrying nothing (2 Samuel 18:29) against a later one carrying the event (18:31), a watch kept day and night answering one trumpet (Nehemiah 4:9, 4:20); backpressure named and remedied with capacity plus routing (Exodus 18:18, 21, 22); a message delivered twice while the thing it established happened once (Genesis 41:32); and the deepest case, where the text sets a loop that never completes against one offering after which **He sat down** (Hebrews 10:11 / 10:12 / 10:14) — Jesus, the Lamb of Yahweh and the Eternal Son of Yahweh. The close is the correction: **Yahweh never calls speed a sin, only haste** (Proverbs 18:13; 19:2; 21:5).

## Trust but verify — and what the checking CHANGED

**The checking made the article's claim STRONGER, and DR-0100 requires saying so.** The article writes the strongest guarantee as *"each effect to appear once"* — **effect**, not delivery. That is correct and careful, and the common version in circulation over-promises it as exactly-once **delivery**, which no system can give over a road that drops and repeats. The lesson states the article's framing plainly and names the over-claim it avoided. Also **measured by us rather than repeated**: its arithmetic holds — 9:01 AM against a midnight run is 14 h 59 m, "almost 15 hours" exactly as written.

**ATTRIBUTED:** the particular tools named per tier, and the claim that many systems settle for at-least-once plus idempotent processing. **UNCHECKED, a blocked road that never reads as verified (DR-0076 §8):** the maintainers' own documentation could not be read. Three roads walked, all refused, quoted not described — `getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND kafka.apache.org` (and `flink.apache.org`, `nightlies.apache.org`); `GitHub access to this repository is not enabled for this session` for the docs source in `apache/kafka`, with `add_repo` requiring an approval no human was present to give; and a web search returning **only third-party blogs**, which DR-0190 §1 forbids as a citation and which were not used. ## The systemic finding — his second instruction, answered on our own files

**Two of our three tiers are on the right mechanism.** Streaming: 42 `.channel()` subscriptions across `app/src` (chat, messages, arrivals, harvest ledger) where worth collapses in seconds. Batch: `ci.yml` fires on a push, grinds the suite in one pass and exits, and the 39 scheduled daily sweeps lose nothing by running at a fixed hour.

**The mismatch is the watchman, and it is the one that matters most.** `site-health.yml:63` asks for `*/10 * * * *` = **144 looks a day**; DR-1301 measured it **delivered about 5**, with the longest gap between two consecutive scheduled runs at **616 minutes — 10 h 16 m**. A monitoring requirement wearing a batch mechanism, on the result whose value collapses fastest, and DR-0107 (*a down site is the worst outcome*) rests on that instrument. It is the whole fleet: on 2026-11-02 five watch lanes asking for 144, 96, 48, 24 and 4 looks all delivered three or fewer. **And our stated policy is the inverse of our fleet:** DR-0255 says watch event-driven first with a timer only as a short fallback, while **39** lanes carry `schedule:` against **3** using `repository_dispatch`. All 6 `infra/nas-loops/registry.json` entries are polling timers; none is event-driven.

**Where we already do the article's remedy correctly, and it is worth naming:** the lesson-intake lane is at-least-once delivery with **idempotent processing keyed on an id** — the article's payment-ID dedupe, built here as `one-thread-one-record-guard.mjs` keyed on the Gmail thread id and `lesson-row-shipped-sweep.mjs` exiting 1 on an already-shipped row. A thread can be forwarded twice; the work happens once. That is Genesis 41:32 in our own tooling.

**The fix is already in our ledger and is this article's answer from the other direction:** DR-1301 says not to ask for more runs — the wrong move against a per-repository ceiling — but to make every verdict carry the elapsed time since the previous look and refuse to read *fresh* across too long a gap. Unknown freshness never reads as fresh. **No change is made to that lane here**, because it is a Tier B witness mid-soak and not this lesson's blast radius; DR-1301 holds it with `re-review: 2026-11-09`. **What I could not answer:** whether the legacy n8n webhook routes are genuinely event-driven in production or wrapped in a poll — that runs on the NAS, beyond this sandbox's road. `re-review: 2026-11-19`, via the tailnet runner (DR-1159).
