The subject: five archaeological witnesses to the endurance of the name — the Soleb inscription, the Moabite Stone, the Ketef Hinnom silver amulets, the Dead Sea Scrolls, and the modern Hebrew Bible — with the speaker's conclusion that the forgotten gods of the ancient world are the contrast, and that the name is finally carried in Jesus.

## What was measured

### The DR-0192 pass: his claims, verified independently

Every checkable external claim was verified against sources outside the agent's priors before a sentence was written. Five corrections and two honest limits came out of it, and all seven are **taught in the lesson rather than quietly fixed**:

| his claim | verdict | what the lesson says |
|---|---|---|
| Soleb inscription, "end of the 15th century BC" | **corrected.** Amenhotep III's reign is conventionally placed c. 1390–1350 BC — the **fourteenth** century | every band corrects the dating and says what the source said |
| Soleb names "the land of the nomads of Yahweh", settled | **genuinely contested, narrowly (tier 2).** Temple, topographic list, entry and four signs are established. Whether `yhwꜣ` there names the One or a locality, and whether those Shasu are Israelites, is argued by Egyptologists; the Egyptian also writes a final sign that is not the final Hebrew letter, so it is not a letter-for-letter transcription | named as argued, in every band, with the diagnostic applied out loud: strike it off and nothing in the lesson falls over |
| Moabite Stone is **granite** | **corrected.** It is **basalt** | "black basalt" in every band |
| Moabite Stone: 9th century, found 1868 at Dhiban by the missionary Frederick Augustus Klein, Louvre since 1873, names Moab, chemosh, Omri, Israel and the four letters | **established (tier 1)** | taught plainly, and taught as **hostile testimony**, which is why it is the strongest of the five |
| Ketef Hinnom: two silver scrolls, Barkay, priestly blessing, 7th century | **established, with the dispute named.** 1979 excavation; read reliably only after high-resolution photography in 1994; new critical edition 2004 (Barkay, Lundberg, Vaughn and Zuckerman, *BASOR* 334). Excavators: end of the 7th or beginning of the 6th century BC. **One scholar (Renz) argued for a Hellenistic date**; the late-monarchic reading is standard | the dates, the 1994/2004 steps, and the dispute stated narrowly in every band |
| Dead Sea Scrolls preserve the name "in the square script" | **corrected, and the correction is better news.** Qumran scribes used several treatments deliberately: the square script; the older **paleo-Hebrew letters inlaid** into an otherwise square-script manuscript (1QpHab, 11Q5); and in some manuscripts **four dots** (tetrapuncta) in the name's place | taught in every band: nobody applies that apparatus to an ordinary word |
| Modern Hebrew Bible shows Jews "still actively use" the name | **corrected, and the correction strengthens it.** The **written** name is preserved and read; it is **not pronounced** — a substitute is read aloud out of reverence. Standard editions (BHS, BHQ) are set from the **Leningrad Codex**, colophon 1008/1009 AD, the oldest complete manuscript; the Aleppo Codex is older and incomplete | both corrections taught; the reticence is presented as itself corroborating |
| (not in the source) the sound of the name | **honest limit, stated rather than left.** Consonants certain; **vowels not transmitted** — the pointing on the four letters belongs to the substitute word. "Yahweh" is a reconstruction from early Greek transcriptions and from the short form inside Hebrew theophoric names: widely held, **not a recording** | stated in every band, with the reason it costs nothing: the house rule was never about the sound |
| "Yeshua means Yahweh saves" | **established as philology, and not leaned on.** The Hebrew form is the shortened divine name joined to the root meaning to save | taught, with the point that Matthew 1:21 supplies the sense in its own sentence without needing the etymology |
| "the Father is Yahweh, the Son is Yahweh, and the Holy Spirit is Yahweh"; "the Messiah is Yahweh who has come to save" | **DR-0098 handling.** The lesson confesses what is written — the Lamb of Yahweh (John 1:29), the Eternal Son (Hebrews 1:8), "The LORD our God is one LORD" (Deuteronomy 6:4) — and declines both to repeat a formula the text does not use and to stage the question as a debate | said in as many words in every band |

**The one thing in the source that became the lesson's spine.** He states on camera that he will *substitute the name into the verses as he reads them*, and he does. That is precisely the line DR-0076 draws and DR-0210 restates: the name belongs in **our** sentences and **never** inside a quotation. The lesson teaches the distinction by doing it — the printed `LORD` appears over and over in the quotations while Yahweh appears only in our own prose — and the gate asserts both halves.

## The decision

**ll259 — *His Name Kept in Stone and Silver — Five Witnesses to the Name That Endured*** joins Living Lessons, in all four bands, as the **evidential** half of the house rule on the name.

Placement is Living Lessons rather than a course of its own because the doctrinal half already lives there (ll155) and because this is the family-facing, every-age series: the subject is objects a child can picture, and the lesson's own method is His — stones set out so that a child would point and ask (Joshua 4:6).

Eleven movements, in order: where it came from, plainly · He named Himself and attached a tenure clause · what a witness can do and what it cannot · Soleb, and the one that is honestly argued · an enemy cut it in stone · silver, worn at the throat · the scrolls in the caves · the book on the shelf this week, and the limit stated · the names that were not kept, and the reason the Word gives · the name that saves · why this house says the name, and never inside a quote · talk about it together.

Two stories that teach rather than decorate:
- **The Name in the Back-Step Concrete** (light; Joshua 4:7): a name pressed into wet concrete in 1961 outlives the siding, the roof, the oak, the well and four mailboxes, and gets itself said out loud by a boy who was not alive when it was written.
- **The Hall Where Nobody Prays** (solemn; Psalms 9:6): a grandson reads the museum cards aloud — chemosh, marduk, asshur, baal, amun — and asks where the people are who prayed to them; on the steps afterwards his grandmother says the name of Yahweh out loud and he understands what he is doing.

## Impact

A reader meets, in their own band, the thing the house asserts and the ground that was asked about it — and meets the discipline at the same time. A child learns that a name set into something hard keeps handing itself back, and learns to look for the capital letter that stays inside a Bible quotation and vanishes in our own sentence. A youth gets a test to use for life: ask what would fall down if one piece of proof were withdrawn, and notice who is leaning. A teen gets hostile testimony as a category and the three-way separation of established, inferred and argued. A senior gets the pastoral cost named: a confidence built on the latest excavation is unsettled by the next retraction, and the eldership of all people should be the ones who notice a verse being improved to make a point land harder.

And DR-0210 gains its receipts. The rule was never phonetic and never antiquarian: *other people call other gods, and I want to be clear.* Five witnesses say the name was there to be clear with.

### Limits, named rather than left

- **Soleb is not evidence this lesson relies on.** The reading is genuinely disputed and the lesson says so in every band. If it is withdrawn, four witnesses remain and no sentence changes — which is stated in the lesson so a reader can hold us to it.
- **The vocalisation is a reconstruction.** Consonants certain, vowels not transmitted. "Yahweh" is widely held among those who study it and is not a recording. Nothing in the house rule depends on the sound.
- **The Ketef Hinnom date is the excavators' and is not unanimous.** Renz argued Hellenistic; Rogerson and Davies hold that palaeography alone cannot settle such a date. The lesson gives the standard late-monarchic reading and names the dissent narrowly rather than burying it or inflating it.
- **The speaker is identified by inference from the book he names on camera.** The transcript does not name him. If that identification is wrong, the correction is one line and nothing else in the lesson moves — and nothing is asserted about him beyond the book's catalogue entry.
- **Nothing is cited to a minute mark.** The research route joins the caption segments and discards timestamps, so the lesson attributes to the speaker and never to a timecode.
- **The source is a machine transcription.** His wording is not quoted anywhere in the lesson, in either direction; the lesson engages his *claims*.
- **Three of the five witnesses were verified through secondary literature, not primary publication.** The *BASOR* 334 article, the Soleb excavation report and the Qumran scribal studies were reached through scholarly summaries and abstracts rather than the articles themselves. The citations are given so a reader with library access can go further, and the lesson's claims are pitched at what those summaries actually support.
- **No band teaches how to read Egyptian or Moabite.** A reader cannot check the inscriptions personally, and the lesson says the limit out loud rather than implying a competence nobody in the room has. `re-review: 2026-11-10` — read L259 on the live app in each band and record whether the Soleb honesty reads as strength or as hedging to the first reader who says either.

## Proven to catch

2. **The bright line, as a machine check.** "I am the LORD: that is my name" is changed to "I am Yahweh: that is my name" — the exact substitution the source performs on camera — and the check must name `Isaiah 42:8`. This is the one break that matters most: it proves the gate catches our own house convention reaching into a verse.
3. **A band quietly shortened.** The teen band is truncated to 600 characters and `shortBands` must name `teen`.

5. **The duplicate guard, on its own terms.** The lesson is given ll155's title opening and `duplicatePairsIn` must return exactly one pair, so the real pass is a property rather than an accident.
6. **The generic-name check.** "Then God was glad." is appended to the child band and the check must fire, while the unmodified band reads clean.
