Darrell: *"Real-estate first then keep closing the gap!!!!!!!"* Ten courses of the department were mounted. This is course eleven.

## Why this course

Ten courses covered the ground, the stewardship, the transaction, the tenant, the building, the partner, the lender, the record, the loss and the looking. This one covers the hardest hour any of them can produce: **the day somebody in your building cannot pay, and you are the one holding the power.**

The subject attracts abuse from both directions, which is why it needed a course rather than a lesson. One direction reads Scripture as a licence to put a family into the street because the debt is real. The other reads it as a claim that no lawful remedy exists at all. **Scripture does neither.** It never cancels the debt anywhere in this material — the pledge is still taken in Deuteronomy 24:10, the walled-city house is genuinely and permanently lost in Leviticus 25:30, and the lord in Matthew 18 held a lawful claim. What Scripture does, over and over, is restrain the **METHOD** and the **MEMORY** of the one holding the power.

## The arc, one argument in eight moves

1. **They turn the needy out of the way** — Job 24:2-4, 7, 9, 12
2. **Cast out from their pleasant houses** — Micah 2:9-10
3. **Thou shalt stand abroad** — Deuteronomy 24:10-13
4. **He taketh a man's life to pledge** — Deuteronomy 24:6, 17, 18
5. **The year to redeem** — Leviticus 25:29-33
6. **Took him by the throat** — Matthew 18:23-35
7. **Job's own oath** — Job 31:13-15, 16, 19, 21, 22, 32, 38, 39
8. **Restored to the debtor his pledge** — Ezekiel 18:7, 16; Amos 2:6-8

Look straight at it rather than at the paperwork → hear Yahweh bring it as a charge in the second person → get the restraint He placed on the creditor's method while leaving the claim standing → learn what may never be seized at all → find a redemption window that is real and bounded at **both** ends → stand in front of the parable that indicts a forgiven man who would not extend what he had received → put yourself under the oath before you put anybody out → and close on the man Ezekiel calls righteous, who is known partly by what he **gave back**.

## The two hinges

**Deuteronomy 24:10-11 is the hinge of the course.** Yahweh closed the threshold to the creditor and told him where to stand instead: *"Thou shalt stand abroad, and the man to whom thou dost lend shall bring out the pledge abroad unto thee."* The debt survives. The pledge is still owed and still handed over. What the lender loses is the right to cross the door and to **choose the item himself** — and the borrower's right of selection **is** the protection, because a man choosing his own pledge shields what his household cannot function without, where a creditor walking the rooms takes whatever is worth most. Then the duration restraint for a poor borrower (24:12-13) very nearly destroys the pledge as security, on purpose: **Yahweh did not abolish the debt, He stripped the leverage out of it** — and attached a verdict to the method, *"it shall be righteousness unto thee before the LORD thy God."*

**Amos 2:8 closes the course where it opened.** Lesson one has people *caused to lodge without clothing, that they have no covering in the cold* (Job 24:7). Lesson eight has men *laying themselves down upon clothes laid to pledge by every altar* — the very garment lesson three said must go back at sundown, underneath a worshipper, in the place where worship is conducted. The covering owed to a cold man, and who is lying on it.

## The four limits this course holds, each pinned as a machine check

1. **Job 24 is JOB SPEAKING inside the dispute,** answering friends who insisted his suffering proved his guilt. It is not a pronouncement from the whirlwind. The description of dispossession is exact and the Word preserved it, which is why it opens the course — but the closing clause of 24:12, *"yet God layeth not folly to them,"* is his complaint from inside the dark, the whole book is the answer to it, and it **must never be taught as doctrine about what Yahweh charges.** Lifting it out and teaching it that way takes a wounded man's cry and puts it in Yahweh's mouth.
2. **Deuteronomy 24:10-13 restrains METHOD, never the claim** — and the limit is held in **both** directions. Teaching it as a general prohibition on collecting reverses the text; reading a real debt as permission to walk in ignores verse 10 entirely.
3. **Matthew 18:23-35 is forgiveness between servants of the King, not a landlord-tenant statute.** The lord's claim was lawful. The hundred pence was small only by comparison and was genuinely owed — the parable does not work by making the second claim fraudulent. The servant is never rebuked for asking. And it ends with him **delivered to the tormentors**, an ending nobody preaching a general amnesty out of this text can account for. Jesus names the subject Himself in 18:35.
4. **Leviticus 25 states its OWN limit, and anyone stopping at verse 29 misreads it.** A walled-city house not redeemed within the full year *"shall be established for ever to him that bought it throughout his generations: it shall not go out in the jubile"* (25:30). A final, irreversible transfer of a dwelling, **inside the jubilee legislation, by the same hand that wrote the reset.** A great deal of loose teaching about biblical economics dies on that verse, and the course says so plainly.

## A claim of fact that had to be corrected, and is now pinned against the KJV

The course teaches that the fellowservant's plea in Matthew 18:29 is the servant's own plea from 18:26. **The first draft said the two were word-for-word identical. They are not** — 18:26 begins *"Lord,"* and 18:29 does not. The course gate caught it as a non-verbatim quotation, and the fix was to correct the claim to the measurement rather than trim the quote to fit the claim: the **seven words are identical**, with that single difference, because the servant was addressing his king.

The corrected claim is now checked **against the repository's own KJV** rather than against our prose — both halves, including that 18:29 does *not* contain *"Lord,"* — and the test also asserts the overstatement is absent. **Proven to catch:** restoring *word for word, the same words* to the lesson body fails exactly one test. The correction is carried on the tutor posture too, or the tutor would restate the overstatement to a learner.

## The care note, which matters more here than anywhere else in the department

This is the one course where the wrong move is **both a sin and, in most places, a crime**. The note names the self-help acts outright rather than leaving them to inference — changing the locks, shutting off heat or water, removing a door, putting belongings on the pavement — says they are lawless before Yahweh whatever is owed, and sends every learner to somebody licensed in their own state. And it routes the other side of the table: **a reader who is the one behind and frightened starts at lesson three and lesson eight**, not at lesson one, because those two are about what may not be done to them and about what an honest person on the other side is supposed to give back.
