## The arc, one argument in eight moves

1. **Abraham refused the gift and paid in public.** Offered the field for nothing, he answered *"I will give thee money for the field; take it of me"* (Genesis 23:13), weighed the silver in the hearing of the assembly, and the title was made sure *"before all that went in at the gate of his city"* (Genesis 23:18). Price, payment, description, record — all four elements of a modern close, in the first land purchase in Scripture. And the lesson names why the gift was the **worse** deal: an unpriced transfer leaves the giver an unwritten claim and the receiver an obligation he cannot discharge.
2. **Two sets of weights in one bag.** The commandment forbids **possession** — *"Thou shalt not have in thy bag divers weights, a great and a small"* (Deuteronomy 25:13) — because each transaction made with a false weight is defensible alone; the fraud is only visible in the pair. And the verdict lands on the **instrument**: *"A false balance is abomination to the LORD"* (Proverbs 11:1). Made measurable: the cap rate, condition allowance, comparables, vacancy and fee you use when buying, written beside the ones you use when selling.
3. **It is naught, it is naught, saith the buyer** (Proverbs 20:14) — and the boast afterwards convicts him. The lesson draws the line rather than banning negotiation: a **defect is a fact** and may be pressed hard; a **verdict on value the speaker does not hold** is a lever made of a lie.
4. **Surety: the trap you talk yourself into.** The snare closes at agreement, not at default — *"thou art taken with the words of thy mouth"* (Proverbs 6:2) — and the remedy's register is an animal escaping a hunter.
5. **Haste is the most expensive thing in the deal.** Proverbs 21:5 sets diligence against **haste**, not laziness, and 28:22 explains why 28:20 calls it a loss of innocence: he *"considereth not that poverty shall come upon him"*. Haste has a precise shape here — **closing before the truth arrives**.
6. **What borrowing makes you, and what it does not.** Both edges, and stop where Scripture stops (DR-0098): it is never called sin, and it is never flattered. Deuteronomy 15:6 names it as the **ruled** position; Romans 13:8 keeps one debt on purpose. Made measurable: months of full vacancy the reserves survive.
7. **Give a portion to seven, and also to eight** (Ecclesiastes 11:2) — and the reason is **epistemic**, not optimising: *"for thou knowest not what evil shall be upon the earth."* The test is survival, not a percentage: name the one event that would take everything.
8. **He that sweareth to his own hurt, and changeth not** (Psalms 15:4). The hurt is stipulated. And the real failure mode is named: **attrition** — the price that becomes a change order, the repair that slides, the date that drifts — each delay defensible alone. Two honourable options, no third; what the psalm removes is **silence**.

**The recurring shape, now named across three courses:** a defect invisible from inside any single instance. The false weight, the lawful process that took Naboth's vineyard, and attrition on a promise. The department teaches the same eye three times on three different objects.
