## Context

Darrell sent a video asking where the Garden of Eden was, and asked that maps, timelines, connections to the Word, and **games** come with the lesson. The video argues that Eden was the whole land of Mesopotamia, that the garden lay in a valley now beneath the Persian Gulf, and that its eastern door was roughly the Strait of Hormuz. This is the **third and last** of the three research-road rows that had been waiting while the NAS builder was dark-in-effect; `60972a31` became sov52 (DR-1258) and `d56b9e85` became ll268 (DR-1259).

**The danger here is the opposite of ll268's, and that is the whole design problem.** ll268 handled a sensational claim that flattered the account and had to be kept out of the evidence pile. This source is sober, close to the text, honest about its weak points, and ends by pointing the viewer to Christ. So the risk is not credulity but agreement: that the lesson would simply nod along, repeat a synthesis as though it were a finding, and never reach the verse that resets the question.

## What was measured

**The distinction the subject turns on is in plain sight, and the video is right that readers miss it.** *"And the LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden; and there he put the man whom he had formed"* (Genesis 2:8). Eden is a region; the garden is planted inside it, toward its east. Then *"And a river went out of Eden to water the garden; and from thence it was parted, and became into four heads"* (Genesis 2:10) — and whether that water runs outward or inward is the single question dividing every theory from every other.

**The river list's unevenness is itself evidence.** Three rivers get a land or landmark; the Euphrates gets nothing (Genesis 2:11-14), because whoever first received the account lived beside it. And the Hiddekel is located by *Assyria* — a kingdom from a far later world than the garden, which is how a kept record names an old river by a marker its own generation knows.

**Two of the four are gone, and Scripture repairs one real confusion by itself.** Hiddekel is the Tigris and Euphrates the Euphrates; Pison and Gihon carry no modern name. The King James gives *Ethiopia* at Genesis 2:13 following the Greek, but the Hebrew is Cush — and *"And Cush begat Nimrod"* (Genesis 10:8), whose kingdom began at *"Babel, and Erech, and Accad, and Calneh, in the land of Shinar"* (Genesis 10:10), which is southern Mesopotamia. The Word supplies an eastern Cush out of its own pages. That is a genuine gain, and it is not a riverbed.

**Scripture uses Eden in at least five senses**, which is the vocabulary a class needs before any geography: a region with a garden in it (Genesis 2:8); a jewelled height (Ezekiel 28:13, with the same onyx Genesis set in Havilah); a place of mountain cedars (Ezekiel 31:8-9); an ordinary later kingdom with merchants, a sceptre and a conqueror (Ezekiel 27:23; Amos 1:5; 2 Kings 19:12; Isaiah 37:12 — the Bit Adini of Assyrian records); and a simile for any green country (Genesis 13:10; Isaiah 51:3; Ezekiel 36:35; Joel 2:3). A search assuming all five are one place has equivocated before examining any evidence.

**Three theories, each given its case and then its failure.** The highlands fit a river going out and Ezekiel's cedars, but their four candidate rivers do not come from one source and two drain toward the Caspian. The drowned Gulf valley fits four rivers meeting — the basin genuinely was dry when sea level stood far lower, a genuine buried channel crosses Arabia — but it requires reading Genesis 2:10 backwards. The upper Euphrates fits the name, the sanctuary and the family memory, but shows no place where one river becomes four. Each explains a part; none explains the whole.

**Three tiers (DR-0100).** Established: the dry basin and the buried channel; Bit Adini as a real kingdom Scripture calls the house of Eden; a real complex of early carved enclosures near Harran, older than Sumer's cities; the earliest domesticated wheat traced to that country; and *paradise* arriving through a Persian word for a walled park. Asserted: that any of those places IS the garden — a dry valley is not a garden, a kingdom that borrowed the name is not the garden, an early sanctuary beside early farming is not the garden; the speaker concedes this, and his verdict is **a synthesis he offers, not a finding anyone has made.** Corrected by the Word: one claim, below, and it concerns Scripture rather than geography.

**The one correction, and it matters more than the map.** The video's opening move is that Moses inherited Genesis as a family record rather than receiving it directly, and that Genesis *"doesn't really read that way"* otherwise. Half of that is a gift: the seams are genuinely there — *"These are the generations of the heavens and of the earth"* (Genesis 2:4), and *"This is the book of the generations of Adam"* (Genesis 5:1); Scripture calls it a book, and says plainly that Moses wrote (Exodus 24:4; Numbers 33:2). What Scripture declines to do is make a mechanism load-bearing. On how the Word came it says one thing of all of it: *"holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost"* (2 Peter 1:21), and *"All scripture is given by inspiration of God"* (2 Timothy 3:16). A kept family record and the moving of the Holy Spirit are not rivals and nobody is asked to choose. **A reader can lose more from one confident sentence about HOW the Word came than from twenty uncertain sentences about where a river ran.**

**And the sentence every theory walks past, which is why this lesson exists.** *"So he drove out the man; and he placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life"* (Genesis 3:24). The garden is not called lost. It is called **kept** — a door on the east, a guard upon it, posted not to hide an address but to keep the way of the tree of life. Beside it, *"to till the ground from whence he was taken"* (Genesis 3:23): the man is outside, the road back is held. So every expedition hunts the exterior of a place Scripture declares guarded on the interior, and **finding the valley would not open the gate.** The Word's own question is therefore not where but whether the way is open — answered at Luke 23:43 (*paradise*, the very word the Greek Old Testament used of the garden), Hebrews 10:19-20 (a new and living way through the veil), and Revelation 2:7, 22:2, 22:14 (the guarded tree promised back, in an entered city). A guarded garden at the front of the Book and an open city at the back, with the sword at the east answered by a veil torn down the middle. And the searching itself is blessed rather than forbidden: *"It is the glory of God to conceal a thing: but the honour of kings is to search out a matter"* (Proverbs 25:2), bounded by Deuteronomy 29:29 and tempered by 1 Corinthians 13:12.
