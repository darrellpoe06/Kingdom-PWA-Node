## Context

DR-0712 marks who spoke in a recorded class, on the NAS: sherpa-onnx finds the voices, TitaNet gives each one a vector, and a voice is named only when it matches an enrolled voiceprint (one voice to one print, cosine at least 0.55). Two prints exist, BG and DP, both made from words Darrell attributed in the L202 recording (`enroll.json`). Every other voice stays S1, S2.

Nobody else could be named, because the only ways to make a print were a person at the NAS (`name_voice.py --name`) or words someone else attributed. A voice is personal. Naming it needs the person's own yes, and a way to take that yes back.

**Reality trace.** Real data: the person's own `voice_enrollments` row (written by three database functions and by the NAS's service role), the sample in their own folder of the private `lesson-audio` bucket, and the voiceprint file on the NAS. End to end: the app writes the consent and the sample; the lesson-voice cycle already running every 15 minutes on the NAS reads them outbound, writes the print, and writes the result back to the row; the transcriber reads the row again before it names anyone. The surface: My profile, which opens from the face in the header on every app and from the Engagement tab, with a link on the lesson recorder and on Your lessons, where a person sees S1 and S2. Premise checked: since REPOINT-ARMED the app's database and storage are the NAS's own Supabase (DR-0614), so the sample's waiting room is on the NAS too.

## Impact

Anyone signed in can add their own voice from My profile, and every class recorded after that shows their words under their letters (for example `JM:`) with their name in the header, the same way BG and DP are shown. Anyone who has not added a voice is still S1, S2. A person can remove their voice at any time; from that moment the transcriber no longer names them, and the NAS deletes the print within one cycle.

Darrell can re-enroll as DP with a clean sample of his own. That print replaces the one made from L202. If he later removes it, DP is marked withdrawn, so `enroll.json` never re-creates it from the old recording.

Without this, a class could name only two people, and anyone else would need someone to stand at the NAS and decide whose voice is whose. That is the guessing DR-0712 forbids.

## Decision

1. **Consent first, recorded.** "Add my voice" shows what is kept (a voice signature, a list of numbers, not the recording), where (only on our own server at home, the NAS, never a cloud company), what it is for (to put their name beside their words in class recordings), and that it can be removed at any time. The person taps "I agree". `give_voice_consent()` stores those exact words and the time in their own row.
2. **The sample rides the lessons' road.** The person reads Psalm 23 (KJV, verbatim from the copy on disk) on the same recorder the spoken lessons use. `sendVoiceSample()` asks the database for the consent row before uploading anything. The sample goes to the person's own folder of `lesson-audio`, and `send_voice_sample()` files it, refusing without consent or for a path outside that folder.
3. **One print per person, on the NAS only.** Each cycle, `voice_enroll.py` downloads the sample, deletes the waiting copy, measures it, and keeps one voiceprint `<LABEL>.json` that carries the owner's id and chosen name. It refuses with a plain reason when the sample is too short, has more than one voice, or matches someone else's print. The sample audio is deleted unless the person ticked "keep my recording". The result ("Your voice is added", or why not) is written to their row and shown in the app.
4. **Remove my voice.** `remove_my_voice()` deletes the consent row immediately and the app says so. Each cycle starts with a reconcile that deletes every self-added print whose consent row is gone, plus any kept sample. Removal is never stopped by a stop-path. A failed read of the table removes no one.
5. **Naming.** The transcriber reads `voice_enrollments` live before it names voices. A print a person added names a voice only while their row says enrolled under that label. The only prints with no consent row behind them are the word-attributed BG and DP (DR-0712). If the read fails, only those two are used. By hand, `name_voice.py --name` now makes only BG or DP.
6. **Labels.** Each person has two or three letters, unique across people. BG is held for Bishop Gwin's word-attributed print. DP can be taken only by the Governor's own sign-in.
7. **Data walls.** RLS is on. A person reads only their own row. No client can insert, update or delete the table directly; every write goes through the three functions above. The Governor sees the label list (letters, name, state) through `voice_enrollment_labels()`. No vector is ever stored in the database, so none can be shown.
8. **Armed by record, with brakes (DR-0247, DR-0248).** The pass runs in the lesson-voice cycle that already runs. Budget: `timeout 150` and 3 samples per pass, at low priority. Lock: `voice-enroll.lock`. Stop-path: `LESSON_VOICE_SELF_ENROLL=0` (or `LESSON_VOICE_SPEAKERS=0`) in `lesson-voice.env`; it stops new enrollments, never removals. While the speaker models are not installed, a sample waits and the row says so.
