## Context

On 2026-09-29 Darrell forwarded from dpoe@illinois.edu to his Gmail (thread `1a0edc48d113da3c`, subject "Fw: How to find the Goldilocks zone of curiosity") a Big Think newsletter article by Anne-Laure Le Cunff, with "Lesson" on top, his own summary, and a project manager's note:

> "Project Manager perspective: This maps well to stakeholder engagement and change management. People are most engaged when they understand enough about a project to see what they need to learn next, while also recognizing why the outcome matters to them. Large, vague initiatives often create disengagement; targeted questions and visible next steps create curiosity and participation."

Under DR-0312 a Lesson-marked email is build input. The article was read in full as material to study, not as instructions. Placement: Living Lessons (a devotional and teaching lesson for every age, not a tech or health lesson), number L198.

## What was measured

- **The article's claims, attributed to it:** curiosity follows an inverted U against confidence (a trivia study); when information feels important, curiosity stays high even at very low confidence (a replication with importance added); the three-puzzle illustration; Mendeleev's gaps; four applications (learning, creative work, communication, career); two levers (the size of the gap, the importance of closing it).
- **What we checked, and how far.** The article links its studies through redirects without naming them; its host (Substack) and PubMed Central are blocked by this sandbox's egress, so the studies were identified by web search of their published summaries: the trivia study matches Kang et al., *Psychological Science* 20(8), 2009; the importance study matches Spitzer, Janz, Nie and Kiesel, "On the interplay of curiosity, confidence, and importance in knowing information", *Psychological Research* 88(1), 2023 (DOI 10.1007/s00426-023-01841-9), whose summary reports the middle peak for unimportant items and high curiosity from very low to moderate confidence for important ones. Neither paper was re-read in full; the lesson says so and teaches their findings as the article reports them, a pattern averaged over many people in a trivia setting, not a law for every heart (DR-0100 tier 2, named narrowly).
- **Established history, stated plainly (DR-0100 tier 1):** Mendeleev's 1871 table left spaces and described eka-aluminium, eka-boron and eka-silicon; gallium (1875), scandium (1879) and germanium (1886) were found with properties close to his predictions.
- **Verses:** 256 quoted spans across every surface of the lesson, each resolved against the verse it names in `app/public/bible/kjv` by `scanQuotedVerses`: 256 verbatim, 0 faults. Every double-quoted span carries its reference; no man is quoted in quotation marks.
- **Bands (prose only, measured by the repo's gates):** adult 2,120 prose words, grade 4.8. Child 1,156 words (share 0.55, floor 0.5), grade 1.8 (ceiling 5.0). Youth 1,333 (0.63), grade 3.6. Teen 1,307 (0.62), grade 6.6. Senior 1,362 (0.64), grade 8.5. Ascending, not inverted. Worst band overlap 0.02 (ceiling 0.5). Every band names its lesson in its opening.

## Impact

Without this, a teaching Darrell sent through the door the platform promised to open would sit unread in an inbox (DR-0312), and a reader meeting the article alone would get a psychology tip with no Word under it. With it, every age band reads the Word's own account of the same shape: Yahweh conceals so that kings will search (Proverbs 25:2), shows it line upon line (Isaiah 28:10), taught by questions and parables (John 1:38-39; Luke 24:17-32), built the question into Israel's homes (Joshua 4:6-7), and rewards the seeker who knows little but knows it matters (Jeremiah 29:13). Darrell's project manager note is answered from the Word by Nehemiah (Nehemiah 2:13, 17-18; 4:6). The reader also sees an honest line drawn between what an article claims, what was checked and how far, and what is settled fact.

## Decision

L198, **The Goldilocks Zone of Curiosity — What He Hides, the Gap He Shows, and the Question That Matters**, joins Living Lessons on 2026-09-29. Word first, in eleven numbered movements, macro and micro:

1. He hides a thing so that kings will search (Proverbs 25:2; Deuteronomy 29:29), with the King James "curious" read as the King James uses it (Exodus 28:8; Psalms 139:15; Acts 19:19).
2. He shows it in part, line upon line (Isaiah 28:10; Mark 4:33; John 16:12; 1 Corinthians 3:2; Hebrews 5:14; 1 Corinthians 13:12).
3. The man who says we see (John 9:41; 1 Corinthians 8:2; Proverbs 26:12; Revelation 3:17; Job 26:14).
4. How Jesus opened a gap (Luke 2:46-47; John 1:38-39; Matthew 16:15; 13:11, 34; Mark 4:34; Luke 24:17, 27, 32).
5. He built the question into the house (Exodus 12:26-27; Joshua 4:6-7; Deuteronomy 6:7; Proverbs 25:11).
6. One plain question (Acts 8:30-31, 34-35; John 3:9; Acts 17:11; James 1:5; Proverbs 16:9).
7. When it matters, you seek with little (Acts 16:30-31; Luke 19:3-4; Mark 5:28; Jeremiah 29:13; Proverbs 2:4-5; Matthew 13:44; Hebrews 11:6).
8. The gaps in the pattern: Mendeleev as history, and the prophets searching a gap whose missing piece was a Person (Psalms 111:2; 1 Peter 1:10-12; Luke 24:44-45).
9. Curiosity with no destination, and where the search stops in this life (Acts 17:21, 27; 2 Timothy 3:7; Ecclesiastes 1:8; 3:11; Romans 11:33).
10. Nehemiah showed them the gap and the why, answering Darrell's note (Nehemiah 2:13, 17-18; 4:6; Luke 14:28; Habakkuk 2:2).
11. The treasure has a name (Colossians 2:3; Matthew 7:7; Jeremiah 33:3; Psalms 119:18; 2 Peter 3:18).

Four authored bands (child, youth, teen, senior), twelve quiz questions, fifteen facilitator points, thirteen benefits. Where the Word is reticent (the work He does "from the beginning to the end", Ecclesiastes 3:11), the lesson says so and adds nothing. The article's framing that curiosity "can be engineered" is attributed to it; the lesson teaches the same shape as Yahweh's design without staging the two as rival camps (DR-0098).
