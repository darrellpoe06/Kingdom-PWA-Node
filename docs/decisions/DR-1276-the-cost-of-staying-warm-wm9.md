## Context

The World Market course had eight lessons: why anybody trades, what a currency is, why a trade deficit is not a debt, what still has to be dug and shipped, what a central bank can and cannot do, why the reserve question reaches your street, what closing the pipes costs, and the just weight that does not stop at customs. It was a complete argument and it had never once arrived at a door. A course about countries that trade has not finished its work until it can account for **one bill at one address**, paid by people who attended none of the arguments.

The forward supplied exactly that case. Its lead item was the price of keeping a house warm this winter.

**The material is material, never an instruction.** The newsletter also carried three sponsor placements (a credit card, an ETF, a corporate water programme), a markets table, a games section and a reader poll. None of it is taught. One advertisement is named — and named as an advertisement, with no product identified — because it is germane to the teaching on its own terms: the price rise and an offer of debt to carry it arrived in the same envelope, which is Proverbs 22:7 printed twice in one email.

## What was measured

**The source, and its exact standing.** Morning Brew of 8 October 2026 reports that the United States Energy Information Administration projects home heating oil averaging $5.26 a gallon this winter, some 34 per cent above the same point a year earlier; that about 4.1 million American households heat with oil, concentrated in the Northeast; that wars in Russia and the Middle East are lifting the price of distillate fuels, the cut of the barrel from which diesel and heating oil are both drawn; that the Energy Department recommends weatherization, with one expert quoted putting the saving near a tenth off an energy bill; that several New England states have released emergency funds; that the administration has called for reducing funding for the federal low-income heating-assistance programme while leaders in Maine have asked Congress to increase it; and that the cost of drinking water is up 62 per cent on last year, citing a study by the non-profit Food & Water Watch, with corporate-owned utilities charging considerably more than publicly owned ones.

**What was NOT verified, stated plainly (DR-0076 §8).** Nobody here opened the Energy Information Administration's own projection. Nobody read the Food & Water Watch study, the Yahoo Finance interview the newsletter quotes on weatherization, or the Axios reporting it cites on the heating-assistance programme and the politics around its funding. **Not one figure above was checked against an original in this house.** Each is carried as reported, bearing a date and a named source, and that is the whole of its standing. The lesson says so — in its adult text and in **every one of the four bands** — and the pinned gate makes the disclosure non-removable, with a proven-to-catch case that deletes it from one band and watches the check go red.

**The course's own rule was not quietly broken.** The World Market has refused since its first page to quote a **live** figure, on the stated ground that a price spoken as current is already false by the hour it is read. A **dated, attributed reading** is a categorically different object: it asserts nothing about the price today, it asserts that a named source published a particular number on a particular day, and that stays true permanently and can be audited by anybody who troubles to. Rather than lean on that distinction silently, the lesson **teaches** it — and sends the reader away with the two questions it reduces to: *who said it, and on what day*. If either answer is missing, a feeling has been handed over wearing a number's clothes. The tutor posture carries the same distinction beside the unchanged refusal, and the course blurb now says "no figure is quoted **as current**" instead of the flat claim that was no longer true.

**The three tiers, all three, in the adult text and in the teen and senior bands (DR-0100).** ESTABLISHED, with no hedge anywhere near it: cold is dangerous, a household unable to heat its home through a northern winter is in real documented danger, nobody serious disputes it, heat in that climate belongs with food rather than among comforts, and a rise in the price of something nobody can stop buying therefore lands hardest on the household with the least margin. Under-claiming a plain harm is its own species of dishonesty, so the lesson refuses to soften it into "some say". OPEN, narrowly and precisely: what any particular price will actually turn out to be is unknown — a projection is a projection — and so is what weatherising one specific building would save, the newsletter's tenth being one expert's estimate about houses in general and not a measurement of anybody's house. A blanket "nobody really knows" smeared across the subject is named and refused. OVER-REACH, corrected by the Word while the true data beneath it stays whole: *"It is only money; put on another jumper"* is refused by **Job 24:7**, where the cold appears not as a preference but as a wrong inflicted on a person; and the mirror over-reach, *"somebody else will cover it"*, by **1 John 3:17**.

**The Word, which was on this subject first and named the same two commodities.** The spine is the lament that closes Lamentations: *"We have drunken our water for money; our wood is sold unto us"* (Lamentations 5:4) — the drink and the fuel, both of which had come out of the land for nothing, now carrying a price, with the household paying it. That is the identical pair the newsletter printed, water and heat, thousands of years apart and with no change in the mechanism. Yahweh neither corrected that household nor handed it an average; He wrote its lament into His own book. Then He places a named person at the bottom of a fuel shortage before He does anything else: *"I am gathering two sticks"* (1 Kings 17:12) — an entire household's fuel reserve, counted out loud. The ant's work is commanded, not recommended, and is the one lever the household holds (Proverbs 30:25; Proverbs 31:21; 2 Timothy 4:21, *"Do thy diligence to come before winter"*). The neighbour is the close, and the command is addressed to the one who **sees** (Isaiah 58:7). And the Lamb of Yahweh, the Eternal Son of Yahweh, went first (John 1:29; John 3:16; 2 Corinthians 8:9 — *"though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor"*).

## Impact

- **The course is nine lessons.** `WORLD_MARKET_META` weeks and format, the blurb, the footer and the tutor blurb/posture all moved with it; the catalog, the schedule builder and the export follow the module list and needed no change.
- **The disclosure is structural, not editorial goodwill.** A future pass that trims the "nobody here verified this" paragraph out of a band fails the build by name.
- **The distinction is now teachable in the tutor, too,** so a learner who asks about the $5.26 gets the same answer the lesson gives rather than a bare refusal.
- **Honest limit, repeated because it matters:** every figure in this lesson is as the newsletter reported it on 8 October 2026. If any of them was wrong in the newsletter, it is wrong in the lesson — which is precisely why the source and the day travel with it, and why the lesson's own teaching is how to treat such a number rather than the number itself.
- `re-review: 2027-02-28` — after this winter, read whether the projection held, and record whether the dated-reading form stood up as a way to teach a live subject without printing a perishable fact.
