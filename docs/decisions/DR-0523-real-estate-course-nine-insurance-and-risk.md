## Why this course, and what it covers that none of the other eight do

Every one of the first eight courses assumes the thing this one handles. The ground, the stewardship, the transaction, the tenant, the building, the partner, the lender and the record all presume that nothing catastrophic happens. This course covers what happens when it does: **who bears the cost, and how a household decides in advance what it cannot afford to lose.**

## The find this course is built on, and it was a surprise

**Exodus 21 draws the actuarial distinction itself, in two adjacent verses.** Where neither owner knew of the danger, the loss is DIVIDED between them — *"then they shall sell the live ox, and divide the money of it; and the dead ox also they shall divide"* (21:35). Where the owner KNEW and did not act, he pays in full and alone — *"he shall surely pay ox for ox; and the dead shall be his own"* (21:36).

Loss-pooling for the genuinely unforeseeable; full liability for the known hazard. That is the moral architecture every insurance contract on earth is built on, and the exclusions page nobody reads is verse 36. It is in Exodus.

## The four limits this course holds, and why each one is load-bearing

Each is pinned as a machine check in `app/src/__tests__/insurance-risk-course.test.js`, because a limit that lives only in prose is a limit a future edit deletes quietly.

1. **Luke 13 — BOTH sentences.** Jesus denies twice that the victims were worse sinners, and in the same breath asserts *"except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish."* A lesson that quotes verse 4 and drops verse 5 flatters the reader; one that quotes verse 5 as though a loss were a verdict is the error He was correcting. The lesson names both abuses as abuses. And He closed the loophole Himself by supplying a second case with no human agent in it — which is why this passage settles the question rather than merely touching it. Where the Word is reticent about why that tower fell, the lesson stays reticent (DR-0098).
2. **1 Timothy 5:8 — the limit is stated BEFORE the application.** Paul is writing about care of relatives, widows in particular. The passage does not mention insurance and cannot be made to command it. What it establishes is that provision for one's own household is a duty of faith rather than a temperament. Teaching it as a policy mandate inflates the verse, and an inflated verse loses its authority the first time a hearer reads the chapter. The test asserts the limit appears in the first 60% of the lesson body, so it cannot drift to the end.
3. **2 Samuel 24 — David is speaking of WORSHIP, not premiums.** The principle he states (a thing that cost you nothing is worth nothing in your hand) is what carries, and nothing is built on top of it. And *"So the LORD was intreated for the land"* is never made a transaction — He was intreated, not purchased. The lesson corrects that misreading in its own text.
4. **Matthew 7 — the storm clauses are identical and both builders heard.** Read side by side, verses 25 and 27 describe the same weather word for word. The weather was never the variable. The course ends by affirming its own eight sessions of practical work as walls worth building AND saying plainly that not one item of it is the foundation — *great was the fall of it* is a warning aimed hardest at the best-prepared household in the room.

**Three candidates were measured and deliberately left OFF**, with the reasons recorded in the file rather than in a commit nobody re-reads. Each would fire on correct content today, which that file forbids:

- **`liability`** — 26 genuine uses across 13 lessons after the matcher fix below, no matcher problem, and a word a young reader genuinely does not own. It is owed a pass that AUTHORS the gloss at each site; it must never be added by hollowing its cues. **re-review: 2026-10-03.**
- **`peril`** — fires at the word start of `perilously`, which is ordinary English (ll66). Needs a matcher that understands inflection, not a list entry. **re-review: 2026-10-03.**
- **`underwriting`** — 2 of its 7 hits are a document TITLE, the FHA's 1938 Underwriting Manual (econ5). A title is not a term use, so the same matcher work gates it. **re-review: 2026-10-03.**

## And the gate's own matcher was improved, non-regressively

Judging `liability` exposed a defect in the gate itself: `termFaults` matched with a bare `indexOf`, so `liability` was found inside **`reliability`** and `peril` inside **`perilously`** — 13 of the 39 `liability` hits were that, and a check that fires on correct content will pass on wrong content. The matcher now requires a **word-start boundary** and deliberately keeps the tail open, so an inflection (`assayer`, `usurious`) still counts as a use.

Measured before and after across 471 mounted modules: **baseline 65, live 65, no new fault, none gone.** A start boundary can only ever match less, and it was re-measured to prove the recorded debt did not move.
