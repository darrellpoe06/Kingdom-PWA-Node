# DR-0373 — I will not go out free: the bondservant answer to the slavery objection

**Date:** 2026-09-13 · **Status:** accepted · **Tier:** B · **Area:** word · **Principles:** WORD-FIRST, TEACH-DONT-DEBATE, VERIFICATION-DOCTRINE, APP-IS-PRIMARY, DECISION-RECORDS

## Directive

The same friend whose four objections produced L142 returned with a fifth. Darrell, 2026-09-13:

> *"also that friend said Yahweh wants us to be His slaves... and laughs... I said bond servants who He created and the died for to repurchase because of His Love For Them... lesson..."*

**His answer is the lesson.** Bond servants — created by Him, and died for to repurchase, out of love. Everything in L145 is that sentence checked against the text.

## Decisions

1. **Concede the vocabulary in the first thirty seconds, out loud.** The language of servitude saturates Scripture and Paul opens letters naming himself a *doulos*. A defence that begins by denying it has lost and deserves to. The reply is not that the word is absent; it is that the Word defines its own terms, and the objection is reading a nineteenth-century institution back into a document legislating something else. **This is framed as a DEFINITION error, not a moral one**, which is what keeps the conversation survivable.

2. **Teach the three provisions consecutively, because the force is cumulative and almost nobody has heard them in a row.**
   - **Freedom is the default, on a clock, and free:** `six years he shall serve: and in the seventh he shall go out free for nothing`.
   - **The master funds the departure:** `thou shalt not let him go away empty` · `Thou shalt furnish him liberally out of thy flock, and out of thy floor, and out of thy winepress`. The servant leaves with capital.
   - **Permanence requires the servant's own spoken declaration of love**, and the law writes his sentence for him: `I love my master, my wife, and my children; I will not go out free`, with Deuteronomy giving the reason — `because he loveth thee and thine house`.

3. **The location of the mark carries the argument.** He is brought `to the door, or unto the door post` before witnesses and pierced there. **He is marked at the exit he refused.** The mark does not record a capture; it records a man standing in an open doorway holding his severance who turned around.

4. **The test is handed to the room rather than the conclusion.** No slave code in history writes automatic release, compulsory severance and a required profession of love into its own founding statute, because those three provisions dissolve the institution they would protect. Let them reach it themselves.

5. **The purchase is inverted, not softened.** Human sale spends money and leaves the buyer intact — that is simply what buying a person is. Peter names silver and gold and **refuses** them: `not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold` but `with the precious blood of Christ`. The buyer paid with Himself, which is why `Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends`.

6. **The standing is raised in His own mouth, with the reason given**, so nobody has to accept it as our interpretation: `Henceforth I call you not servants; for the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth: but I have called you friends` — the stated reason is disclosure. Then `no more a servant, but a son; and if a son, then an heir`, and the fear removed by name: `not received the spirit of bondage again to fear`. Servant → friend → son → heir, running the opposite direction from every system the objection imagines.

7. **The premise underneath is denied outright.** The objection assumes a choice between being owned and being autonomous. `to whom ye yield yourselves servants to obey, his servants ye are to whom ye obey`. Nobody is unowned. The only live question is which master and what he pays, and both accounts are published: `the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life`. One pays wages that are earned; the other gives a gift, which by definition is not.

8. **THE CLOSE THE OBJECTION CANNOT FOLLOW.** Psalm 40 carries a line that reads oddly until a reader knows the bondservant law: `mine ears hast thou opened`. Hebrews places that psalm in Christ's mouth as He enters the world. **The Son takes the bondservant's opened ear.** He is the one who stood at the door with the release in His hand and would not go out free — and the household He stayed for had not yet been bought.

9. **The laugh is answered without flinching and without preaching.** A man laughing at a master who wants slaves is laughing at something that is not there, and nobody is obliged to defend a caricature. The lesson ends by handing over the passage and **one question**, and the lesson text itself ends on a question mark rather than a summary — pinned by test.
