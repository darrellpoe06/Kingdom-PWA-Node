## Two sources, one lesson

The **occasion** is the Prince message. It is credited by name, treated as the occasion, and **not reproduced** — every line of the lesson is our own prose taught from the Scriptures the message opened. Its spine supplied five movements: Yahweh as Giver rather than taker (Hebrews 7:7), faith as the window, short prayer versus pretentious prayer, covenant authority (exousia), and grace running to glory.

The **second source is Darrell**, who spoke four words into the build while it was being written:

1. *"hearts of stone changed to hearts of flesh"*, because *"life situations make us have hard or stone hearts"*, and *"only believing Yahweh about the Good News of His Son"* is how *"we can't get helped"* turns into helped.
2. *"Only righteousness because of bringing thoughts to obey Him... like He Obeyed the Father"*.
3. *"to the death of me... self me to His version of the me"*.
4. *"each one chosen and need to also choose Him"*.

**Those four are not an appendix to the sermon outline — they are the machinery it assumes and never describes**, which is why they are movements six through eight and why the hardened heart is taught as the **HINGE**. None of the first five movements can get in through a stone.

## The weld

The lesson turns on **two stones**, and the join was not in either source — it came out of the texts themselves. The stone that must **GO** is the stony heart (Ezekiel 36:26). The stone you are **BUILT INTO** is a **LIVELY** one (1 Peter 2:5), set in a wall with others. One stone out, another made; and a destiny in Scripture is therefore corporate and located — a place in a house, never a solo career.

The anchor is **Romans 8:30**, deliberately, not the authority material: a room that meets *exousia* first hears a technique. Predestinate → called → justified → **glorified**, every link His verb, glorification in the past tense about people still walking around unfinished.

## Where we taught rather than repeated

- **exousia vs dunamis.** The traffic-officer illustration is sound and it is warranted **by the vocabulary of Luke 10:19 itself** — the KJV renders both nouns "power," but what He *gives* is `exousia` (delegated right) and what the enemy *has* is `dunamis` (raw force). The illustration is taken out of the sentence rather than laid on top of it.
- **The keys (DR-0098, exegetical restraint).** It is commonly preached that Jesus took the keys back from the adversary between the cross and the resurrection. The Word says He **HAS** them (Revelation 1:18) and that He **DESTROYED** him that had the power of death (Hebrews 2:14). **It narrates no handover.** The lesson teaches the two written sentences and says out loud that it declines to supply the third. Gated (`does not narrate`).
- **The order of righteousness.** Received by **one** obedience (Romans 5:19; 2 Corinthians 5:21; Philippians 3:9), then **walked** by captive thoughts (2 Corinthians 10:5; Romans 6:16). Inverted, Darrell's second word becomes a treadmill that exhausts a sincere person for thirty years. Both halves are gated together.
- **Chosen and choosing.** Both are His sentences (John 15:16; Joshua 24:15), and Revelation 17:14 holds *"called, and chosen, and faithful"* in one line. Taught, with no contest staged for the reader to referee.

## The fences, gated as load-bearing

This is faith-and-authority material — the class that wounds people when it is preached bare. Three fences are asserted by the suite and may not be removed:

1. **Sceva's sons** (Acts 19) — the name is not a password; a spirit could tell the difference immediately.
2. **Paul's thorn** (2 Corinthians 12:8-9) — a clean, faith-filled prayer, thrice made, by an apostle, answered **No**. Any presentation of the window that cannot hold this will wound a grieving person in the exact hour they most need the Word. James 4:2-3 supply the other two honest causes.
3. **Luke 10:20** — the authority is real and is **not the treasure**.
