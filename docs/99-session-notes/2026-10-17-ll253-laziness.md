## Where it came from, said plainly

Two inputs, kept apart on purpose.

1. **A third-party video summary.** Darrell forwarded this house a summary of a video on laziness by **Jonny Ardavanis and Hank Bowen**. It is *not* a teaching spoken into this house, it is *not* a message from Bishop Gwin, and **no teacher of ours is identified in it** (DR-0712 / DR-0719). The lesson credits them by name on every surface and then tests every point against the Word, which is the only authority it stands on.
2. **Darrell's own three words, spoken into the build the same day:** *"Work is worship..."* His framing, not the video's, and the deepest ground the lesson has. It is given its own movement, placed after the image-of-Yahweh ground and before the four domains, and it reframes the close: if work is worship, slothfulness is not merely unproductive and not only a failure to love the neighbour — it is **worship withheld**.

The hinge verse is the one where his sentence and the Word meet: Romans 12:11, where the opposite of *slothful* is not *busy* but **serving the Lord**.

## The twelve movements, in the same order in all five places

1. Made in the image of a working Yahweh — Genesis 1:27; Genesis 2:15 (the garden job, **before** the fall); Genesis 3:19 (the sweat, after); Exodus 20:9; John 5:17.
2. **Work is worship** (Darrell, 2026-10-17) — Romans 12:11; Colossians 3:23-24; Colossians 3:17; 1 Corinthians 10:31; Romans 12:1; Ephesians 6:6-7 (**eyeservice** named); Exodus 20:8, 20:11 (labour and worship-rest in one commandment, on His own example); Psalms 90:17. **Both guards here:** worship is never a purchase (Ephesians 2:8-9; Titus 3:5), and the measure is faithfulness with what is actually in your hand, not output (Ecclesiastes 9:10).
3. Work is how we love our neighbour — Mark 12:31; Ephesians 4:28; 2 Thessalonians 3:10; 1 Thessalonians 4:11-12; 1 Timothy 5:8.
4. What the speakers said, and what the Word says — the antonym framing and "a mature Christian cannot be lazy" named as **theirs**; the Word **commands** believers not to be slothful (Hebrews 6:11-12; Romans 12:11), which presupposes they can be. Sin named; repentance offered; salvation and maturity not under review. Not staged as two views (DR-0098).
5. The four domains — physical (1 Corinthians 6:19-20; 1 Timothy 4:8), mental (Romans 12:2; 2 Corinthians 10:5; Philippians 4:8), spiritual (Joshua 1:8; 2 Timothy 2:15), vocational (Colossians 3:23; Proverbs 22:29; Ecclesiastes 9:10).
6. The first mark, the lion in the road — Proverbs 26:13; 22:13.
7. The second mark, the door on its hinges — Proverbs 26:14; 6:9-11; 20:4.
8. The third mark, the hand that never comes back — Proverbs 19:24; 26:15; 12:27.
9. The fourth mark, wiser in his own conceit — Proverbs 26:16.
10. The fifth mark, no guide, overseer, or ruler — Proverbs 6:6-8. The structural gift: 6:7 is the exact inverse of the mark and sits one verse after the invitation, in the same breath as the remedy.
11. What it costs, and what it costs others — Proverbs 10:4; 13:4; 14:23; 12:24; 10:26 (vinegar and smoke, a verse about the people waiting on him); 18:9; 1 Corinthians 12:21, 25, 26; the field of the slothful, Proverbs 24:30-34.
12. The remedy and the practice — Proverbs 6:6; 27:1; James 4:13-14; Proverbs 27:23; Philippians 2:13 (He supplies the willing and the doing); James 4:17; 1 Corinthians 15:58 — then **TALK ABOUT IT TOGETHER**, all three directions, in every place.
