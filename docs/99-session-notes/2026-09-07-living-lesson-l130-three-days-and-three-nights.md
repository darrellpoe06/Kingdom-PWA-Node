## The answer, before the machinery

**His POWER was destroyed. His PERSON was not, and the Word never records a fight down there.**

Both halves are Scripture, and holding only one of them is a lie in one of the two
directions DR-0076 forbids:

- **Over-claiming** — a vivid three-day battle. Scripture narrates no such thing: no
  dialogue, no struggle, no ransom paid to the adversary, no timeline of the hours.
- **Under-claiming** — "nothing happened" or "we can't know." The Word is explicit
  about the disarming, and precise about its DATE.

The date is the cross. `"It is finished"` (John 19:30) was said **before** He died;
Colossians 2:14-15 puts the public triumph "in it" — the cross of the preceding
verse; Hebrews 2:14 names the weapon as the dying itself, **through death**. The
three days are the Victor taking possession of territory already lost, not a duel
deciding who owns it.

## The four movements the lesson is built on

1. **The clock and the sign.** Matthew 12:40 sets three days and three nights and
   names *Jonah* as the sign — and Jonah did not conquer the deep, he was brought
   up out of it (Jonah 2:2, 2:6). Jesus chose a **rescue** sign over every conquest
   image available to Him.
2. **The translation knot, which is where most of the confusion actually lives.**
   The KJV uses one English word, *hell*, for two Greek words: `hadēs` (G86, the
   unseen realm of the dead — Acts 2:27, quoting Psalms 16:10) and `geenna` (G1067,
   the final fire — Matthew 5:22). **The Word proves they are two places in one
   sentence:** *"And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the
   second death."* (Revelation 20:14) What is cast in is not what it is cast into.
   Luke 23:43 then forecloses the torment reading outright: *paradise*, that same day.
3. **The four verbs.** Every text about those hours gives one of four actions, and
   not one of them is *fight*: He **preached** (1 Peter 3:19 — `kērussō`, G2784, the
   herald's word: heralds publish verdicts already reached), He **led captivity
   captive** (Ephesians 4:8-9), He **holds the keys** (Revelation 1:18), He **rose**
   (Matthew 28:6). Occupation, not combat.
4. **`katargeō`.** The KJV *destroy* in Hebrews 2:14 is G2673 — render inoperative,
   put out of commission — not annihilate; and 1 John 3:8 names the object with the
   same care: the **works**. That precision is what lets both halves of the answer
   stand together without strain.

## Why he is still walking — proved from inside the Word

The strongest evidence is that every letter cited was written by men who already
knew the tomb was empty, and they still describe him as active: 1 Peter 5:8
(*"as a roaring lion, walketh about"*), Ephesians 6:11-12 (armour still issued),
Revelation 12:12 (*"because he knoweth that he hath but a short time"* — he **knows**).
A dead enemy is not angry; a winning one is not in a hurry.

**Three dates, kept apart**, and keeping them apart is what dissolves the apparent
contradiction: the **verdict** is past (John 12:31, John 16:11), the **binding** is
future and bounded (Revelation 20:2-3, *"after that he must be loosed a little
season"*), the **execution** is future and final (Revelation 20:10). We live between
the gavel and the sentence.

## Yahweh's perspective — taken from His own purpose clauses, not from our guesses

Three purpose statements, and none of them contains a fight scene:

| Purpose of the… | Text | Stated goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| descent | Hebrews 2:15 | *"deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage"* — **your chain**, not a spectacle |
| appearing | 1 John 3:8 | the **works** of the devil undone |
| resurrection | Colossians 1:18; Philippians 2:9 | the Son's **preeminence** in all things |

## The points for the body of Christ

Fight **from** victory, never for one (Ephesians 6:13 — *"having done all, to stand"*).
The fear of death is legally broken (Hebrews 2:15; 2 Timothy 1:10). The keys are held
by one Person (Revelation 1:18; Romans 6:9). Disarmed is not absent — be sober,
neither dismissive nor impressed (1 Peter 5:8; Jude 1:9). Submit **then** resist; the
promise hangs on both halves (James 4:7). The overcoming is the blood and your own
spoken testimony, never a technique (Revelation 12:11). His sentence is fixed and
yours is settled (Revelation 20:10; Romans 8:1). You were not merely rescued but
**transferred** (Colossians 1:13; Ephesians 2:6). And keep the Word's own proportion:
four Gospels on the Lamb, one verse on the adversary's end (Revelation 5:9).

## Where the Word stops, the lesson stops

Deuteronomy 29:29 is the fence, and it is taught explicitly rather than merely
observed. A detailed play-by-play of those hours is an **addition**, and every
addition of that kind moves the adversary toward the center of a book about the Son.
Romans 10:7 even warns against the instinct to go looking down there for Him.

**Continuity with L129.** The L129 note already recorded this restraint on the
adjacent question — it is commonly preached that Jesus took the keys *back* from the
adversary, and the Word says only that He **has** them (Revelation 1:18) and that He
destroyed him that had the power of death (Hebrews 2:14); it narrates no handover.
L129's gate pins that (`does not narrate`). L130 extends the same discipline to the
whole three days rather than contradicting it.
