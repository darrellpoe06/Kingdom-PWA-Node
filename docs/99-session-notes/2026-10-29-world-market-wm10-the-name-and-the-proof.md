# wm10 — The name and the proof: what a seller's reputation is worth when the works are checked

**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Module id:** `wm10-the-name-and-the-proof`
**Course:** The World Market: Countries That Trade (`world-market`), lesson 10 of 10
**Record:** DR-1282
**Source:** Gmail thread `1a11be244e5f5452` — Retail Dive, 8 October 2026, "Consumer behavior is changing. Are you ready?", forwarded by Darrell to himself with one word at the top of the body: **"Lessons"**. Three weeks old when it was built.

## What the request was

One word, over an email that turned out to contain no journalism at all.

**The source is thin, and saying so is the first honest act of this lesson.** The whole message is a single sponsor placement for the Kearney Consumer Institute's report "The future consumer": "This week's Retail Dive brought to you by" across the top, a banner, four separate links to the same report, a Chicago street address, four social icons, a media-kit pitch, an unsubscribe footer. There is no Retail Dive reporting in it. Darrell's standing rule is that advertising is ignored entirely unless the advertisement is itself germane, and in almost every case that rule would have ended this with no lesson built.

The germane content is **three claims and a study size** — 21,500 consumers surveyed; $20 trillion in spending possibly becoming contestable; 86 percent caring more about whether a thing works than about the brand; 80 minutes a day freed by automation. That is all of it. Nothing else in the email is taught.

## Why it was taught anyway

Because what the advertisement sells is a report about **trust**, and it arrived as an advertisement — which is, structurally, a name asking to be believed. The material and the envelope say the same thing from opposite sides, and noticing that is worth more than the report.

The lesson is careful that this is **structural, not moral**, and says so in its own words: nothing in it accuses Kearney of dishonesty and nothing in it knows whether Kearney is honest. The objection is only the one Scripture made first — *"Let another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth"* (Proverbs 27:2). Self-praise is not wicked; it is simply not the witness.

## Why this module and not another application

The course had nine lessons. Eight built the machinery and gave the just weight; the ninth took all of it to one front door and accounted for a winter heating bill.

**All nine stood the reader on the same side of the counter** — the family needing corn in Egypt, the household unable to stop buying heat in January. Every one taught the reader as a buyer, and not one ever asked what the name on the other side was worth or how a buyer checks it. That is a hole in the argument, not a missing application, and it is the hole a reader falls into the moment he leaves the course and has to decide whether to believe a seller. The tenth lesson crosses the counter.

**And its spine is deliberately not the ninth's.** wm9 teaches the provenance of a FIGURE: who said it, on what day. wm10 teaches the standing of a NAME: what shows it, and the buyer's own weight. wm10 *uses* wm9's instrument instead of re-teaching it.

## The thing this lesson could do that wm9 could not

wm9 had to admit its figures were unverifiable because they arrived inside an email, and an email is not a publisher's page a runner can read a number back off.

**This email is a sponsor placement, and a sponsor's entire purpose is to send the reader to the sponsor's own page.** So the page exists, is named in the email, and a runner reaches it. Measured: this cloud sandbox cannot reach `www.kearney.com` at all (403 to the CONNECT from the agent proxy, by policy), and a GitHub runner read it in under two seconds — DR-0108, the team's reach rather than one agent's.

Two probe runs on a throwaway branch (**40791955446**, **40792095572**) found what reasoning would not have:

- the page says **"86 percent"**, not the email's **"86%"**;
- it carries neither **"proven performance"** nor **"up for grabs"** — both are the email's copy, not the publisher's;
- it does carry "21,500 consumers", "$20 trillion in", "whether something works", "brand name", "contestable";
- the email's **"80 minutes a day" is absent from the page entirely.**

So wm10 takes the FIRST branch of `the-third-pile-is-a-worklist` on its first commit: a real row in `lesson-sources.json`, `CHECKED_LESSONS` 2 → 3, and **the GAP does not move** — it stays 13.

## And the limit of that row is the lesson

This is the part worth remembering, because a green read-back is more dangerous than a red one.

**A read-back proves ATTRIBUTION and nothing else.** It proves Kearney asserts these figures on its own page. It proves nothing about whether the study is sound, whether 21,500 people were in fact surveyed, what question produced that 86, or whether a number the size of $20 trillion means anything a person can act on — and **the publisher here is also the party selling the report**, so the witness is the seller.

One sentence carries it, and it occurs exactly once in the adult text and once in each of the four bands: *we asked the name whether the name said it, and we did not test the works.*

## What nobody verified, carried on all five surfaces

The report itself was never read — it is sold, not given. The study's method, sample and question wording are unknown here. The 80-minutes claim did not read back and stands as the email's claim and nothing further. The adult text and all four bands say each of these, and the pinned gate makes every one non-removable with a strip proof that deletes it from one band and watches the check name that band.

## The Word it stands on

Both halves of a name, never one alone. A good name is real property, *"rather to be chosen than great riches"* (Proverbs 22:1; Ecclesiastes 7:1) — so the lesson refuses to teach a reader to sneer at every reputation, which is not discernment but an inability to receive what faithfulness produces. AND a name does not certify itself: Proverbs 27:2, and the sharpest line in the Word on the subject, spoken by the risen Christ to a church — *"I know thy works, that thou hast a name that thou livest, and art dead"* (Revelation 3:1), where the **works are known first and the name is measured against them.**

The test is the fruit (Matthew 7:16), put as a challenge by James (2:18). The proving is commanded **and so is the holding** — *"Prove all things; hold fast that which is good"* (1 Thessalonians 5:21) — which is the clause that keeps the lesson from curdling into cynicism. The outward is named the wrong instrument (1 Samuel 16:7), and the scale a household can actually operate is the small thing (Luke 16:10).

**Then the turn nobody is warned about.** The buyer carries a weight too: *"It is naught, it is naught, saith the buyer: but when he is gone his way, then he boasteth"* (Proverbs 20:14) — talks it down while bargaining, boasts of the bargain once clear of the shop. Lesson eight's rule was never addressed to sellers only: *"Divers weights, and divers measures, both of them are alike abomination to the LORD"* (Proverbs 20:10), and Yahweh fixes it with His own name attached (Leviticus 19:36). A reader who leaves better at suspecting sellers and no better at weighing his own claims has taken the wrong half.

The freed-time claim gets the question no advertisement asks — what the minutes are FOR — answered by *"Redeeming the time, because the days are evil"* (Ephesians 5:16). We hold ourselves to the same measure (2 Corinthians 8:21), which is why the lesson shows its working in public. And one Name answers the test exactly: Jesus, the Lamb of Yahweh and the Eternal Son of Yahweh (John 1:29; John 3:16), who did not send a claim ahead of Himself but came and did the work.

**Two parables, never testimonies.** A lesson built from an advertisement has nobody's life in it, and `kind: 'testimony'` tells the reader on the page that a real person lived this and consented to its telling. Both stories are third-person parables and the gate refuses both the label and a first-person narrator.

## What the gates and the measurements caught that the author did not

1. The **child band was short at 0.47** against its floor. Fixed by giving that band the three concrete actions and the unread-report disclosure it was genuinely missing — not by cutting adult teaching to flatter a ratio.
2. The **child band named no publication at all**, so a reader of that one band met four figures with no provenance. Found by counting provenance strings across all five places.
3. **The firm was named nowhere.** The first draft said "a consulting firm" throughout, reasoning that naming it would read as a verdict. Wrong on provenance (DR-0076 §8): a reader cannot check a figure whose publisher is withheld, and naming who published a study is attribution, not accusation.
4. **A pin that would have proved nothing.** Every figure in this lesson occurs **twice** in each adult-register band — once in the claim, once in the read-back list — so pinning "86 percent" to prove a disclosure exists would have passed with the disclosure deleted. Exactly the trap `367,000` set for DR-1275 and `Mitch Berk` for DR-1278. Avoided rather than survived: all five disclosure signals were counted first, each occurs exactly once, and the test asserts that uniqueness in its own right.
5. **The course's care note was already false on main.** `WORLD_MARKET_CARE_NOTE` promised the course "quotes no figure" — a promise wm9 broke hours earlier with `$5.26 a gallon`, and nobody corrected. A DR-0239 defect that predates this lesson, fixed here rather than left.
