## What the verification pass changed (DR-0192)

Five corrections to the speaker and two honest limits, **all seven taught in the lesson** rather than quietly fixed:

1. **Dating.** The source said the Soleb inscription is from the end of the 15th century BC. Amenhotep III's reign is conventionally c. 1390–1350 BC — the **14th**. Corrected in every band, with what the source said.
2. **Soleb's reading is genuinely argued** (tier 2, narrowly). Established: the temple, the topographic list, the entry, the four signs. Argued among Egyptologists: whether `yhwꜣ` there names the One or a locality, and whether those Shasu are Israelites. The Egyptian also writes a final sign that is not the final Hebrew letter. The lesson says so in every band and **builds nothing on it** — and applies the diagnostic out loud: strike it off and nothing here falls over.
3. **The Mesha Stele is basalt, not granite.** "Black basalt" in every band.
4. **Qumran handled the name several ways on purpose** — the square script, paleo-Hebrew letters inlaid into an otherwise square-script manuscript, and tetrapuncta in some scrolls — not only the square script. The correction is the better witness and is taught as such.
5. **The written name is preserved and read; it is not pronounced.** A substitute is read aloud out of reverence. Standard editions are set from the Leningrad Codex (colophon 1008/1009 AD). The reticence is itself corroborating.
6. **The vocalisation limit, stated rather than left.** Consonants certain; vowels not transmitted — the pointing belongs to the substitute word. "Yahweh" is a widely-held reconstruction from early Greek transcriptions and the theophoric short form, **not a recording**. Stated in every band, with the reason it costs nothing: the rule was never about the sound.
7. **The speaker is named only on the basis the source gives.** The transcript never names him; he names his own book on camera, *Where God Came Down*, catalogued as the work of Joel P. Kramer (Expedition Bible, 2020). No faith label, affiliation or motive is attached — the correction World Issues 21 needed — and the gate scans for the absence.

**The spine came out of the source's own mistake.** He states on camera that he will substitute the name into the verses as he reads them, and does. We never do. The lesson teaches the distinction by performing it, and the gate's second proven-to-catch break is exactly that substitution — "I am Yahweh: that is my name" for Isaiah 42:8 — so our own house convention reaching into a verse fails the build.
