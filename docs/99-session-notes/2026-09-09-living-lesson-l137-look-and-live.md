## The answer, and why it is not ours to give

**Yes — and the Son gave it first.** The serpent on the pole is not an analogy this lesson proposes; it is the one Jesus reached for to explain His own death, to Nicodemus, one breath before John 3:16: *"And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life."* (John 3:14-15). John 3:16 is the sentence that FOLLOWS the serpent. *Lifted up* is defined by Him too: *"This he said, signifying what death he should die."* (John 12:33).

The lesson takes Darrell's question in its parts and shows the Word answering each — and in every part the Word says more than the question dared:

1. **The type is His.** John 3:14-16; John 12:32-33.
2. **What was on the pole.** Why a serpent and not a lamb: *"in the likeness of sinful flesh"* (Romans 8:3). The cure is shaped like the curse — made sin (2 Corinthians 5:21), made a curse, hanged on a tree (Galatians 3:13; Deuteronomy 21:23). Not the devil honoured but the bite's cause **displayed as judged**, which is what the cross did to the powers (Colossians 2:15). The Lamb (John 1:29) is held beside it, not replaced.
3. **The look was believing.** The sin in the camp was a *sentence* — *"the people spake against God"* (Numbers 21:5) — the serpent's oldest line (Genesis 3:4; John 8:44). Yahweh said *look*, not *be healed*: trusting His word with your eyes, poison already in the veins. **Darrell's phrase, believe Him over the serpent, is the exact shape of the text.** Isaiah 45:22, Psalms 34:5, Hebrews 12:2, John 6:40 — seeing and believing are one verb. And nobody was told to look *well*: the look is not a work (Ephesians 2:8).
4. **Reach and length.** Every one that is bitten; any man; whosoever — and no further (John 3:18, 3:36). *Eternally healed* is the Word's own language (1 Peter 2:24; John 11:26). The one distinction the Word keeps: the brass serpent healed a body for a lifetime; the Son destroys death (1 Corinthians 15:26). The picture is smaller than the Person.
5. **The serpent's end.** Genesis 3:15 → 1 John 3:8 → Hebrews 2:14 (*through death* — the pole was the weapon) → Revelation 20:10, Matthew 25:41. *Eternal flames* is right; the precision kept (DR-0100) is that the second death (Revelation 20:14) is not a ceasing — **his end is not an ending.**
6. **What the story adds, and where it stops.** The serpents were **not taken away** (Numbers 21:7 vs 21:8) — a cure placed inside the danger. And the sign became an idol: Nehushtan (2 Kings 18:4). Faith looks *through* the sign. Where the Word is silent (why brass, what the pole looked like) the lesson is silent.
