## Why COLG isn't in the local-pack search results

### Problem 1 — The site identifies as "THE LOVE CORNER" not "The Church of the Living God"

The HTML title is "THE LOVE CORNER - HOME". The Open Graph title is "THE LOVE CORNER". The site name in OG is "THE LOVE CORNER". Google indexes by title; people search for "Church of the Living God" or "Champaign Illinois church" and Google's index has this site under a completely different name. "The Love Corner" is a meaningful nickname the congregation uses, but it's the OPPOSITE of what searchers type when looking for a church.

**Estimated impact:** this single fix is worth 50-70% of the visibility gap.

### Problem 2 — Meta description has zero value proposition + keywords

"The Church Of The Living God" alone tells Google nothing about why a searcher would click vs the other 10 churches in the list. The meta description is what shows in search results below the title — it's the elevator pitch to a stranger seeing the site for the first time.

### Problem 3 — Missing or incomplete Google Business Profile

Every church in the local-pack screenshot has: a star rating (4.5-5.0), a review count (11-68 reviews), hours of operation, "Call/Directions/Website/Share" buttons. These all come from Google Business Profile (GBP), which is the SINGLE biggest local-SEO lever Google offers. Without a verified, complete, actively-managed GBP, you're invisible in the local pack regardless of how good the website is.

**Estimated impact:** this is worth the OTHER 30-40% of the visibility gap. Combined with Problem 1, it's most of the difference.

### Problem 4 — No Schema.org structured data

Google's local pack uses Schema.org markup (specifically `Church` schema with `PostalAddress`, `OpeningHoursSpecification`, `Place`, `geo` coordinates, etc.) to populate the rich snippets that appear in search results. The current site has no structured data visible — it's just HTML.

### Problem 5 — Sermons live on YouTube but aren't on the church's site

YouTube has the sermon corpus. The website has a "BROADCAST" link but the actual sermon content isn't on the church's domain. This means:
- Searches for sermon-specific topics (e.g., "sermon on 1 Samuel 3 Champaign", "Hannah and Samuel sermon", "Pastor Bishop Gwin teaching") don't land on the church's site
- The site doesn't compound SEO value over time as sermons accumulate
- The church's domain authority doesn't grow from sermon content

Each sermon SHOULD be its own page on the site with the sermon title, scripture reference, summary, embedded YouTube video, and full transcript. Over 78 years' worth of sermon catalog at scale, that's hundreds of pages of original content — Google rewards that.

### Problem 6 — Address and hours buried in a table layout

Search engines + accessibility tools both prefer addresses in semantic HTML (or Schema.org `PostalAddress`), not table cells. Current rendering puts the address in a table layout that crawlers parse less reliably.

### Problem 7 — "African American Non-Denominational" not on-page

The phrase "African American Non-Denominational Church" is in the meta keywords but NOT in the visible page content. This matters because:
- Google indexes visible page content much more heavily than meta tags
- The intentional community + denomination identity is part of WHY someone would specifically seek out COLG vs a different church
- Searches like "African American church Champaign" or "non-denominational church Champaign" should find COLG and don't (currently)

### Problem 8 — No reviews / testimonials on-site

Trust signals matter for both Google ranking + actual visitor conversion. The site has no testimonials, member stories, or "what to expect" content for a first-time visitor.

### Problem 9 — Newsletter signup has weak conversion language

"NEWSLETTER Email* I agree to receiving marketing and promotional materials* Subscribe to Newsletter" — the legal compliance is fine, but the framing is corporate-marketing-speak, not pastoral-welcome. A visitor's first interaction shouldn't feel like signing up for a brand's email list.

### Problem 10 — "About Us" content is good but undifferentiated

The about copy is warm + biblical + true — but doesn't ARTICULATE the specific differentiator Darrell named: "exist as the church body of Christ as He says not the flesh so we all submit to the Spirit collectively as community." That message is the GOLD — it's what would attract the specific seeker who's looking for Spirit-led authentic community vs entertainment-driven mainstream-church alternatives.

## The decisions, with their rationale

Per the "give from understanding" principle:

### Decision 1 — Fix the HTML title FIRST, before anything else

**We chose:** change the site title from "THE LOVE CORNER" to "The Church of the Living God — Champaign, IL — African American Non-Denominational Church".

**We did NOT choose:** keep the "Love Corner" branding consistently as the primary identity.

**Because:** "The Love Corner" is a beloved internal nickname; "The Church of the Living God" is the actual legal + searchable name. Search engines index by title. People search "Church of the Living God Champaign" + "Champaign Illinois church" + "African American church Champaign" — none of those queries match "The Love Corner." The site can keep "The Love Corner" as visible branding, but the HTML title must match what people actually search for. This single change moves the needle more than any other single fix.

### Decision 2 — Google Business Profile is the SINGLE biggest local-SEO lever; claim + complete it

**We chose:** make GBP setup the second-highest-priority fix.

**We did NOT choose:** treat GBP as optional or rely on the website alone.

**Because:** the local-pack search results (the screenshot Darrell shared) come from Google Business Profile data, NOT from website crawling. A church can have a perfect website + zero GBP and remain invisible in the local pack. A church can have a mediocre website + perfect GBP and appear at the top of the local pack. The local pack is where 70%+ of "church near me" clicks go. GBP is non-negotiable for local visibility.

### Decision 3 — Add Schema.org Church markup

**We chose:** add structured data to the site.

**We did NOT choose:** rely on Google's auto-detection of address + hours from page content.

**Because:** Schema.org is the standard Google reads first for local-business data. Auto-detection from HTML is unreliable. Explicit Schema markup is reliable + gives Google all the data needed to populate rich snippets in search results (hours, address, phone, "open now" indicator). Low-cost (one JSON-LD block in the Weebly header) for high-confidence indexing benefit.

### Decision 4 — Put the differentiator copy ON the homepage above the fold

**We chose:** the "Body of Christ as He says, not the flesh" framing in visible homepage content + meta description + HTML title alternations.

**We did NOT choose:** leave it implicit in About Us or hide it in pastoral letters.

**Because:** the specific seeker who is tired of entertainment-church is googling for AUTHENTIC Spirit-led community. They scan local-pack results + click into the church that sounds different. The differentiator copy IS what makes COLG sound different. Burying it deprives the seeker of the signal they're looking for AND deprives COLG of the very members who'd be most aligned long-term.

### Decision 5 — Use the existing sermon corpus as both internal prep tool AND external SEO content

**We chose:** Build 2.1 (sermon pages on site) shares the corpus + workflows that build the BG sermon-prep tool (workflows 73-77).

**We did NOT choose:** duplicate effort across internal vs external use.

**Because:** the corpus is the same data. The ingest pipeline is the same workflow. The theological review pipeline is the same Quality Gatekeeper logic. Building once + using twice (sermon prep for BG + SEO content for the website) gets dual value from a single infrastructure investment. Each new sermon becomes both BG's future-reference asset AND a page that compounds the church's domain authority.

### Decision 6 — Seasonal marketing as recurring content cadence, NOT one-off campaigns

**We chose:** build a year-round seasonal content + outreach calendar that follows the Christian liturgical year + American cultural seasons + COLG-specific dates.

**We did NOT choose:** "marketing campaigns" launched ad-hoc when growth is needed.

**Because:** search behavior is predictable + cyclical. People search "Christmas Eve service" every December. People search "Easter Sunday" every March/April. People search "VBS near me" every May-July. A church that ships content matching these patterns shows up in the searches; a church that doesn't is invisible during the highest-discovery seasons. Recurring cadence also means content compounds — Year 2's Easter content builds on Year 1's, etc.
