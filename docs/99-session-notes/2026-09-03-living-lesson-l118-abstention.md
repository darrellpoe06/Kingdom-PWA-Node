## What he brought in

A spoken declaration against being kept as an option. *Abstention. Don't you pick me. Don't you weigh me on a scale of convenience... I was never meant to be compared. I was meant to be chosen... Love is not a pros and cons list... I'm not paper. I'm flesh... you don't analyze fire. You respect it or you burn... I'm not the maybe in your sentence, I am the period... Don't call me friend when all you mean is convenience... You can't bargain with what's priceless... you can't discount what's divine... if I'm an option, don't you pick me. I wasn't built for deliberation. I was built to be honored... I ain't waiting nowhere to be picked. I was already chosen — by peace, by purpose, and by God.*

Six distinct claims. Four of them Scripture states outright and more strongly than the piece does. One it qualifies. And the last — *already chosen* — is the only ground on which the other five can stand without turning to poison. That ordering is the lesson.

## The premise held, not smoothed over (feedback-surface-premise-conflicts)

The easy failure here is a lesson that simply **cheers the piece**. That would leave a wound dressed as a virtue, and it would arm people. Two places the lesson refuses to do that:

- **Movement 6 — the qualification.** "Love is not a pros and cons list" is true about a covenant already made, and false as a blanket rule. Scripture *commands* deliberation: *"which of you, intending to build a tower, sitteth not down first, and counteth the cost"* (Luke 14:28); *"therefore choose life"* (Deuteronomy 30:19); Joshua and Elijah both say **choose**. What Scripture forbids is weighing that continues **after** the yes — *"Better is it that thou shouldest not vow, than that thou shouldest vow and not pay."* (Ecclesiastes 5:5); *"Yea, yea; Nay, nay"* (Matthew 5:37). **The line is temporal, not emotional:** weigh fully before, never after. A young reader who does not learn that line reads ordinary carefulness as disrespect.
- **Movement 11 — the correction the piece does not contain.** Three cautions, all from the Word. A guarded heart (Proverbs 4:23) and a long-suffering heart (1 Corinthians 13:4-7) are the same Bible. "Respect it or you burn" is not ours to administer — *"avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath"* (Romans 12:19). And the deepest one: *"I'm good"* can be a locked door wearing the clothes of peace (Ephesians 4:31-32). Beneath all three sits an idolatry-shaped danger — **a demand to be chosen without comparison, made of a human being, seats a creature where only the Creator belongs** (Jeremiah 17:5,7; Psalm 118:8). It will crush them and it will not satisfy you. Named as relief, not rebuke: most people asking to be chosen are wounded, not proud.

## Source discipline

The spoken piece is the **occasion** for a Word-first study — pinned in the lesson and its test as *tested by the Word: upheld where the Word upholds it, corrected where the flesh runs past it*, and never an authority standing beside Scripture. The facilitator notes require saying that out loud before the room brings its own stories in.

## Where the doctrinal weight landed

**The ground is the refused stone, not the grievance.** Before this touches a marriage, a friendship or a group chat, it is laid where Scripture lays it: the deepest experience of being passed over in the Bible is His. *"He is despised and rejected of men"* (Isaiah 53:3); *"The stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner."* (Psalm 118:22). Peter sets the two verdicts in one breath — *"disallowed indeed of men, but chosen of God, and precious,"* (1 Peter 2:4). Two ledgers on one stone, and only the Father's decided anything.

**The choosing predates every comparison.** Ephesians 1:4-5, 2 Timothy 1:9, John 15:16, Jeremiah 1:5. And Deuteronomy strips the metric out on purpose: not more in number, but *"the fewest of all people:"* (Deuteronomy 7:7). A choosing that never ran through a comparison cannot be reopened by one.

**Scripture out-says the piece on bargaining.** Song of Solomon 8:7 does not merely decline the transaction — everything a man owns, offered for love, *"would utterly be contemned."* Despised, because the offer insults what it is trying to buy. And the price question is closed by what was already spent: not silver and gold but *"the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:"* (1 Peter 1:19), landing on *"Ye are bought with a price; be not ye the servants of men."* (1 Corinthians 7:23).

**The closing trio got its texts, in his order** — peace (Colossians 3:15), purpose (Romans 8:28; 2 Timothy 1:9), Yahweh Himself (Isaiah 43:1; Hebrews 13:5; Romans 8:38-39) — and closes on the roll that fixes the order for good: *"they that are with him are called, and chosen, and faithful."* (Revelation 17:14). Faithfulness comes **third**, after the choosing, never as its price.

## The research finding — four words for one act

He asked for it researched, and the research produced something the lesson now turns on. **Scripture quotes Psalm 118:22 four separate times and uses a different English word each time:**

| Citation | Word |
|---|---|
| Psalm 118:22 | *"The stone which the builders **refused**"* |
| Matthew 21:42 · Mark 12:10 · Luke 20:17 | *"the builders **rejected**"* |
| Acts 4:11 | *"**set at nought** of you builders"* |
| 1 Peter 2:7 | *"the builders **disallowed**"* — the committee word, striking a name off a list |

Refused, rejected, set at nought, disallowed — four words for one act, and every one is a word somebody in the room has felt. Four times out of four the sentence ends at *the head of the corner*. That is now movement 2, and it is carried into the senior level, the quiz and the facilitator notes.

## Verification (DR-0076)

- **189 quoted spans, every one letter-for-letter KJV**, read from the in-repo corpus at `app/public/bible/kjv/`. Nothing quoted from memory. The authoring sweep found **zero** in-quote alterations — stated plainly rather than dressed up.
- **The research exposed a real, NEW hole in the existing gate, and this session closed it.** The whole-span gate asks only *"does this string exist somewhere in the Bible?"* It **cannot see a quotation that is real KJV hung on the wrong verse** — and this lesson walks straight through that trap, because "The stone which the builders **rejected**" is genuine KJV (Matthew 21:42) and is exactly what memory reaches for when writing Psalm 118:22. A new **ATTRIBUTION gate** checks all 83 pinned fragments against *that specific verse*, not the whole corpus.
- **Both gates proven-to-catch, demonstrated by real mutation, not assertion:**
  - *Correct text, wrong reference* — Psalm 118:22 rewritten with the Matthew wording. The whole-span gate **passed it silently** (109/110 green); the attribution gate failed it. That is the hole, demonstrated.
  - *One-letter in-quote drift* — `contemned` → `condemned` in Song of Solomon 8:7. The whole-span gate caught it.
  - Both mutations reverted; suite returned to 110/110.
- **Negative controls pinned:** cross-verse joins built from this lesson's own texts (Song 8:6→8:7, Revelation 3:15→3:16), single-word drift (`contemned`/`condemned`, the dropped second *and* in Revelation 17:14, `elect` for `chosen` in 1 Peter 2:4), and four checks that our own framing never wears Scripture's quotation marks.
- **Typography:** no generic "God" in our authored voice (34 mentions of Yahweh), no capitalized adversary name, `baal` stays lower-case in our voice and is read as the corpus has it in 1 Kings 18:21, Jesus confessed as the Lamb of Yahweh (John 1:29).

## Age levels

- **Child** — reframed entirely off romance and onto being **picked last for a team**. Jesus was the stone the builders threw away and it did not make Him worth less; you were picked before there was a world; and the courage part — being left out is not permission to stop being kind (Ephesians 4:32). Bedtime verse: Psalm 27:10. Gated in the test against romantic vocabulary.
- **Teen** — the exact vocabulary of the experience (being an option, being on read, the eleven-o'clock text), then the five things the Word does with it, then the honest part: someone thinking carefully **before** a commitment is not disrespecting you. Ends on Jeremiah 17:5,7 — do not make a person's yes your god and their no your verdict.
- **Senior** — the full weight, plus the teaching burden: agreement where Scripture agrees, correction where the flesh has run past it, and the four words to teach the young.
