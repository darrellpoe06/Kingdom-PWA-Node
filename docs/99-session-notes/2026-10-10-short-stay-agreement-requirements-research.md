## Summary — before taking a direct booking

- [ ] **Illinois HOOT:** register a Hotel Operators' Occupation Tax account on MyTax Illinois. File Form **RHM-1** for every stay under 30 consecutive days that is booked directly.
- [ ] **City of Champaign hotel/motel tax (7%):** file the city's *Hotel and Motel Tax Business Registration Form*, then send the monthly remittance to the Finance Department (217-403-8940).
- [ ] **Champaign rental registry:** register the building, including the short-term unit, between **Jan 1 and Mar 1, 2027**. Name a local agent within 45 miles. There is no fee.
- [ ] **Zoning:** get written confirmation from Champaign Planning & Development that renting a whole unit to transient guests is allowed in this zoning district (**UNVERIFIED**, see below).
- [ ] **Smoke alarm** within 15 ft of every sleeping room (the Act names "hotel" and "tourist home"). **CO alarm** within 15 ft of every sleeping room. **Portable alarm for deaf and hard-of-hearing guests** kept available.
- [ ] **No camera or audio device indoors.** Disclose any exterior camera and where it is before booking. Never record audio.
- [ ] **Book at most 29 nights.** A stay of 30 or more nights goes on a standard lease instead.
- [ ] **Guest ID:** keep collection minimal and delete it on schedule. **No face-matching.**
- [ ] **Screening:** apply the same written rules to every guest. Do not use protected traits.

## 1. City of Champaign rules for short-term rentals

- **No short-term-rental ordinance found.** Searches of champaignil.gov and the Champaign Municode library found no short-term-rental license, permit, or definition. Third-party sites claim a "Short-Term Rental Registration Certificate" with an annual fee. That claim has no official source, so treat it as **false until the city confirms it**. In 2018, WGLT reported that city staff were *exploring* short-term-rental licensing (https://www.wglt.org/show/wglts-sound-ideas/2018-07-03/as-local-airbnb-scene-thrives-hotels-want-regulation). No follow-up ordinance was found.
- **Rental registry (new, applies here).** The City Council passed it unanimously on April 21, 2026.
  - It covers buildings with one or more rental units, **"including short-term rentals."** Hotels are exempt.
  - Owners must list contact details for themselves and for a 24-hour local agent within **45 miles** of the property.
  - Registration runs **Jan 1 – Mar 1, 2027**. There is no fee in 2026 or 2027. Fines apply after notice from the city.
  - The city says it will not inspect units or collect lease information under this registry.
  - Sources: https://engagechampaign.org/rental-registration-commercial-vacant-building-ordinance and https://www.wcia.com/news/champaign-county/champaign-city-council-passes-bill-to-establish-registry-of-rental-properties-vacant-commercial-buildings/
  - **Not found:** the ordinance number and its code section.
- **Inspections.** No routine inspection program for individual units was found.
  - Inspections are **complaint-driven** (tenant request) under the city's Property Maintenance Code and International Fire Code (https://champaignil.gov/neighborhood-services/neighborhood-code-compliance/tenant-inspection-form/).
  - Multi-family **common areas** are inspected on a schedule under a program running since 2005 (https://champaignil.gov/neighborhood-services/neighborhood-code-compliance/multi-family-common-area-inspections/).
  - The Property Maintenance Code is Municode Chapter 22.
- **Zoning.** Champaign Zoning (Ch. 37) defines a **bed and breakfast** as an *owner-occupied single-family detached* dwelling with at most 3 rooms rented, at most 7-day stays, and no cooking facilities in rented rooms (https://library.municode.com/il/champaign/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=MUCO_CH37ZO_ARTIIDE_S37-16DE; §37-248). Apt 2 is a whole apartment in a multi-unit building, so **it does not fit the B&B definition**. No zoning definition of short-term rental was found. Whether whole-unit transient rental is allowed in this district is **UNVERIFIED**; ask Planning.
- **Occupancy limits** (unrelated-persons cap, sleeping-room size) and **egress** rules for this unit: **UNVERIFIED.** They come from Chapter 22 and Chapter 37. Use the lower of the code limit and 2 guests per bedroom.
- **Safety (state law)** — https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/ILCS/Articles?ActID=1640&ChapterID=38 and https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=2786&ChapterID=39
  - **Smoke Detector Act (425 ILCS 60/3).** Every "dwelling unit or hotel" needs an approved smoke detector within 15 ft of each sleeping room. The Act's definition of "hotel" includes tourist homes. A hotel must keep portable alarms for deaf and hard-of-hearing guests. Willful failure to install or maintain a detector is a Class B misdemeanor.
  - **Carbon Monoxide Alarm Detector Act (430 ILCS 135).** Every dwelling unit needs a CO alarm within 15 ft of each sleeping room. Install one regardless.

## 2. Taxes

| Tax | Rate | Direct booking (this app) | Airbnb/platform booking |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IL Hotel Operators' Occupation Tax** (35 ILCS 145/3) | 5% + 1% of **94%** of gross rental receipts | Poe Properties registers (MyTax Illinois), collects, files **RHM-1** | Platform is the operator / marketplace facilitator (see below) |
| **City of Champaign Hotel & Motel Privilege Tax** (Code Ch. 32, Art. VI) | **7%** (city lists 13% total = 6% state + 7% city) | Register with City Finance; remit monthly direct to City | Airbnb states it collects Champaign's 7% |
| **Champaign County** | **None found** for property inside the city | — | — |

- **State tax (HOOT).**
  - It applies to rooms rented for **fewer than 30 consecutive days**. "Permanent guests" (30 or more consecutive days) are excluded. Sources: RHM-1 instructions (https://tax.illinois.gov/forms/excise/hoteltaxes/rhm-1-instr.html) and Pub. 106 (https://tax.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/tax/research/publications/pubs/documents/pub-106.pdf).
  - **Platforms:**
    - Since July 1, 2025, short-term-rental hosting platforms count as "re-renters" (Bulletin FY 2025-28, https://tax.illinois.gov/research/publications/bulletins/fy-2025-28.html).
    - From **July 1, 2026**, a hotel marketplace facilitator that meets the $100,000 threshold is treated as the operator and pays the tax, and the host does not owe it on those bookings (Bulletin FY 2026-33, https://tax.illinois.gov/research/publications/bulletins/fy-2026-33.html; P.A. 104-0468).
  - **Direct bookings get no such pass.** Poe Properties is the operator and owes the tax.
  - Keep taxable and non-taxable receipts in **separate records**, as the Act requires.
- **City tax.**
  - The 7% rate comes from https://champaignil.gov/planning/business-portal/starting-your-business/.
  - Forms and payment run through https://champaignil.gov/finance/payments/tax-remittance/ (Citizen Self Service portal). Call Finance first to get an account.
  - A 2012 city notice asked filers to attach a copy of their state RHM-1. Whether that still applies is **UNVERIFIED**.
  - Airbnb's statement that it collects the city tax comes from https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2303. That page is old; check the listing's Taxes tab.

## 3. Enforceability of the agreement

- **E-signatures are valid.**
  - Illinois adopted the **Uniform Electronic Transactions Act, 815 ILCS 333**, in 2021 (SB 2176). It **repealed** the Electronic Commerce Security Act (§20.87): https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/ILCS/Articles?ActID=4165&ChapterID=67
  - Under UETA, a signature cannot be denied legal effect just because it is electronic. A signature is any "sound, symbol, or process… adopted… with the intent to sign." The app's typed name, attestation, consent to sign electronically, and timestamp fit that definition.
  - Federal **E-SIGN** (15 U.S.C. §7001) also applies.
  - Keep a tamper-evident copy of exactly what the guest saw and signed.
- **Guest vs. tenant.**
  - Illinois eviction law (735 ILCS 5/Art. IX) covers tenancies. No statute or case was found that draws a bright line between a hotel guest and a tenant at 30 days. **UNVERIFIED.**
  - The 30-day mark is real in tax law ("permanent guest").
  - **Practice:** limit direct bookings to 29 nights, call the guest a "licensee/guest" (not a "tenant"), and give no exclusive right to the home beyond the dates booked. For 30 nights or more, use the standard residential lease.
- **Innkeeper Protection Act (740 ILCS 90).**
  - It limits a hotel's liability for guest valuables ($250/$500 caps; safe and posted-notice conditions).
  - P.A. 103-0147 added §§10–11: refusing or removing guests for damage, non-payment, disturbance, or underage drinking. A removed guest must get a refund of prepaid unused nights, and these sections cannot be used as a pretext to discriminate (https://ilga.gov/Legislation/ILCS/Articles?ActID=2038&ChapterID=57).
  - Search results say the Act's "hotel" definition requires **25 or more rooms**, which would leave a single unit outside it. **UNVERIFIED.** Write the house-rules removal clause into the contract rather than relying on the Act.
- **Cameras and recording.**
  - **Audio:** recording a private conversation without the consent of **all parties** is a felony (720 ILCS 5/14-2: https://www.ilga.gov/documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/072000050K14-2.htm). **Never record audio.**
  - **Video:** **720 ILCS 5/26-4** makes it a crime to record or stream someone without consent in a restroom, changing room, or **"hotel bedroom,"** or to place a device there (http://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=072000050K26-4).
  - **Industry reference:** since April 30, 2024, Airbnb bans indoor cameras and requires disclosure of exterior cameras and their location before booking (https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3061). Decibel monitors that do not record audio are allowed if disclosed.

## What the agreement must contain (checklist)

- [ ] Parties: Poe Properties (legal name, contact) and the lead guest (legal name, phone, email). List adult guests by name.
- [ ] Property address; the unit only; any shared or excluded areas.
- [ ] Dates and nights; check-in/check-out times; late-checkout fee.
- [ ] Rate ($150/night). Taxes listed separately (state HOOT and city 7%). Cleaning or other fees. Total. When payment is due.
- [ ] Damage deposit: amount, what it covers, and a deadline to return it with an itemized list (see §5).
- [ ] Cancellation and refund schedule. Refund of unused prepaid nights if the guest is removed.
- [ ] Maximum occupancy (number of guests and overnight visitors). Minimum age of the lead guest.
- [ ] House rules: no parties or events; quiet hours; no smoking or vaping (with a cleaning fee); pets (yes or no, and fee); parking; trash.
- [ ] Removal clause: damage, non-payment, disturbance, over-occupancy, or illegal activity ends the stay.
- [ ] Damage liability. Lost keys and lockout fees. No liability for guests' valuables beyond what the law requires.
- [ ] Owner entry rights: emergencies, and repairs with reasonable notice.
- [ ] **Device disclosure:** "No cameras or audio-recording devices inside the unit." Exterior cameras and their locations listed. Noise monitor disclosed if one is used.
- [ ] Safety: where the smoke and CO alarms are; emergency exits; emergency contact (the local agent).
- [ ] Term limit: "This is a transient occupancy, not a lease; maximum 29 nights."
- [ ] Governing law (Illinois) and venue (Champaign County).
- [ ] Consent to sign electronically; typed signature; timestamp; a copy delivered to the guest.

## 4. Guest verification — minimal lawful set

No Illinois or Champaign law **requires** a host to check guest ID. Checking ID is common practice for safety and payment fraud.

- [ ] Lead guest's legal name matches the name on the payment method.
- [ ] Lead guest is **at least 21** (a business rule that supports the no-underage-drinking rule; 18 is the legal floor to contract). Check it once, by **looking at** a government photo ID.
- [ ] If an ID image is kept: store it encrypted, limit access to the family, and **delete it within 30 days after checkout**. Never collect a Social Security number.
- [ ] **No face-matching or "selfie vs. ID" tools.**
  - Photos are excluded from BIPA. A **scan of face geometry** is a biometric identifier.
  - Using one requires a written public retention policy, a written release, and destruction of the data (740 ILCS 14/10, /15: https://ilga.gov/Legislation/ILCS/Articles?ActID=3004&ChapterID=57).
  - Skipping biometrics avoids BIPA entirely.
- [ ] **PIPA (815 ILCS 530):** a name plus a driver's license or state ID number is "personal information."
  - Poe Properties must keep **reasonable security** (§45).
  - After a breach, it must notify affected guests without unreasonable delay, and the Attorney General if more than 500 residents are affected (§10).
  - Source: https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/ILCS/Articles?ActID=2702&ChapterID=67
- [ ] **Fair treatment:** write the screening rules down and apply them to everyone.
  - **Illinois Human Rights Act §5-101** covers lodging as a public accommodation. It exempts a building with **5 or fewer units that the proprietor lives in**.
  - **Federal Title II** (42 U.S.C. §2000a(b)(1)) has a similar exemption for 5 or fewer rooms when the owner lives there.
  - Poe Properties does not live at this building, so **assume both laws apply**.
  - Champaign's Human Rights Ordinance also covers public accommodation, with broader protected classes that include source of income, arrest/conviction record, and sexual orientation (https://champaignil.gov/equity-and-engagement/diversity-equity-inclusion-division/human-rights-ordinance-complaints/).

## 5. Security deposits on short stays

- The **Security Deposit Return Act (765 ILCS 710)** applies to a *lessor* of residential property; several versions of the text say "containing 5 or more units." Under it, the owner must give an itemized damage statement within 30 days, or return the deposit within 45 days; bad faith exposes the owner to 2× the deposit (https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/ILCS/Articles?ActID=2202&ChapterID=62).
  - Whether the 5-unit threshold is still current is **UNVERIFIED**.
  - Whether the Act reaches a transient guest (a licensee, not a tenant) is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Safe practice:** treat the deposit as if the Act applies. Send photos and an itemized statement within 14 days and return the balance within 30 days. The app can do this automatically.
- No Champaign deposit ordinance for short stays was found.
