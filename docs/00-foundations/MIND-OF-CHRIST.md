# Mind of Christ
> *"For who has understood the mind of the Lord so as to instruct him? But we have the mind of Christ."* — 1 Corinthians 2:16 (ESV)
## What This Document Is
This is a foundational layer of SKOS — sitting under every module and every practice. It defines how a person of The Way stewards the mind: what fills it, what's allowed to remain, what gets evicted, and what gets amplified.
This is mental stewardship as discipleship. Not self-help. Not pop-psychology. The disciplined renewing of the mind that scripture commands.
## The Identity Claim First
Before we discuss discipline, we name the identity:
**ESV — 1 Corinthians 2:16:** *"For who has understood the mind of the Lord so as to instruct him? But we have the mind of Christ."*
**KJV — 1 Corinthians 2:16:** *"For who hath known the mind of the Lord, that he may instruct him? But we have the mind of Christ."*
**AMP — 1 Corinthians 2:16:** *"But we have the mind of Christ [to be guided by His thoughts and purposes]."*
> Greek: *nous Christou* (νοῦν Χριστοῦ) — Strong's G3563 (mind, understanding) + G5547 (Christ). Possession, not aspiration. Already given.
Mental discipline doesn't *create* the mind of Christ. It *yields* to what's already been given. The believer doesn't strain to manufacture Christ-like thinking; the believer removes what blocks it.

## The Sequence: NOTICE → TEST → CAPTURE → REDIRECT
When a thought arises (any thought, any time, any source), it goes through four steps. This is the operational form of mental stewardship.
### NOTICE
Become aware that a thought has arisen. This is metacognition — thinking about your thinking. Many thoughts run undetected; the first discipline is detection itself.
**Practice:** Pause throughout the day. Ask: *What am I thinking right now? Where did it come from?* The Holy Spirit will quicken awareness as this practice deepens.
### TEST
Run the thought through the Philippians 4:8 filter. Eight questions.
**For adults:**
1. **Is it TRUE?** — *alēthēs* (G227). Factually accurate. Not distortion, exaggeration, or assumption posing as fact.
2. **Is it HONORABLE?** — *semnos* (G4586). Dignified. Worthy of respect. Not petty, mocking, or beneath who I am in Christ.
3. **Is it JUST?** — *dikaios* (G1342). Righteous by God's standard. Not by my preference or convenience.
4. **Is it PURE?** — *hagnos* (G53). Clean. Uncontaminated by lust, envy, malice, or bitterness.
5. **Is it LOVELY?** — *prosphilēs* (G4375). Beautiful. Attracts the heart toward good rather than darkness.
6. **Is it COMMENDABLE?** — *euphēmos* (G2163). Would I say this out loud to someone I respect? Good-sounding, not gossip or slander.
7. **Is it EXCELLENT?** — *aretē* (G703). Virtuous. The best version of thinking, not the lazy version.
8. **Is it PRAISEWORTHY?** — *epainos* (G1868). Does it deserve to be amplified and grown, or does it deserve to die in this moment?
**For children** (Christian, Christyn, Christiana, and any child being raised in The Way):
1. Is it real, or am I making it up?
2. Would I be proud to think this in front of Yahweh?
3. Is this fair? Does it match what God says is right?
4. Is this clean, or is it dirty with bad stuff?
5. Does this turn my heart toward good?
6. Would I say this out loud to Mom and Dad?
7. Is this my best thinking, or am I settling for worse?
8. Does this deserve to get bigger in my mind, or smaller?
**Pass all 8 → think on it. Fail any 1 → capture and redirect.**
### CAPTURE
2 Corinthians 10:5 — *"take every thought captive to obey Christ."* The Greek verb is *aichmalōtizō* (G163), and it is military: prisoner of war. The thought is arrested, bound, brought under authority. It is not negotiated with. It is not entertained "just to see." It is captured.
**Practice:** Name the failed thought, out loud or in a journal: *"This thought fails the Test on [criterion]. I capture it in the name of Yahshua."*
### REDIRECT
Replace the captured thought with content that passes the Test. The mind cannot hold a vacuum — emptied of one thought, it will fill with another. Choose the replacement deliberately:
- A scripture (memorized for this purpose)
- A truth statement about God or yourself in Christ
- A prayer
- An act (move the body, do the next right thing)
- A song
**Practice:** Have a "replacement library" ready *before* the moment of need. The middle of a spiraling thought is too late to start memorizing scripture.
## How AI Coaching Fits
There are two legitimate modes for running the Test:
**Mode A — Self-run.** You internalize the eight questions and run them silently as thoughts arise. This is the destination. The goal of discipleship is that the Test runs automatically, in the background, as native cognition.
**Mode B — AI-coached.** The user asks an AI assistant (like Claude) to run the Test on a specific thought, decision, piece of content, or piece of writing. The AI walks through all eight criteria and reports back. Useful when:
- The user is too emotionally close to evaluate clearly
- The user is sleep-deprived or in heat-of-moment
- The user wants a second voice as a check
- The user is teaching a child and wants to model the process out loud
**The discipleship principle:** Mode B trains for Mode A. Mode B is a *coaching scaffold*, not a substitute. Long-term, every SKOS user runs the Test themselves and lives from the mind of Christ that is already theirs.
## Mental Diet
Stewardship isn't only reactive. It's also proactive: what is the mind being fed?
A person of The Way curates inputs the way an athlete curates food:
- What books?
- What podcasts?
- What videos?
- What music?
- What conversations?
- What news?
- What social media?
Each input either passes or fails the Test in aggregate. SKOS does not prescribe a content list (this is between the user and the Holy Spirit), but it prescribes the *practice* of evaluation. Audit your inputs regularly. Cut what fails. Add what feeds.
**ESV — Psalm 101:3:** *"I will not set before my eyes anything that is worthless."*
**KJV — Psalm 101:3:** *"I will set no wicked thing before mine eyes..."*
## Religion AND Relationship in This Foundation
**Religion-side:** The disciplined practice of the Test. The memorization of scripture for redirection. The journaling of captured thoughts. The structure that doesn't drift with mood.
**Relationship-side:** Hearing the Holy Spirit's specific conviction about specific thoughts. The personal, intimate work of God identifying what you alone need to capture. The grace when you fail. The Father's love that doesn't condemn while He convicts.
Both. Always.
