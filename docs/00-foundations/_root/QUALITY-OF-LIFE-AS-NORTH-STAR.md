## The principle

**Every workflow, every feature, every revenue layer evaluates against a single senior question: does this measurably improve quality of life for the family or community using it?**

Not engagement metrics. Not session length. Not feature adoption. Not retention curves. Quality of life — measured across multiple sectors that the family + community themselves define as mattering.

This is the WHY of the platform. Stewardship of finances + family + ministry + body + mind + community + vocation — all in service of abundant life (John 10:10 ESV: *"I came that they may have life and have it abundantly"*).

## The QoL framework

The system surfaces signals across multiple sectors. Each sector is OPT-IN per family. The family chooses what matters to them; the system serves what they chose.

**Sectors (initial set; community defines additions):**

- Financial wellness — debt trajectory, buffer fund, tithe consistency, income stability
- Physical health — sleep, HRV, activity, nutrition (per IoT + biosensors spec)
- Relational health — communication frequency, shared meals, conflict patterns
- Spiritual rhythm — devotion consistency, scripture engagement, prayer + tithe practices
- Mental clarity — stress markers, decision-confidence, anxiety patterns
- Community engagement — church attendance, volunteer hours, mutual-aid participation
- Education + growth — reading time, learning progress, skill milestones
- Vocational + business — work satisfaction, customer feedback, revenue stability
- Environmental — home energy use, food waste, sustainability practices

## The seven rules

### Rule 1 — System is a mirror, never a judge

The platform surfaces patterns the family chose to track. It does not score. It does not compare the family to others. It does not gamify. It does not nag.

The family interprets their own patterns in their own context. The system is the mirror; the family is the discerner.

### Rule 2 — Family + community define what matters

Each family selects which QoL sectors to track + which indicators within each sector matter to them. A family that doesn't track "screen time" doesn't have a "screen time" surface; their dashboard reflects their values, not an imposed standard.

Community-level QoL definitions emerge from the community itself (COLG defines what church-community wellness looks like for them; chef-served families define what nutrition wellness looks like for them; etc.).

### Rule 3 — Opt-in per sector, per family member, per analysis

Nothing tracks by default beyond what's needed for the family's chosen baseline features. Each new sector requires explicit opt-in. Each family member (with age-appropriate process for minors) gets per-sector opt-in. Each new analysis (e.g., "compare your financial decisions to your stress markers") requires explicit opt-in.

Opt-out is one tap, immediate, retroactive (the data already collected can be retained for the family's own use OR purged at their request).

### Rule 4 — Family configurations vary; the platform honors what each family IS

Per Darrell's framing 2026-05-29: families with present fathers, families with absent fathers, single-parent households, separated co-parents, blended families, multi-generational households — all served by the platform without judgment about which configuration is "right."

The existing persona system already accommodates this (Family of 4 / Separated co-parents / etc.); the QoL framework extends the accommodation across every sector.

### Rule 5 — Community-level insights via opt-in aggregation, never extraction

When the family explicitly opts in to community-level research (e.g., "share my anonymized debt-trajectory data with COLG family wellness research"), data flows ONE TIME, ANONYMIZED, FOR A NAMED STUDY, with results RETURNING TO THE COMMUNITY.

Aggregation is never automatic. Aggregation is never for vendor benefit. Aggregation is research-quality serving the community itself, governed by the community.

### Rule 6 — QoL measurement is faith-grounded, not secular metric-chasing

Per THE-WAY + MIND-OF-CHRIST + EXCELLENCE-STANDARD: the QoL framework is grounded in the family's actual values. A family whose stated value is "tithe consistently" tracks tithe and celebrates consistency. A family whose stated value is "family meals 5+ nights/week" tracks shared meals and celebrates achievement.

The platform does NOT propose secular wellness frames that contradict the family's faith framework. The platform DOES help the family see their faith commitments producing measurable outcomes (sound-minded living per 2 Tim 1:7 KJV).

### Rule 7 — QoL is the senior evaluation question for every product decision

When evaluating whether to ship a feature, run a workflow, hire a team member, take an opportunity — the senior question is "does this measurably improve quality of life for the families and communities we serve?" Per BUSINESS-PROCESS-CONNECTIONS, this question is THE other end of every connection.

A feature that increases engagement but doesn't improve QoL is rejected. A revenue layer that increases ARR but extracts from family wellness is rejected. A partnership that increases reach but compromises QoL alignment is rejected.

## How this applies operationally

- **Pre-merge check (added to Quality Gatekeeper Role 10):** Does this PR's change to a visible surface improve QoL for the family/community using it? If not — what is it improving? If the answer is "engagement" or "retention" without underlying QoL improvement, the gate refuses merge.
- **Quarterly QoL review (Governor cadence):** Darrell + Christina review the platform's overall QoL impact each quarter. Sectors trending up; sectors trending down; sectors the family + community asked for that haven't shipped yet; sectors no one's using that should be retired.
- **Family-voice channel for QoL feedback:** workflow 30 (family feedback intake) gets a `qol-impact` tag for feedback specifically about whether the platform is improving the family's life. Higher priority than other feedback types.
