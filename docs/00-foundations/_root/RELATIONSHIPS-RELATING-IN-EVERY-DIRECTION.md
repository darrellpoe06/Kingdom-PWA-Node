## 1. Why this exists (his words, rendered for meaning, DR-0331)

The Relationships shelf was first filled by searching titles and keywords, and came back with a few dozen lessons. Darrell said that made no sense: out of hundreds of Living Lessons about life, almost every one is about how a person relates to something. He asked that relationships be understood across the whole range of emotions, and through the fruit of the Spirit (love, joy, peace, and the rest): how we, as humans, relate in general. Not only how one person treats another, and not only one man and one woman, but the relationship to oneself (the inner relationship) and then the outer ones. Understand how relating works, rigorously, so that a quality relationship with one person later has something to stand on. Word first, then research.

### 1b. His second word the same day: everything we touch, and the mirror

Later the same day Darrell widened it (rendered for meaning): what is our relationship with learning, with studying, with governing our mood and doing what is necessary even when we do not feel like it and the situation is hard? With change and transition? With understanding someone else's situation, admitting we could have done better, and letting that move us to do better? With the Word itself, and how it shapes us? With a husband or wife, a child, a mother: what are we doing that benefits each one, and what are we doing that quietly undermines it? Can we see ourselves? That is the mirror the Word is meant to hold up. The king's level is the servant's level: serving everyone, present on purpose, so that they understand and we understand, they grow and we grow. We have a relationship with everything we have a relationship with; the question is how to keep each one quality filled and sustainable. All of it comes from the Word: a tree planted by rivers of water whose fruit lasts into eternity. We are eternal souls in flesh for a vapour of time, given the opportunity to know an eternal King. The Word is what divides soul and spirit, so a person can see the difference and bring soul, body and spirit into line with Him, with nothing subtle undermining it. Faith in the only true Yahweh is how a man wins and goes home; humble and present in His presence is the game, His game, designed by Him. He is the way, the truth and the life; He says come, let us reason together; every other voice, our own included, is a liar beside His truth.

## 2. The definition

**On this platform, a relationship is any direction in which a person gives and receives: to Yahweh, within oneself, and toward every other person. "Relationships" is the study and practice of relating well in every one of those directions, with the full range of human emotion governed by a sound mind, and measured by the fruit of the Spirit.**

Three parts carry the definition: the **directions**, the **range**, and the **measure**.

## 3. The Word first

### 3.1 The order of the directions is given in the commandment itself

**KJV — Matthew 22:37-39:** *"Jesus said unto him, Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind. This is the first and great commandment. And the second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself."*

Three directions are named in one sentence: upward (the Lord thy God), outward (thy neighbour), and inward (*as thyself*). The inward one is the measuring rod of the outward one. A person who has no settled, honest, governed relationship within has a broken ruler for loving anyone else. **KJV — Matthew 22:40:** *"On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets."* Every relationship the Word regulates hangs on these.

### 3.2 Upward is first, and it is first because He loved first

**KJV — 1 John 4:19:** *"We love him, because he first loved us."* **KJV — Romans 5:8:** *"But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us."* The relationship with Yahweh is not earned by relating well to others; it is the source. **KJV — John 15:5:** *"I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing."* The fruit in every other direction grows on that branch.

### 3.3 Upward and outward cannot be separated

**KJV — 1 John 4:20:** *"If a man say, I love God, and hateth his brother, he is a liar: for he that loveth not his brother whom he hath seen, how can he love God whom he hath not seen?"* How a person treats the one in front of them is the visible evidence of the invisible relationship.

### 3.4 Inward: the heart, the spirit, the mind, the body

- The heart is guarded: **KJV — Proverbs 4:23:** *"Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."*
- The spirit is ruled: **KJV — Proverbs 16:32:** *"He that is slow to anger is better than the mighty; and he that ruleth his spirit than he that taketh a city."* Its opposite: **KJV — Proverbs 25:28:** *"He that hath no rule over his own spirit is like a city that is broken down, and without walls."*
- The mind is sound: **KJV — 2 Timothy 1:7:** *"For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind."*
- Thoughts are taken captive and the mind renewed: **KJV — 2 Corinthians 10:5:** *"Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ;"* and **KJV — Romans 12:2:** *"be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind"*. The practice is `MIND-OF-CHRIST.md`.
- Worry is brought to Him and the mind is filled with what is true: **KJV — Philippians 4:6-8:** *"Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus. Finally, brethren, whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of good report; if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things."*
- The body is a temple, not a stranger: **KJV — 1 Corinthians 6:19:** *"What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own?"*
- The self is searched by Him, not left to itself: **KJV — Psalms 139:23:** *"Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts:"* (the heart cannot audit itself alone: **KJV — Jeremiah 17:9:** *"The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?"*).

### 3.5 The range of emotions is human, and Jesus carried it without sin

The Word does not teach the absence of emotion. It teaches emotion governed. **KJV — John 11:35:** *"Jesus wept."* **KJV — Mark 3:5:** *"And when he had looked round about on them with anger, being grieved for the hardness of their hearts..."* **KJV — Ecclesiastes 3:4:** *"A time to weep, and a time to laugh; a time to mourn, and a time to dance;"* **KJV — Psalms 42:5:** *"Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted in me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him for the help of his countenance."* (the psalmist speaking to his own soul is the inner relationship in action). **KJV — Hebrews 4:15:** *"For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin."*

Anger has a boundary and a clock: **KJV — Ephesians 4:26:** *"Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath:"* and a destination: **KJV — Ephesians 4:31-32:** *"Let all bitterness, and wrath, and anger, and clamour, and evil speaking, be put away from you, with all malice: And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you."* The order of the outward exchange: **KJV — James 1:19-20:** *"Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath: For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God."* Fear is answered by love: **KJV — 1 John 4:18:** *"There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear: because fear hath torment. He that feareth is not made perfect in love."*

### 3.6 Outward: every person, to the edge

Spouse (**KJV — Genesis 2:18:** *"It is not good that the man should be alone"*; **KJV — Ephesians 5:25:** *"Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;"*), children (**KJV — Ephesians 6:4**), friends (**KJV — Proverbs 18:24:** *"A man that hath friends must shew himself friendly"*; **KJV — Proverbs 27:17**), the church (**KJV — Hebrews 10:24:** *"And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works:"*), the neighbour and the stranger (the Samaritan, **KJV — Luke 10:36-37:** *"He that shewed mercy on him. Then said Jesus unto him, Go, and do thou likewise."*), and the enemy (**KJV — Matthew 5:44:** *"Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you"*). The limit is set by the Word, not by mood: **KJV — Romans 12:18:** *"If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men."* Our part is what lies in us; their part is theirs.

### 3.7 The measure: the fruit of the Spirit

**KJV — Galatians 5:22-23:** *"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance: against such there is no law."*

Every direction is measured by the same nine. They are one fruit (singular), and they are relational: love, joy and peace are what a person holds; longsuffering, gentleness and goodness are how a person treats others; faith, meekness and temperance are how a person governs self. The list just before it (**Galatians 5:19-21**: hatred, variance, emulations, wrath, strife, envyings) is the same set of directions gone wrong. Love is senior to all of it (**1 Corinthians 13:1-3**), and its behaviour is specified: **KJV — 1 Corinthians 13:4-7:** *"Charity suffereth long, and is kind; charity envieth not; charity vaunteth not itself, is not puffed up, Doth not behave itself unseemly, seeketh not her own, is not easily provoked, thinketh no evil; Rejoiceth not in iniquity, but rejoiceth in the truth; Beareth all things, believeth all things, hopeth all things, endureth all things."*

**Why inward comes before outward in the practice (not in worth):** the fruit grows from inside out (**Proverbs 4:23**: the issues of life come *out of* the heart; **Matthew 7:5**: *"first cast out the beam out of thine own eye; and then shalt thou see clearly"*). A person cannot give a peace they do not hold, or a gentleness they never show themselves when they fail. That is what Darrell meant: understand relating, inside first, so the one relationship later can be full.

## 4. The research tier (DR-0100: established fact stated plainly; DR-0098: the Word is the authority)

What follows is established research that agrees with what the Word already teaches. It is cited as observation, not as the foundation. Each citation below was looked up on 2026-11-26 (PubMed / publisher / journal records); none is invented.

1. **People need to belong; it is a fundamental motive.** Baumeister, R. F., & Leary, M. R. (1995). The need to belong: Desire for interpersonal attachments as a fundamental human motivation. *Psychological Bulletin, 117*(3), 497–529. doi:10.1037/0033-2909.117.3.497. (Genesis 2:18: *"It is not good that the man should be alone"*.)
2. **Relationships are a matter of life and death.** Holt-Lunstad, J., Smith, T. B., & Layton, J. B. (2010). Social relationships and mortality risk: A meta-analytic review. *PLoS Medicine, 7*(7), e1000316. doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316. 148 studies, 308,849 participants; stronger social relationships carried an odds ratio of 1.50 for survival, comparable with well-established risk factors. (Stated precisely: an odds ratio, not a 50% higher survival rate.)
3. **How a person handles their own emotions shapes their relationships.** Gross, J. J., & John, O. P. (2003). Individual differences in two emotion regulation processes: Implications for affect, relationships, and well-being. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 85*(2), 348–362. doi:10.1037/0022-3514.85.2.348. Habitual reappraisal (rethinking the meaning of what happened) went with better interpersonal functioning and well-being; habitual suppression (hiding the feeling while it still burns) went with worse. This is the inner relationship deciding the outer one (Proverbs 16:32; 2 Corinthians 10:5).
4. **Naming a feeling calms it.** Lieberman, M. D., et al. (2007). Putting feelings into words: Affect labeling disrupts amygdala activity in response to affective stimuli. *Psychological Science, 18*(5), 421–428. doi:10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01916.x. (The psalmist naming his own soul's state, Psalms 42:5.)
5. **Self-rule in childhood predicts the whole adult life.** Moffitt, T. E., et al. (2011). A gradient of childhood self-control predicts health, wealth, and public safety. *PNAS, 108*(7), 2693–2698. doi:10.1073/pnas.1010076108. The Dunedin cohort of 1,000 children followed to age 32, and a 500-sibling-pair comparison; held after social class and IQ were accounted for. (Proverbs 25:28; temperance in Galatians 5:23.)
6. **The first bond is the template for later ones.** Hazan, C., & Shaver, P. R. (1987). Romantic love conceptualized as an attachment process. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 52*(3), 511–524. doi:10.1037/0022-3514.52.3.511. Building on Bowlby (*Attachment and Loss*, Vol. 1, 1969) and Ainsworth et al. (*Patterns of Attachment*, 1978): adult attachment styles occur in roughly the same proportions as infant ones and shape how love is experienced. (Proverbs 22:6; Ephesians 6:4: the family direction forms the marriage direction.)
7. **In marriage, positive affect and accepting influence predict a lasting, happy union.** Gottman, J. M., Coan, J., Carrère, S., & Swanson, C. (1998). Predicting marital happiness and stability from newlywed interactions. *Journal of Marriage and the Family, 60*(1), 5–22. (Proverbs 15:1; James 1:19; Ephesians 5:21.)

**Not verified, and therefore not claimed:** the full texts of items 3 and 7 were not read (abstracts and catalog records only), so no effect sizes are quoted from them. The Bowlby (1969) and Ainsworth et al. (1978) books are cited by their standard bibliographic record, not re-read. The press figure that complex social networks "raise survival 91%" (reported in popular coverage of item 2) is not used: the paper reports OR = 1.91 for complex measures of integration, an odds ratio. `re-review: 2027-02-26` for any added claim.

**Where research over-reaches, the Word corrects that claim** (DR-0100 tier 3): research that treats the self as the final authority ("your truth," "protect your peace" as permission to stop loving) does not overrule Matthew 22:39 or Romans 12:18. The data on self-regulation stands; the conclusion that the self is the centre does not.

## 5. The inclusion criteria for the Relationships shelf

A lesson is on the shelf when **its main teaching (its big idea and the body of the lesson, read in full, not its keywords)** teaches a person how to relate in at least one of these six directions, in a way the fruit of the Spirit could measure:

| Group | It belongs when the lesson's subject is... |
|---|---|
| **1. With Yahweh** | the relationship itself: being loved first, abiding, trust, knowing and being known by Him, coming to Him as Father, and the relationship with the Word itself (how it reads us, divides soul and spirit, and shapes us). (Not every doctrine lesson: a lesson about His attributes, prophecy or history belongs only when it teaches the reader to relate to Him.) |
| **2. Within yourself and your emotions** | the inner relationship: the heart, thoughts, a sound mind, fear, anxiety, anger, grief, shame, identity, self-rule, the body as temple, rest; and (his second word) a person's relationship with learning and study, with governing mood and doing what is necessary when it is hard, with change and transition, and with seeing oneself in the Word's mirror and doing better. |
| **3. Marriage** | husband and wife, covenant, courtship toward marriage, singleness as it prepares for it. |
| **4. Family and children** | parents and children, siblings, generations, the home, inheritance of character. |
| **5. Friendship, church and neighbour** | friends, the body of believers, neighbour, stranger, enemy, how we serve and honour people, understanding another person's situation, the servant who leads. |
| **6. Conflict and forgiveness** | the tongue, hard conversations, anger between people, forgiveness, repair, reconciliation, vengeance left to Him. |

**Excluded, with the reason recorded in the audit:** a lesson that only mentions a spouse, a friend or a feeling in passing; a lesson whose subject is money, business, property, technology, history or doctrine where relating is incidental (even if it is a good lesson); and "relationship" in a commercial sense (tenant, customer, vendor) unless the lesson's subject is how to treat the person.

**The audit is committed** at `docs/99-session-notes/2026-11-26-relationships-shelf-audit.md`, one line per included lesson with its group and reason, and the near-misses with why they were left off. A test (`app/src/__tests__/relationships-shelf-audit.test.js`) fails the build if any shelf id does not resolve to a mounted lesson, or is not in the audit with a reason, so the shelf can never again drift from this definition silently.
