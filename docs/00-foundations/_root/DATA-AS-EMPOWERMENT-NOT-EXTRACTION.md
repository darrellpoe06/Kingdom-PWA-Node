## Why this is structural, not marketing

Most "we respect your privacy" claims from tech companies are PR. The structural reality of PoeTech's data-as-empowerment is built in by architecture:

### Commitment 1 — Architecturally sovereign

Data lives on the family's NAS. PoeTech does not run a cloud. There is no central database of family data anywhere except the family's own infrastructure.

A vendor that can't extract data because it doesn't have access to data is structurally different from a vendor that promises not to extract data they have full access to.

### Commitment 2 — Open-source core

The core platform code is open-source. Any audit can verify what the platform does and does not do with data. Customers + community members + researchers + journalists can inspect.

A platform whose behavior is publicly auditable is structurally different from a platform whose behavior is verified only by the vendor's word.

### Commitment 3 — Exportable always

Every data record is exportable in standard formats. The family can leave the platform at any time and take their data with them. PoeTech retains nothing after they leave.

A platform that respects the right to leave is structurally different from a platform that depends on lock-in to retain customers.

### Commitment 4 — No advertising business model

PoeTech earns from subscription + services + hardware partnerships. PoeTech does NOT earn from advertising. PoeTech does NOT earn from data resale. PoeTech does NOT earn from user-behavior-based pricing.

A platform whose revenue comes from serving customers is structurally different from a platform whose revenue comes from monetizing customer attention.

### Commitment 5 — No engagement optimization

Features are designed to be USEFUL, not ADDICTIVE. Sessions are short by design. There is no "infinite scroll." There are no "streak" gamification mechanics designed to manipulate behavior. There are no "people you may know" patterns designed to maximize network growth at the cost of user wellbeing. There is no algorithmic feed designed to maximize time-on-app.

The platform is a TOOL, not a casino. Tools serve their users; casinos extract from them.

A platform that doesn't optimize for engagement is structurally different from a platform whose KPIs reward attention capture.

## The eight binding behaviors

For every data-touching feature decision, these eight behaviors are non-negotiable:

### Behavior 1 — Family is the owner of every record about them

The family's data is the family's. The platform processes it under license. The license is revocable. The license never extends to third-party transfer without explicit per-event consent.

### Behavior 2 — Opt-in per data stream, per family member, per analysis

Nothing tracks by default beyond what's required for the family's chosen baseline features. New tracking requires explicit opt-in per family member. New analyses (e.g., correlating financial decisions with wellness markers) require explicit opt-in.

### Behavior 3 — Per-family-member minor protections

Children's data has additional protections: parental consent + age-appropriate child assent, no external surfaces acting on minor data, additional retention controls. Per the IoT + biosensors spec + the user_family memory.

### Behavior 4 — No insurance company, employer, or advertiser access — ever

There is no path in the workflows for family health, financial, or behavioral data to flow to insurance companies, employers, advertisers, or other commercial third parties. Not aggregated. Not anonymized. Not "for product improvement." Not via any business partnership. Structurally impossible.

### Behavior 5 — Aggregation requires explicit per-study opt-in

Community-level insights are only generated when families EXPLICITLY opt in to a named study for a named purpose with results returning to the community. Aggregation is never automatic. Aggregation is never for vendor benefit.

### Behavior 6 — Audit log on every data access

Every access to family data — by family members, by family-approved doctors, by anyone — writes to an audit log. Family can review who accessed what when. Per IDENTITY-ROLES-AUDIT foundation.

### Behavior 7 — Data deletion is immediate and verifiable

When the family deletes data, it's deleted. From bind-mounted storage. From backups (within the next backup cycle). From any cached views. The family can verify deletion. There are no "soft delete" patterns that retain data after the family has asked for it gone.

### Behavior 8 — Family voice governs all data-touching changes

Per BUSINESS-PROCESS-CONNECTIONS Family-Voice-Is-The-Connection extension: any new data-touching feature passes the five-question test with the family's voice included. Quality Gatekeeper (Role 10) enforces.

## The five anti-patterns that don't ship — ever

The platform NEVER ships:

### Anti-pattern 1 — Dark UX patterns

Designs intended to manipulate users into actions they wouldn't take with full information. Hidden costs, obscured cancel buttons, forced binary choices, "trick questions" in flow design, urgency manipulation, fake social proof.

### Anti-pattern 2 — Engagement maximization

Features whose purpose is to increase time-on-app for its own sake. Infinite scroll. Algorithmic feeds. Streak gamification. Notification spam. "Pull-to-refresh" rewards. Loss-aversion framing of optional features.

### Anti-pattern 3 — Surveillance disguised as service

Data collection beyond what's needed for the user's chosen features. "Helpful suggestions" that require pervasive tracking. Location collection when location isn't relevant to the feature. Contact list scraping. Browsing history collection.

### Anti-pattern 4 — Data lock-in

Features designed to make leaving the platform harder. Proprietary export formats. Missing export options. Re-import friction. Data hostages disguised as "premium" tiers.

### Anti-pattern 5 — Consent fatigue

"By using this you agree to..." that hides material changes in long legal documents. Privacy policy updates that change practices without explicit re-consent. Pre-checked opt-in boxes. Deceptive consent flows.
