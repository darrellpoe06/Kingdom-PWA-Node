### Entry: The Parable of the Talents, Stewardship, and "His Opinion IS Wealth"

The binding line of this whole Study: **Yahweh's opinion IS wealth — money or not.** What He thinks, what He says, what He values is the true treasure. Money is the stewardship of *lesser* things; the Word is the treasure itself.

The **Parable of the Talents** (Matthew 25:14–30) frames it: each servant is entrusted with something belonging to the master, and is measured not by how much he started with but by what he *did* with what he was given. Stewardship — faithful use of what is entrusted — is the test. The servant who buried his talent out of fear lost it; the ones who put it to work were called *good and faithful*. Money is one of the lesser talents entrusted to us; the greater treasure entrusted is the Word and the relationship with Yahweh.

**The treasure that does not rust:**

**ESV — Psalm 119:72:** *"The law of your mouth is better to me than thousands of gold and silver pieces."*

**ESV — Proverbs 8:11:** *"for wisdom is better than jewels, and all that you may desire cannot compare with her."*

**ESV — Matthew 6:19–21:** *"Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy and where thieves break in and steal, but lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust destroys and where thieves do not break in and steal. For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also."*

The point lands in verse 21: where the treasure is, the heart follows. If Yahweh's opinion is what we treasure, our heart is anchored in Him — and *that* is wealth, whether or not there is money. Money, by contrast, is a talent to be stewarded faithfully (Matthew 25), never the treasure itself.

**Supporting references:** Matthew 25:14–30 (the talents); 1 Corinthians 4:2 (it is required of stewards that they be found faithful); Luke 16:10–11 (faithful in little → trusted with much; money as the *lesser*, true riches as the greater).

### Entry: Joy vs. Happiness — Broken-and-Healed in the Wilderness, the Table and the Footstool

We drew the line between **happiness** (which depends on *happenings* — circumstance, and so it rises and falls) and **joy** (which is sourced in Yahweh and so holds even when circumstances do not). Joy is not the denial of the wilderness; it is the strength carried *through* it. We spoke of being **broken and healed in the wilderness** — that the dry place is where the breaking and the healing both happen, and that the joy of the Lord is the strength that carries a person across it.

**Joy is strength, and it does not depend on circumstance:**

**ESV — Nehemiah 8:10:** *"Then he said to them, 'Go your way. Eat the fat and drink sweet wine and send portions to anyone who has nothing ready, for this day is holy to our Lord. And do not be grieved, for the joy of the Lord is your strength.'"*

**ESV — 1 Peter 1:8:** *"Though you have not seen him, you love him. Though you do not now see him, you believe in him and rejoice with joy that is inexpressible and filled with glory,"*

**ESV — James 1:2:** *"Count it all joy, my brothers, when you meet trials of various kinds,"*

**Joy that stands even when everything fails — the wilderness verse:**

**ESV — Habakkuk 3:17–18:** *"Though the fig tree should not blossom, nor fruit be on the vines, the produce of the olive fail and the fields yield no food, the flock be cut off from the fold and there be no herd in the stalls, yet I will rejoice in the Lord; I will take joy in the God of my salvation."*

Habakkuk names every circumstantial happiness failing at once — no blossom, no fruit, no oil, no food, no flock, no herd — and then turns on the word *yet*. That *yet* is the difference between joy and happiness.

**The table and the footstool — provision in the presence of enemies, and the enemies already underfoot:**

**ESV — Psalm 23:5:** *"You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies; you anoint my head with oil; my cup overflows."*

**ESV — Psalm 110:1:** *"The Lord says to my Lord: 'Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies your footstool.'"*

The table is set *in the presence of* the enemies — not after they are gone — and the footstool is the picture of those enemies already placed under the feet of the King at Yahweh's right hand. Joy sits at that table.

### Entry: The Eternal Research → Plan → Execute Pattern

We saw that **Research → Plan → Execute** is not merely a good engineering habit — it is an *eternal* pattern, visible in how Yahweh Himself works and in how He instructed His people to work. PoeTech's build discipline (research first, plan plainly, then execute) is an echo of this pattern, not an invention of it.

**Build to the revealed pattern — the plan is given before the work (research → the shown pattern):**

**ESV — Exodus 25:40:** *"And see that you make them after the pattern for them, which is being shown you on the mountain."*

**ESV — Hebrews 8:5:** *"They serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly things. For when Moses was about to erect the tent, he was instructed by God, saying, 'See that you make everything according to the pattern that was shown you on the mountain.'"*

**Count the cost before building — plan:**

**ESV — Luke 14:28:** *"For which of you, desiring to build a tower, does not first sit down and count the cost, whether he has enough to complete it?"*

**Write the vision plainly so it can be run — the plan made executable:**

**ESV — Habakkuk 2:2:** *"Write the vision; make it plain on tablets, so he may run who reads it."*

**The original execution — Genesis as the first Research → Plan → Execute:**

**ESV — Genesis 1:1:** *"In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth."*

Genesis 1 is the pattern in full: Yahweh speaks (the declared plan), it is so (execution), and He *sees that it is good* (the review/verification step) — repeated day by day. The whole creation account is plan → execute → verify, in order, with nothing skipped. Our build discipline is downstream of this.

**Supporting references:** Genesis 1 (the day-by-day "and God saw that it was good" review loop); Proverbs 24:27 (prepare the work outside, then build the house — sequence matters).

### Entry: The Sword of the Spirit — Clear Vision and a Weapon; Metanoia and the Mind of Christ

We spoke of the Word as **both clear vision and a weapon** — the Sword of the Spirit. It is the one offensive piece of the armor, and it is also living and discerning: it shows you what is true (vision) and it cuts (weapon). And we tied this to **metanoia** — the renewing, the change of mind — and to having **the mind of Christ**: the Word does its dividing work in the mind, and the mind is what gets renewed.

**The Word is the sword:**

**ESV — Ephesians 6:17:** *"and take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God,"*

**ESV — Hebrews 4:12:** *"For the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing to the division of soul and of spirit, of joints and of marrow, and discerning the thoughts and intentions of the heart."*

**The weapons of the warfare are not fleshly — they take thoughts captive:**

**ESV — 2 Corinthians 10:4–5:** *"For the weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh but have divine power to destroy strongholds. We destroy arguments and every lofty opinion raised against the knowledge of God, and take every thought captive to obey Christ,"*

**Metanoia — the renewed mind:**

**ESV — Romans 12:2:** *"Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind, that by testing you may discern what is the will of God, what is good and acceptable and perfect."*

> Greek: *metanoia* (G3341) — a change of mind, a turning. Not mere regret; a reordering of how the mind thinks. Romans 12:2 is metanoia as an ongoing renewal.

**The mind of Christ — the renewed mind's destination:**

**ESV — 1 Corinthians 2:16:** *"For who has understood the mind of the Lord so as to instruct him? But we have the mind of Christ."*

This connects directly to the `MIND-OF-CHRIST.md` foundation and the **NOTICE → TEST → CAPTURE → REDIRECT** sequence: the sword (the Word) is what does the dividing in the TEST step, and the mind of Christ is the identity the whole discipline yields to.

### Entry: The Tree of Good and Evil — "What Fruit Do You Keep Eating?"

We turned the tree of the knowledge of good and evil into a present, practical question: **"What fruit do you keep eating?"** Whatever you keep consuming — what you watch, hear, rehearse, and feed on — becomes the tree you are eating from, and it produces a harvest in you. A tree is known by its fruit; so is a life, and so is a thought-diet. The discipline is to *notice the fruit*, trace it back to its tree, and ask Yahweh to search and show what is actually growing.

**A tree is known by its fruit:**

**ESV — Matthew 7:16–20:** *"You will recognize them by their fruits. Are grapes gathered from thornbushes, or figs from thistles? So, every healthy tree bears good fruit, but the diseased tree bears bad fruit. A healthy tree cannot bear bad fruit, nor can a diseased tree bear good fruit. Every tree that does not bear good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire. Thus you will recognize them by their fruits."*

The test is not the leaves or the look of the tree — it is the *fruit*. What is your life actually producing? That tells you what you have been eating.

**Two harvests — the works of the flesh vs. the fruit of the Spirit:**

**Supporting reference — Galatians 5:19–23 (ESV).** The flesh produces one harvest (its works are named in vv. 19–21); the Spirit produces another — *"love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control"* (vv. 22–23). The fruit you keep eating determines which harvest grows. *(To be quoted verbatim in-app where the full list deepens the entry.)*

**Invite the search — let Yahweh show you the tree:**

**Supporting reference — Psalm 139:23–24 (ESV):** *"Search me, O God, and know my heart..."* — the prayer that asks Yahweh to surface what is actually growing, rather than self-assessing blind. *(Anchor verse to be quoted verbatim in-app.)*

This entry pairs with the `MIND-OF-CHRIST.md` discipline: noticing the fruit is the NOTICE step; tracing it to the tree and asking for the search is how the REDIRECT begins.

### Entry: "Yahweh Willing I'll Build a Great System to Support Our Needs ASAP"

In the middle of a hard session — after surfacing real, frustrating problems in the work (stale financial data that should not have persisted, the Gmail import down since 2026-05-15, the orchestrator that was designed but never deployed) — and after setting the bar plainly for what the system must be (**verifiable, dynamic, open-source done in better ways, with every piece of data having a path and a purpose**), Darrell said:

> **Darrell:** *"Yahweh willing I'll build a great system to support our needs ASAP."*

The reflection frames the building itself as **service under Yahweh's will.** The phrase *"Yahweh willing"* is not a figure of speech here — it places the work *and its timing* in His hands. It is the posture James names directly: not "I will do this," but "if the Lord wills, we will live and do this." The **great system** is not the goal for its own sake; it is **to support our needs** — the family's, the church's, the community's. That is the Father's Business: the work is *for* the people Yahweh has given to steward, done under His will, on His timing.

**"Yahweh willing" — the timing and the doing both belong to Him:**

**ESV — James 4:13–15:** *"Come now, you who say, 'Today or tomorrow we will go into such and such a town and spend a year there and trade and make a profit'—yet you do not know what tomorrow will bring. What is your life? For you are a mist that appears for a little time and then vanishes. Instead you ought to say, 'If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that.'"*

The "ASAP" and the "Yahweh willing" are held together honestly: the urgency is real (the needs are real, the broken pieces are real), and the surrender is also real — the outcome and the timing are submitted to Yahweh, not seized.

**The builder does not build alone — without Him the labor is in vain:**

**ESV — Psalm 127:1:** *"Unless the Lord builds the house, those who build it labor in vain. Unless the Lord watches over the city, the watchman stays awake in vain."*

This is the verse beneath "Yahweh willing." A *great* system built without Him is vain labor; the same system built under His will is the house He builds through faithful hands. The frustration of the session — the stale data, the dead import, the undeployed orchestrator — is the labor; "Yahweh willing" is the confession that the labor is not vain only because He is the one building.

**Commit the work, and let Him establish it:**

**ESV — Proverbs 16:3:** *"Commit your work to the Lord, and your plans will be established."*

The data must each have a path and a purpose; the plans must be committed to Him to be established. Verification and surrender are not opposites here — the system is to be *verifiably* right (DR-0076), and that verified work is *committed to Yahweh* to be made to stand.

**The work is for the needs of the household — the Father's Business:**

**ESV — Luke 2:49:** *"And he said to them, 'Why were you looking for me? Did you not know that I must be in my Father's house?'"*

> KJV — Luke 2:49: *"...wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?"* — the "Father's Business" wording Darrell uses. The great system is built to support our needs *because* the needs are the Father's business: the family, the church (the Body), the community PoeTech serves first.

**The building itself is rest and peace for Darrell — the anchor that His opinion is the true wealth:**

**ESV — Matthew 11:28–29:** *"Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest for your souls."*

This ties back to the binding line of this whole Study — **Yahweh's opinion IS wealth, money or not** (see the Parable of the Talents entry, 2026-06-16). For Darrell, the building is not only labor; done under Yahweh's will and for His people's needs, the work is itself a form of *rest and peace* — the yoke that is easy because it is shared with Him, not carried alone. The hard session is heavy laden; "Yahweh willing" is where the burden is laid down even while the hands keep working.
