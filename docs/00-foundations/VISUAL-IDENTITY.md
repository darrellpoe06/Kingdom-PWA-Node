# Visual Identity
> *"On his head are many diadems... From his mouth comes a sharp sword with which to strike down the nations, and he will rule them with a rod of iron... On his robe and on his thigh he has a name written, King of kings and Lord of lords."* — Revelation 19:12, 15-16 (ESV)
## What This Document Is
This document establishes the visual identity of the SKOS PWA — the design DNA, iconography, color systems, theme modes, and reaction/expression tools. It is the visual expression of `EXCELLENCE-STANDARD.md`: representatives of the King, in pixel form.
This is not branding for marketing. This is *visual liturgy* — what the user sees, repeatedly, day after day, becomes part of their formation. Excellence here is worship.
## Design DNA: Apple Meets Galaxy Note
SKOS draws from two flagship phone design traditions and fuses them:
**Apple iPhone / iOS contributes:**
- Restrained polish. White space as a feature, not absence.
- Considered motion. Animations have meaning; nothing moves arbitrarily.
- Premium tactility. Every tap, swipe, and transition feels intentional.
- Typography respect. SF Pro-style precision. Letterforms breathe.
- Default clean. Out of the box, almost nothing competes for attention.
**Samsung Galaxy Note contributes:**
- Depth and density when summoned. Power-user features available, not hidden behind subscription tiers.
- Multitasking respect. The user can hold multiple contexts.
- Customization. The user's environment is theirs to shape.
- Stylus-quality precision (when input requires it).
- Information richness as a virtue when warranted.
**The merge: clean default for everyone, deep capability for those who want it.** This IS progressive disclosure (from `UX-PATTERNS.md`) stated as brand language. The first-time user is not overwhelmed. The advanced user is not patronized. Both are honored.
## The Four Theme Modes
SKOS ships with four polished theme modes. The user selects one as their default. All four meet the same quality bar — none is "the lite version" of another.
### 1. Sovereign Black (Default)
**Base:** True black backgrounds (#000000 on OLED for power efficiency, near-black #0A0A0B otherwise).
**Text:** High-contrast off-white (#F5F5F7).
**Accents:** Royal purple, gold leaf, deep crimson.
**Mood:** Throne room. The user is in the presence of the King.
This is the default for three reasons:
1. **Reverence.** Black is the color of awe, depth, and weight. Walking into the throne room is not a casual experience.
2. **Power efficiency.** On OLED screens (most modern phones), pure black pixels are off — extending battery life.
3. **Eye comfort.** Most SKOS use happens in early morning devotion or late evening reflection. Dark mode reduces strain in those hours.
### 2. Apple Silver
**Base:** Apple-white (#FFFFFF, #FAFAFA).
**Text:** Near-black (#1C1C1E).
**Accents:** Soft silver, muted gold, navy.
**Mood:** Daylight study. Clean and bright. Library-like.
For users who prefer the iPhone-native aesthetic, or who study scripture in bright environments.
### 3. Galaxy Charcoal
**Base:** Deep charcoal grey (#202124).
**Text:** Warm off-white (#E8E8EA).
**Accents:** Cool blue, copper, soft violet.
**Mood:** Galaxy Note premium dark. Industrial elegance. Tool-like.
For users who prefer the Samsung-native aesthetic, or who want a less stark dark mode than Sovereign Black.
### 4. Cathedral Grey
**Base:** Mid-grey (#3A3A3C).
**Text:** Off-white (#F0F0F2).
**Accents:** Stained-glass jewel tones used sparingly.
**Mood:** Quiet sanctuary. Neither bright nor dark. Contemplative.
For users who find pure dark too intense and pure light too clinical. A third path. Reading-room feel.
### Theme Mode Quality Requirements
All four modes must:
- Meet WCAG AA contrast minimums (AAA where feasible)
- Render the Scripture component identically in structure (only colors change)
- Handle the crown and lion iconography appropriately for the mode
- Display reactions (hearts, shields, etc.) with mode-appropriate styling
- Auto-switch based on system dark mode preference if user chooses "system" override
## Iconography: Crown and Black Lion
### The Crown
**Biblical anchor:**
**ESV — Revelation 19:12:** *"His eyes are like a flame of fire, and on his head are many diadems, and he has a name written that no one knows but himself."*
**ESV — Revelation 4:10:** *"the twenty-four elders fall down before him who is seated on the throne and worship him who lives forever and ever. They cast their crowns before the throne, saying..."*
**ESV — 2 Timothy 4:8:** *"Henceforth there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will award to me on that day, and not only to me but also to all who have loved his appearing."*
**KJV — Revelation 19:12:** *"His eyes were as a flame of fire, and on his head were many crowns; and he had a name written, that no man knew, but he himself."*
The crown signifies:
- **Yahshua's rule.** He is King of kings.
- **The believer's destiny.** Crowns received, then cast at His feet in worship.
- **Authority delegated.** Walking The Way is walking with kingly identity — *"a royal priesthood"* (1 Peter 2:9).
**Visual treatment:** Multiple diadems stacked (per Revelation 19:12, "many crowns") rather than a single circle. Suggests fullness of authority. Rendered in gold leaf with deep shadow on dark modes; in soft gold with refined linework on light modes.
### The Black Lion of Judah
**Biblical anchor:**
**ESV — Revelation 5:5:** *"And one of the elders said to me, 'Weep no more; behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has conquered, so that he can open the scroll and its seven seals.'"*
**ESV — Genesis 49:9-10:** *"Judah is a lion's cub; from the prey, my son, you have gone up. He stooped down; he crouched as a lion and as a lioness; who dares rouse him? The scepter shall not depart from Judah, nor the ruler's staff from between his feet, until tribute comes to him; and to him shall be the obedience of the peoples."*
**KJV — Revelation 5:5:** *"And one of the elders saith unto me, Weep not: behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath prevailed to open the book, and to loose the seven seals thereof."*
The lion signifies:
- **Yahshua's conquest.** He has already conquered.
- **Tribal identity.** Judah is the line through which the Messiah came.
- **Power held under self-control.** A lion is supremely powerful AND supremely poised. Both at once.
**Why black:**
The lion is rendered black in SKOS for two converging reasons:
1. **Cultural identity recovery.** Judah was Middle Eastern and African. The Ethiopian Orthodox Church has held the Lion of Judah as central iconography for 1700 years. For Black families especially, recovering the Black Lion of Judah is reclaiming what was always true about biblical geography and lineage. This is not reframing — this is recovery.
2. **Visual elegance.** A black lion against rich color (gold crown, deep purple backgrounds in Sovereign Black mode) is visually arresting in a way a generic golden lion is not. It commands attention. It says "this is not a generic Christian app."
**Visual treatment:** A lion in profile, mane in motion, head crowned. Black silhouette with subtle highlighting (mane texture visible). Sometimes rendered with the crown integrated; sometimes the lion alone; sometimes the crown alone. Three modes of use depending on context.
### The Yahshua Standard
The character: **humble, all-powerful, self-controlled, full of Love.**
This is the visual standard for every depiction of Yahshua-as-King across SKOS:
- **Humble.** Not a vain or arrogant rendering. The King who washed feet.
- **All-powerful.** Not a weak or sentimental rendering. The Lion has conquered.
- **Self-controlled.** Power held in restraint. The strength is fully present, fully contained.
- **Full of Love.** Warmth visible. Not a distant or judgmental rendering.
**The literal Super Hero.** Every fictional superhero story is a faint echo of the true story. Christian, Christyn, and Christiana growing up inside SKOS see — repeatedly, visually — that Yahshua has more power than Superman, more cunning than Batman, more sacrifice than all of them combined, AND He's real. The fictional heroes are echoes; He is the original.
C.S. Lewis called it the True Myth. Tolkien said the gospel is the story all stories are reaching for. SKOS visualizes that claim.
## Reactions and Expression Tools
Users need to express response to content — agreement, encouragement, prayer, conviction. SKOS uses a reaction set grounded in the Armor of God (Ephesians 6:10-18) plus standard supplements.
### The Armor of God Reaction Set
**ESV — Ephesians 6:11, 13-17:** *"Put on the whole armor of God... the belt of truth, and having put on the breastplate of righteousness, and, as shoes for your feet, having put on the readiness given by the gospel of peace. In all circumstances take up the shield of faith, with which you can extinguish all the flaming darts of the evil one; and take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God."*
Each reaction maps to a piece of armor:
| Icon | Name | Meaning When Tapped |
|------|------|---------------------|
| 🛡️ **Shield of Faith** | "I receive this in faith" | Affirms a truth claim or scripture |
| ⚔️ **Sword of the Spirit** | "This cuts through" | Marks content that brought conviction |
| 🪖 **Helmet of Salvation** | "This protects my mind" | Saves content for re-reading in temptation |
| 👞 **Shoes of Peace** | "I'm walking this out" | Commits to action on the content |
| 🦺 **Breastplate of Righteousness** | "Yes, this is right" | Affirms moral/ethical alignment |
| 🪢 **Belt of Truth** | "This is true" | Marks something as foundationally true |
These are SKOS-native. They are deeper than a Facebook "Like." A user reading a friend's testimony might tap Shield of Faith — meaning *"I receive your testimony, I stand with you in faith on this."* A user reading a scripture exposition might tap Sword of the Spirit — meaning *"this convicted me."*
### Standard Reactions Also Available
For ease and familiarity, SKOS also provides:
- ❤️ **Heart** — "I love this"
- 🙏 **Prayer hands** — "I'm praying for/with this"
- 🔥 **Fire** — "This is on fire / Holy Spirit move"
- 💎 **Gem** — "This is precious / save this"
And custom expressions:
- ✝️ **Cross** — "I bring this to the cross"
- 👑 **Crown** — "All glory to the King"
- 🦁 **Lion** — "The Lion has conquered this"
### Implementation Notes
Reactions are:
- One-tap to apply, one-tap to remove
- Show aggregate counts on social/community content (when applicable)
- Always show your own reactions distinctly
- Available on any "reactable" content: posts, testimonies, scripture entries, journal entries (private), teaching content, etc.
- Free to use — no paywall, no premium tier required
## Typography
**Primary typeface:** A clean, readable sans-serif (Inter, SF Pro, or Roboto — whichever the platform provides natively for best performance). Used for UI, body copy, and most reading.
**Scripture typeface:** A slightly distinguished serif (Source Serif Pro or similar) for scripture text. Subtle visual cue that scripture is *set apart* from surrounding prose.
**Headings:** Same as primary, weighted heavier. Optional display variant for hero moments.
**Sizing:** Respects user's system text size preference. Minimum body 16pt; minimum touch target 44×44pt.

These colors appear in some form across all four theme modes:

- **Gold Leaf** (#C9A227) — crowns, glory
- **Crimson** (#8B1A1A) — covenant blood

Each theme mode emphasizes different members of this palette while keeping the set consistent.
## Motion and Animation
**Principles:**
1. **Purposeful.** Every animation communicates state change or hierarchy. Nothing animates for decoration alone.
2. **Quick.** Default duration 200-300ms. Slower only when the moment warrants (a verse fade-in for meditation, e.g., 500-800ms).
3. **Eased.** Spring or ease-out curves. Never linear.
4. **Reduced-motion respect.** Honor system "reduce motion" preferences. Replace with crossfades.
5. **Reverent for scripture.** When scripture appears, the motion is gentler than UI motion. The Word does not pop in.
## Imagery and Iconography Standards
**Use crowns and Black Lions as branded marks.** They appear in:
- App icon (composite of crown + lion silhouette)
- Splash screen
- Empty-state illustrations
- Achievement/milestone celebrations
- Section dividers in foundation docs and teaching content
**Do NOT use:**
- Generic stock photography of "Christian" imagery (sunset crosses, etc.)
- Saccharine or sentimental illustrations
- Overly-tribal aesthetics that exclude rather than include
- Imagery that could be mistaken for a prosperity-gospel product
**Do use:**
- Original SKOS-commissioned or SKOS-generated illustrations following the Yahshua Standard
- Hebraic and African artistic traditions where appropriate (the historical roots of the faith)
- Modern editorial illustration style — clean lines, considered composition
## Religion AND Relationship in This Identity
**Religion-side:** Consistent symbol use. Disciplined color application. Typography that respects the gravity of the content. Symbols anchored in scripture, not in market trends.
**Relationship-side:** Four theme modes meeting four reader preferences. Reactions that let users express heart with one tap. Warmth in the type, the motion, the empty states. The product *welcomes*.
Both.
